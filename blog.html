Hospitality · Downtown Austin

Crews break ground on dual-branded Embassy Suites and Tempo hotel downtown

Austin Business Journal · Sep 15, 2026 · also DLR Group

I’m still watching this as the clearest hospitality signal in the core. Austin Business Journal reports a dual-branded Hilton project at Fifth and Lavaca — part Embassy Suites, part Tempo — as one of the first new construction starts downtown in some time, with about 480 rooms expected online in the coming years. Coverage around the Sept. 14 groundbreaking describes a 30-story tower co-developed by Peachtree Group and Merritt Development Group, with architecture and interiors by DLR Group, plus a rooftop bar and lounge, podium restaurant, ballroom/conference space, and an early-2029 opening target. For me, this is capital committing vertical inventory in the CBD even while office absorption stays selective.

Office · Near downtown

Endeavor breaks ground on 198,000 SF 5th & Walsh office-retail project

REBusinessOnline · Sep 14, 2026

Selective office delivery is still happening — when the lease story is already half written. REBusinessOnline reports Endeavor Real Estate Group has broken ground on 5th & Walsh, a 198,000-square-foot office and retail project near downtown Austin designed by Studio8 Architects and Lake Flato Architects. The building will include four floors of office (Endeavor plans to take 25,000 square feet for its new headquarters) plus 21,000 square feet of ground-floor retail and restaurant space, with shell construction targeted for late 2028. Other undisclosed office tenants have already leased another 85,000 square feet, bringing the office component to about 55% preleased. I’m reading this as the walkable, amenitized product thesis in action: new office still pencils when preleasing and owner-user commitment de-risk the start.

Mixed-use · Northeast Austin

Airbnb commits $6.4M to restart St. John redevelopment with retail and park

CBS Austin · Sep 14, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Airbnb newsroom

Capital just unlocked a long-stalled North Austin site with a clear CRE overlay. CBS Austin and Airbnb’s own announcement say the company is investing $6.4 million — the first project under its new Housing Accelerator — to fund 201 affordable apartments at 800 E. St. John Avenue. Under the city’s agreement, that fully affordable piece unlocks a companion 325-unit mixed-income project; together the redevelopment is expected to deliver more than 500 homes plus retail, an expanded St. John Park, and public art, with delivery anticipated in 2028. City-owned since 2013, the vacant site is now being cleared with partners including Greystar and the Housing Authority of the City of Austin. Austin Business Journal separately frames it as Airbnb investing in a big Austin project as part of a national push. For me, this is a mixed-use land story: last-dollar capital moving dirt on a corridor where retail and public realm sit next to housing delivery.

Land · Georgetown

Georgetown’s Yearwood MUD heads toward a Sept. 22 final vote — with a commercial corner on Reagan

Community Impact · Sep 14, 2026

The northern MSA land pipeline is still in motion this week. Community Impact reports Georgetown City Council gave initial approval Sept. 8 to establish an in-city municipal utility district for the Yearwood property — roughly 728 acres north of Sun City, west of Ronald Reagan Boulevard and Rattlesnake Road, long operated as a stone quarry. The Yearwood family, Freehold Capital Management, and the city have been negotiating since February. The plan is multiuse: majority residential (duplexes, townhomes, and 45-to-60-foot single-family lots, house count reduced to 1,728) with commercial land planned at the Reagan–Rattlesnake corner. In exchange for MUD consent, dedications include a 140-acre Berry Creek Park, trails, amenity centers, a 7-acre public-safety site, and a 110-foot-wide minor arterial linking Reagan northward. Final approval is slated for the Sept. 22 meeting — so I’m watching that commercial pad as the CRE piece of a much larger Georgetown entitlement story.

Data centers · Taylor

Project Mustang withdraws Taylor annexation bid but keeps pushing a 665-acre data center

Taylor Press · Sep 9, 2026 · also City of Taylor Sep 3

The eastern MSA digital-infrastructure fight just shifted jurisdictionally. Taylor Press reports that Big Watt Digital and PH Digital Data Center Development — the sponsors of Project Mustang — withdrew their annexation and zoning application with the City of Taylor, while saying the project remains committed to advancing on about 665 acres of former farmland between U.S. 79 and FM 112 in the city’s extraterritorial jurisdiction. The city’s Sept. 3 notice is blunt: because the site sits outside city limits, Taylor currently has no authority to stop or regulate the development, even as talks on annexation continue over noise, water use, setbacks, and community benefits. A Sept. 10 council public hearing was canceled; developers have previously said they intend to pursue the project with or without city collaboration, with operations targeted for 2028. For me, this is the ETJ version of the data-center story: power and land demand pushing past municipal process when annexation stalls.

Economic development · Bastrop County

Bastrop County’s backwoods boom is being fueled largely by Elon Musk

Austin Business Journal · Sep 13, 2026 · companion Sep 12

The CRE story east of Austin keeps widening beyond warehouse parks. Austin Business Journal’s Colin Pope reports that Bastrop County is growing notably — largely due to Elon Musk — with wartime startup Bravo Ordnance recently opening a major headquarters facility in the Cedar Creek area, where it 3D-prints warheads. A companion Justin Sayers piece the day before captures the bittersweet neighbor view: high-paying jobs and ancillary development on one side, a changing rural landscape and sellers who waited out the first wave on the other. Paired with the data-center acreage nearby, I’m watching Bastrop as a mixed industrial and digital-infrastructure corridor where land, power, and manufacturing demand are stacking on the same map.

Data centers · Bastrop County

Data centers swarm Cedar Creek as neighbors push back

Austin Business Journal · Sep 12, 2026

The digital-infrastructure map keeps stretching southeast of Austin — and it pairs with what we’re seeing in Taylor’s ETJ. Austin Business Journal reports that rural Cedar Creek in Bastrop County is among the hardest-hit pockets of Texas’ data-center boom, with the paper tabulating more than 5,500 acres of projects in the area — some nearing completion, others just starting, and more still in planning — while neighbors raise concerns. EdgeConneX already has a facility there and plans for additional capacity nearby. For me, the throughline is clear: AI load is chasing power and land in the ETJ and rural counties even as Austin debates large-load rules inside city limits.

Capital / policy · Texas & Austin MSA

Texas nominates 605 Opportunity Zone 2.0 tracts — including Austin’s Dog’s Head area

Texas Today · Sep 11, 2026 · also Office of the Governor

Capital-formation policy is back on my CRE watchlist. Texas Today reports the Texas Economic Development & Tourism Office submitted 605 census tracts across 105 counties to the U.S. Treasury on Sept. 4 under Opportunity Zone 2.0 — the renewed federal program set to run Jan. 1, 2027, through Dec. 31, 2036. The Office of the Governor confirms the same nomination count and process; designations are not final until Treasury certification, expected by Nov. 28, 2026. Austin-area nominations include the Dog’s Head area between downtown and Austin-Bergstrom International Airport — a large land assemblage shaped by the Colorado River that already sits in the city’s longer-range development conversation. For me, this is a patient-capital map: if certified, OZ incentives could steer equity toward eligible MSA tracts just as industrial, multifamily, and mixed-use pipelines keep stretching north and east.
