Retail · Central Austin

Ivy and 35 South Capital buy 2825 Hancock for Highland Market

CultureMap Austin · Sep 10, 2026

Neighborhood retail is getting a fresh Central Austin bet. Ivy Companies and 35 South Capital purchased 2825 Hancock Drive — between Rosedale and Allandale, across from Austin Memorial Park and accessible from MoPac — and plan to renovate it into Highland Market, about 18,900 square feet for roughly 12 tenants spanning retail, local restaurants, and neighborhood services. Local firm Firmah is on design; Ivy already owns Casis Village in Tarrytown. For me, this is the kind of small-box, walkable repositioning I’m watching as capital looks past trophy corridors for everyday retail demand.

Industrial · Manufacturing

Allen Control Systems leases 192,000 SF for North Austin manufacturing

Commercial Real Estate Direct · Sep 9, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal

Defense tech is absorbing industrial space north of the core. Allen Control Systems signed a lease for 192,000 square feet at 15700 Long Vista Drive in North Austin, with plans to rapidly scale production of its Bullfrog autonomous weapon station. Per Austin Business Journal’s coverage, the company also holds an option to expand by another 150,000 square feet, and the owner had repositioned a long-vacant building for advanced manufacturing. I’m reading this as another user-driven industrial lease — the kind that keeps north-corridor product relevant even when speculative absorption is uneven.

Industrial · Taylor

Partners Real Estate breaks ground on 573,000 SF speculative building in Taylor

Austin Business Journal · Sep 9, 2026

The eastern industrial pipeline keeps extending. Partners Real Estate broke ground on a 573,000-square-foot speculative, rail-served industrial building in Taylor — next to the firm’s already-leased Compal USA Technology building — with a cross-dock configuration, office pods, truck courts, ample parking, and heavy power. For me, this is the Samsung-adjacent corridor thesis in motion: large-bay, power-ready product still pencils when rail and manufacturing demand sit nearby.

Industrial · Cedar Park

Aeon Industrial nearly triples Cedar Park footprint to 138,000 SF

Austin Business Journal · Sep 8, 2026

Defense manufacturing demand is showing up in the northwest suburbs too. Austin Business Journal reports Aeon Industrial is expanding its Cedar Park headquarters and production footprint to about 138,000 square feet — nearly tripling prior space — as it shifts HQ operations from Austin and cites customer demand. I’m pairing this with the Allen Control Systems lease as a reminder that user-driven, defense-adjacent industrial still competes for product outside the core.

Industrial · Georgetown

Aalo Atomics files $25M Georgetown production facility permit

Williamson County Sun · Sep 8, 2026 · also Austin American-Statesman

Advanced manufacturing tied to power and data centers is landing north of Austin. Aalo Atomics — an Austin-based nuclear research and manufacturing company — submitted an Aug. 31 Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation filing for a roughly $25 million production facility in Georgetown, renovating an existing warehouse of about 204,350 square feet. Statesman coverage of the same filing points to a lab, machine shop, and production floor, with work expected to start mid-November and finish by early 2028. For northern MSA industrial clients, this is another demand signal for large existing shells that can be build-to-suit for specialized manufacturing.

Multifamily · Affordable

NRP breaks ground on Catalina — 336 affordable units in southeast Austin

REBusinessOnline · Sep 8, 2026

Affordable multifamily is still getting built even as market-rate permits have thinned. Cleveland-based The NRP Group broke ground on Catalina, a 336-unit community in southeast Austin developed with the Housing Authority of Travis County. Twelve three-story buildings will offer one- to four-bedroom homes for households at 30–70% of AMI, with resident services (afterschool care, financial literacy, ESL, first-time homebuyer programs). Tax-credit equity comes from Huntington Community Development Corp.; PIMCO provided construction and permanent financing arranged by Berkadia. First units are expected next fall, with full completion targeted for spring 2028. I’m watching this as a live counterweight to the thinner conventional pipeline.

Multifamily · Investment

Knightvest buys 1,027-unit North Austin multifamily portfolio

REJournals · Sep 9, 2026 · also REBusinessOnline Sep 3 / Multifamily Business Sep 8

Capital is still rotating into Austin apartments. Newmark arranged the sale of three Northwest Austin / Round Rock communities totaling 1,027 units — The Enclave at La Frontera (411), Lakeside at La Frontera (366), and Legends Lake Creek (250), known as The Silicon Hills Trio — from San Antonio–based LYND to Dallas-based Knightvest Capital. Built 2001–2004, the assets average about 936 SF per unit. Patton Jones and Andrew Dickson represented the seller; Tip Strickland and David Schwarz arranged buyer financing. This is the kind of mid-vintage corridor trade I’m watching as investors re-enter after the supply overhang.

Data centers · Hutto

Colovore plans December start on $280M, 192,000 SF Hutto data center

CRE Market Beat · Sep 4, 2026

Digital infrastructure keeps anchoring northern industrial parks. Colovore is advancing a 192,000-square-foot data center at 2401 Innovation Blvd. in Hutto, with construction slated to begin in December, a roughly $280 million investment, and completion targeted for December 2028. The project sits on a 30-acre tract Colovore acquired in July 2025 inside Hutto Crossing — a 118-acre park planned for more than 1 million square feet, developed with Velocis, Ironwood Realty Partners, and MBK Industrial Properties. Earlier concepts described a larger program; the latest reporting frames a single 192,000-SF facility. For MSA land and power-constrained sites, I’m watching how Hutto and other ETJ corridors absorb AI-driven demand while Austin’s own data-center code rewrite continues.
