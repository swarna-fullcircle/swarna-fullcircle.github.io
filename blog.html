What I’m watching in CRE

Hospitality · Downtown Austin

Crews break ground on dual-branded Embassy Suites and Tempo hotel downtown

Austin Business Journal · Sep 15, 2026 · also DLR Group

Downtown finally has a major new hotel pouring dirt again. Austin Business Journal reports a dual-branded Hilton project at Fifth and Lavaca — part Embassy Suites, part Tempo — as one of the first new construction starts in the core in some time, with about 480 rooms expected online in the coming years. DLR Group’s Sept. 15 release fills in the program: a 30-story tower that broke ground Sept. 14, designed for architecture and interiors by DLR Group, co-developed by Peachtree Group and Merritt Development Group, with a rooftop bar and lounge, podium restaurant, ballroom/conference space, and an early-2029 opening target. For me, this is a hospitality land-use signal: capital is willing to commit vertical inventory in the CBD even while office absorption stays selective.

Office · Near downtown

Endeavor breaks ground on 198,000 SF 5th & Walsh office-retail project

REBusinessOnline · Sep 14, 2026

Selective office delivery is still happening — when the lease story is already half written. REBusinessOnline reports Endeavor Real Estate Group has broken ground on 5th & Walsh, a 198,000-square-foot office and retail project near downtown Austin designed by Studio8 Architects and Lake Flato Architects. The building will include four floors of office (Endeavor plans to take 25,000 square feet for its new headquarters) plus 21,000 square feet of ground-floor retail and restaurant space, with shell construction targeted for late 2028. Other undisclosed office tenants have already leased another 85,000 square feet, bringing the office component to about 55% preleased. I’m reading this as the walkable, amenitized product thesis in action: new office still pencils when preleasing and owner-user commitment de-risk the start.

Mixed-use · Northeast Austin

Airbnb commits $6.4M to restart St. John redevelopment with retail and park

CBS Austin · Sep 14, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Airbnb newsroom

Capital just unlocked a long-stalled North Austin site with a clear CRE overlay. CBS Austin and Airbnb’s own announcement say the company is investing $6.4 million — the first project under its new Housing Accelerator — to fund 201 affordable apartments at 800 E. St. John Avenue. Under the city’s agreement, that fully affordable piece unlocks a companion 325-unit mixed-income project; together the redevelopment is expected to deliver more than 500 homes plus retail, an expanded St. John Park, and public art, with delivery anticipated in 2028. City-owned since 2013, the vacant site is now being cleared with partners including Greystar and the Housing Authority of the City of Austin. Austin Business Journal separately frames it as Airbnb investing in a big Austin project as part of a national push. For me, this is a mixed-use land story: last-dollar capital moving dirt on a corridor where retail and public realm sit next to housing delivery.

Land · Georgetown

Georgetown gives initial approval to 728-acre Yearwood MUD with commercial corner

Community Impact · Sep 14, 2026

The northern MSA land pipeline just got another large entitlement step. Community Impact reports Georgetown City Council gave initial approval Sept. 8 to establish an in-city municipal utility district for the Yearwood property — roughly 728 acres north of Sun City, west of Ronald Reagan Boulevard and Rattlesnake Road, long operated as a stone quarry. The Yearwood family, Freehold Capital Management, and the city have been negotiating since February. The plan is multiuse: majority residential (duplexes, townhomes, and 45-to-60-foot single-family lots, house count reduced to 1,728) with commercial land planned at the Reagan–Rattlesnake corner. In exchange for MUD consent, dedications include a 140-acre Berry Creek Park, trails, amenity centers, a 7-acre public-safety site, and a 110-foot-wide minor arterial linking Reagan northward. Final approval is slated for the Sept. 22 meeting. I’m watching the commercial pad at Reagan and Rattlesnake as the CRE piece of a much larger Georgetown annexation story.

Economic development · Bastrop County

Bastrop County’s backwoods boom is being fueled largely by Elon Musk

Austin Business Journal · Sep 13, 2026 · companion Sep 12

The CRE story east of Austin keeps widening beyond warehouse parks. Austin Business Journal’s Colin Pope reports that Bastrop County is growing notably — largely due to Elon Musk — with wartime startup Bravo Ordnance recently opening a major headquarters facility in the Cedar Creek area, where it 3D-prints warheads. A companion Justin Sayers piece the day before captures the bittersweet neighbor view: high-paying jobs and ancillary development on one side, a changing rural landscape and sellers who waited out the first wave on the other. Paired with the data-center acreage nearby, I’m watching Bastrop as a mixed industrial and digital-infrastructure corridor where land, power, and manufacturing demand are stacking on the same map.

Data centers · Bastrop County

Data centers swarm Cedar Creek as neighbors push back

Austin Business Journal · Sep 12, 2026

The digital-infrastructure map keeps stretching southeast of Austin. Austin Business Journal reports that rural Cedar Creek in Bastrop County is among the hardest-hit pockets of Texas’ data-center boom, with the paper tabulating more than 5,500 acres of projects in the area — some nearing completion, others just starting, and more still in planning — while neighbors raise concerns. EdgeConneX already has a facility there and plans for additional capacity nearby. For me, this sits next to the Hutto pipeline: AI load is chasing power and land in the ETJ and rural counties even as Austin debates large-load rules inside city limits.

Data centers · Hutto

Colovore files for $280M, ~193K SF AI data center in Hutto

Community Impact · Sep 11, 2026

Digital infrastructure is still choosing the northern industrial parks. Community Impact reports Colovore — a California-based AI data center developer — plans a one-story, 192,694-square-foot facility at 2401 Innovation Boulevard in Hutto, with about $280 million in investment, 40 megawatts of capacity, and liquid cooling. Construction is slated to start Dec. 1, 2026, and finish Dec. 1, 2028, per a Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation filing. The site sits next to land that could host Blue Origin. Paired with Cedar Creek, I’m reading a clear pattern: AI colocation keeps landing outside Austin’s city limits while the core debates large-load rules.

Retail · Central Austin

Ivy and 35 South Capital buy 2825 Hancock for Highland Market

CultureMap Austin · Sep 10, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal Sep 9

Neighborhood retail is getting a fresh Central Austin bet. Ivy Companies and 35 South Capital purchased 2825 Hancock Drive — between Rosedale and Allandale, across from Austin Memorial Park and accessible from MoPac — and plan to renovate it into Highland Market, about 18,900 square feet for roughly 12 tenants spanning retail, local restaurants, and neighborhood services. Local firm Firmah is on design; Ivy already owns Casis Village in Tarrytown. Austin Business Journal separately notes leasing is underway for retail, restaurant, and service-oriented businesses. For me, this is the kind of small-box, walkable repositioning I’m watching as capital looks past trophy corridors for everyday retail demand.
