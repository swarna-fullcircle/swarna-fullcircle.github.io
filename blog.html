Industrial & advanced manufacturing · Bastrop County

Musk’s companies start work on more factories in Bastrop, including one building planned with a 2.14 million-square-foot footprint

Austin Business Journal · Oct 30, 2026 · Bloomberg · May 21, 2026 · TCEQ air permit record

The Austin Business Journal reports that work has started on additional factory buildings at Elon Musk’s growing Bastrop campus, which already includes X, SpaceX, The Boring Company and the Ad Astra School, and that one of the new buildings is planned with a 2.14 million-square-foot footprint. ABJ frames it as part of the plan to launch data centers into space. For context, Bloomberg reported in May that permit filings with Bastrop County describe a SpaceX solar-cell facility with two floors that would each make five gigawatts of solar cells, ten gigawatts in all, to power Starlink and orbital AI data centers. Separately, TCEQ’s air permit database shows a pending SpaceX application for aluminum furnaces at the “Starlink Factory Bastrop” site at 858 FM 1209; it held a public meeting Oct. 15 and its comment period closed Oct. 23. I haven’t seen a published investment or job figure for the new buildings, so I’m not putting a number on it. My read: Bastrop keeps pulling large-format manufacturing east along SH 71 and FM 1209, and that is where I’d expect demand for supplier space, workforce housing and service retail to keep building.

Office & mixed-use sales · Central Austin, Northwest Austin & downtown

Gabriel’s Court trades for the first time in 40 years, and new detail on Lakeside at Quarry Oaks shows what a lease-up can do

Austin Business Journal · Oct 30, 2026 · Commercial Property Executive · Oct 30, 2026 · CRE Daily · Oct 30, 2026

ABJ reports a local firm bought Gabriel’s Court, the roughly 45,000-square-foot office and retail property at 2905 San Gabriel St., which sold for the first time in about 40 years. It’s about 8% vacant and sits between the medical complexes and UT Austin. Price and seller weren’t disclosed. Commercial Property Executive added useful detail on last week’s DML Capital purchase of Lakeside at Quarry Oaks: Brick Row Holdings lifted occupancy from 36% to 98% after buying the 1998-built, 123,272-square-foot building in June 2025, and tenants include HeartFlow, York Space Systems and UBEO. On the sublease side, CRE Daily’s Oct. 30 brief recaps Indeed’s full 308,000-square-foot listing at Indeed Tower and Meta’s remaining block of a little over 456,000 square feet after InKind’s roughly 32,000-square-foot deal. It also notes that CBRE reported occupancy gains this month even as vacancy and availability kept climbing. My read: well-located, smaller buildings and value-add plays with a clear leasing story are what’s trading, while the big downtown blocks still give tenants leverage.

ABJ: Gabriel’s Court · CPE: Lakeside at Quarry Oaks · CRE Daily: downtown subleases

Healthcare & institutional · North Austin

UT launches UT Dell Medicine and lays out what its North Austin hospital, opening in 2030, will focus on

UT News · Oct 27, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 30, 2026 · JLL · Oct 28, 2026

UT Austin introduced UT Dell Medicine, its academic health system anchored by Dell Medical School. The former UT Health Austin clinical practice and ambulatory surgery center take the new name. UT says the future UT Dell Medical Center, opening in 2030, will focus on cardiovascular, cancer, neurological and transplant care plus advanced intensive care, with UT MD Anderson physicians embedded in cancer care, and that once fully operational the system plans to double its clinical capability and more than triple its patient volumes. Community Impact ranked it the top trending Austin story of the week. JLL’s release on Lakeside at Quarry Oaks put that building right next to the planned 300-acre campus. For medical office and nearby retail, the hospital is the anchor I’m watching most in North Austin over the next few years.

Data centers / permitting · Statewide & Hays County

Inside Climate News says new Texas data-center development is essentially paused until after the election, while STEERS stays down until mid-November

Inside Climate News · Oct 30, 2026 · TCEQ · checked Oct 31, 2026 · Hays County meeting record · Oct 27, 2026

Inside Climate News reports that the climate around data centers has soured enough that new development in Texas is essentially on pause until after November’s elections. Abbott’s press secretary told the outlet that until data centers complete the ERCOT and TWDB audits, no state agency will issue related permits. Aurora Energy Research’s Olivier Beaufils told the outlet the political risk won’t disappear in November and that he expects some speculative projects in Texas’s 400-plus-gigawatt pipeline to simply disappear. Nothing changed on TCEQ’s STEERS page this morning: the e-filing portal is still down until mid-November, the MSGP renewal deadline is still Dec. 28, and the episodic waiver still allows email filings for MSGP, construction and concrete-production permits. Hays County’s rewritten Development Regulations could come back for adoption Nov. 10 or Dec. 1. My advice is unchanged: plan for manual stormwater filings this fall, and price political risk into any powered-land deal.

Retail & restaurants · Round Rock, Cedar Park, Bastrop, Liberty Hill & Georgetown

Suburban retail keeps filling in: Fogo de Chão for Cedar Park, Burlington for Bastrop, a second Georgetown Tractor Supply, and The District’s restaurant row

Community Impact · Oct 27, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 30, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 15, 2026

Community Impact’s weekly state permit roundup includes a $4.5 million Fogo de Chão at 1005 E. New Hope Drive in Cedar Park, a $3.3 million Burlington at Burleson Crossing East in Bastrop, and a P. Terry’s on Hwy. 29 in Liberty Hill. The same list carries the $97.3 million, 30-story, 300-unit student tower at 2307 Rio Grande St. in West Campus. CI also reports Tractor Supply filed a $2.8 million second Georgetown store on Williams Drive. Catching up on one I missed: in Round Rock, Mark IV Capital broke ground Oct. 15 on a 41,525-square-foot food and retail phase at The District, at I-35 and SH 45, with room for nine to 14 restaurants and completion expected in early 2028. Mark IV’s release calls metro Austin’s retail market among the tightest in the country. That matches what I see: national tenants are still following rooftops north and east, and good pad sites go quickly.

Land & master plans · Georgetown, Round Rock, Granger & Leander

Carried over: Heirloom’s Nov. 5 groundbreaking, KB Home’s 330-home Salerno expansion, Granger’s 765 lots, and Leander’s Oakwood Hills phasing

Community Impact · Oct 29, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 26, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 29, 2026 · Hyperlocal Loop · Oct 29, 2026

Condensed from this week. Georgetown approved the Heirloom PID and $20.89 million in city bonds for the 620-acre, 3,600-unit project near Williams Drive and Ronald Reagan Boulevard, with a Nov. 5 groundbreaking. Round Rock confirmed approval of KB Home’s 71.88-acre Salerno expansion, about 330 homes in two phases (181, then 149), in the subdivision’s existing MUD. Granger approved WBW Development’s 765-lot Lakeside at Granger. Hyperlocal Loop recaps Leander’s Oct. 15 phasing plan for the first 183 homes at the 337-acre Oakwood Hills, plus a Williamson County water-infrastructure study of nearly $500,000. These rooftops are what eventually justify the retail and service pads in the card above.
