Hays County · Manufacturing & land

Daimler Truck North America is eyeing San Marcos for a 2 million SF truck plant, “Project Hercules”

Austin Business Journal · Nov 19, 2026 · City of San Marcos · Daimler Truck

This could be the biggest industrial story along the I-35 corridor this year. Austin Business Journal reports Daimler Truck North America is looking at San Marcos for a roughly 2 million SF plant that would create thousands of jobs. Officials cautioned that the company is still evaluating other places, but San Marcos is the only Texas site on its list. The City of San Marcos has posted a Project Hercules page confirming that, and it says the company expects to choose a site by the end of the year. Daimler Truck’s own announcement from earlier this year describes a brand-new “greenfield” truck plant, with construction expected to begin in late 2026 after a site is chosen and production targeted for 2029. No incentive terms have been made public yet; the San Marcos council has only discussed the project in closed session so far. A plant like this brings suppliers, logistics space and workforce housing with it, so I’ll be watching land and industrial demand from Kyle down to New Braunfels. It isn’t a done deal until Daimler announces the site.

Far East Austin · Industrial

Link Logistics is set to break ground on more than 1 million SF of industrial space north of Tesla’s Gigafactory

Austin Business Journal · Nov 19, 2026 · Our Austin · Nov 13

Austin Business Journal reports that Link Logistics, the industrial company founded by Blackstone, is about to start a project of more than 1 million SF a short distance north of the Gigafactory, and filings show it could be finished late next year. This looks like the same project I’ve been tracking as Project Juniper. The state (TDLR) filing lists a 1,015,000 SF, $67 million warehouse at 11902 FM 969, with a Nov. 23 start and an estimated finish of Dec. 15, 2027. Our Austin notes that earlier Statesman reporting tied the site to Link Logistics, but the filing itself doesn’t name the owner or the tenant. In a companion feature, ABJ describes how Tesla, the airport and the Dog’s Head plans are pulling bigger projects to Austin’s eastern edge. I’m watching who leases Juniper and how it moves industrial rents along the SH 130 corridor.

ABJ: Link Logistics · Our Austin: Project Juniper · ABJ: Far East Austin

Del Valle · Advanced manufacturing

Ambrosia Energy is building a battery-pack factory at Airpark 130 near the airport to supply power for data centers

Austin Business Journal · Nov 19, 2026 · Lee & Associates – Austin

Austin Business Journal reports California-based Ambrosia Energy will make Airpark 130 near Austin-Bergstrom its main manufacturing site. Over the next few years it plans to build out a 10 GWh (gigawatt-hour) battery factory, which works out to about 300,000 battery packs a year, with as many as 200 employees. Its co-founder said a headquarters could eventually come to Austin too. Lee & Associates – Austin, which represented the company, says the lease covers 146,000 SF of distribution warehouse space in Del Valle. It’s another sign that the data-center boom is filling local industrial space with suppliers, not just with server buildings.

Austin City Council · Nov. 19 results

Council approves the $6.35 million Winding Trails utility agreements and a $25 million Lady Bird Lake boardwalk; Howard Plaza is pushed to Dec. 3

City of Austin action notes · KUT · Nov 19, 2026

According to the city’s action notes (which the city calls tentative until the minutes are approved), Council approved on consent both cost-participation agreements with Z Modular. The city will reimburse up to $1.85 million to oversize a water main and up to $4.5 million to oversize wastewater lines for the Winding Trails mixed-use project at 7308 N. FM 973 near SH 130. That city-funded capacity is what opens up neighboring tracts along SH 130. Council also nominated YETI at 7601 Southwest Parkway for a state Texas Enterprise Project designation, started the process of rezoning the John Henry Faulk Building at 800 Guadalupe as a historic landmark, and approved the 3601 Davis Lane and 3908 Menchaca Road rezonings. Howard Plaza (3127 E. Howard Lane) was postponed to Dec. 3 at the neighborhood’s request, as was Foundation Communities’ request to end a restrictive covenant on its 601 W. Braker Lane site, postponed at the applicant’s request. KUT reports Council also accepted TxDOT’s $25 million for the Riverside Shores boardwalk in front of the Hyatt Regency and Related’s One Lady Bird Lake, with the Austin Public Facilities Corporation managing the project.

Data centers · Local follow-ups

Georgetown’s Inner Loop Exchange heads to Council, Austin’s Environmental Commission weighs in on the 3M campus, and Kyle is still unresolved

Community Impact · Nov 19, 2026 · Environmental Commission · LoneStar Network/KVUE · Texas Tribune · Austin Politics

Community Impact has the details on Georgetown P&Z’s unanimous recommendation to deny the 68 MW Inner Loop Exchange. The planned unit development (PUD) zoning that owners Phillip Niels and Tim Keith requested for about 28 acres at NE Inner Loop and E. University Ave. would raise the allowed building footprint from 5,000 SF to 290,000 SF, cut setbacks from 250 to 50 feet and raise roof coverage to 65%. City staff found it didn’t fit the future land use plan, and the city received 22 written comments opposed and none in favor. City Council takes it up Dec. 8, where approval needs six of seven votes (CBS Austin). In Austin, the Environmental Commission’s posted recommendation 20261118-004 on SB Energy’s data center at 6801 River Place Blvd. (the old 3M campus) is sponsored by Commissioners Brimer, Fierro and Sullivan, but the vote count is still blank. In Kyle, I still can’t confirm what Council did on Nov. 17. LoneStar Network’s Nov. 18 recap, drawing on KVUE, describes only the discussion of Council Member Claudia Zapata’s item, and any ban would need at least one more formal vote. For context, a Texas Tribune analysis found Texas cities and counties are forgoing more than $1 billion in property taxes for data centers through 53 agreements that guarantee 465 jobs in total. Austin’s first data-center rules are expected on Council’s Dec. 3 agenda (Austin Politics).

Multifamily & UT land · Condensed

Wood Partners lines up three Austin-area apartment projects, and UT studies housing at its Dobie and Brackenridge sites

Austin Business Journal · Nov 19, 2026

ABJ reports that Atlanta-based Wood Partners is picking up its building pace in the Austin metro, with three apartment complexes slated to start construction in the coming months. The project details are behind ABJ’s paywall, so I’m not repeating them here. ABJ also reports UT is considering future housing at two sites with real redevelopment potential: its Brackenridge land on Lake Austin Blvd. near Hula Hut, and the former Dobie mall beneath the Dobie student housing tower. That comes ahead of Council’s Dec. 3 vote on the Muny agreement.

Earlier this week · Condensed

TCEQ resumes data-center reviews, Indeed’s 308,469 SF sublease, CBD vacancy near 28% and Manifold’s Block 16

Texas Tribune · Austin American-Statesman · Austin Business Journal · Nov 17–18, 2026

TCEQ has resumed its normal permit reviews for data centers now that the governor’s pause is over, and its STEERS online filing system has been back up since Nov. 9 (Texas Tribune). Indeed has listed all 308,469 SF of its space at Indeed Tower for sublease, while Aquila puts CBD vacancy at about 28%, down from roughly 30% (Statesman). Manifold still plans its 47-story, roughly 743,175 SF Block 16 office tower on Trinity Street (ABJ).
