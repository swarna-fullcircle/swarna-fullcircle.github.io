Data centers · State policy & Bastrop County

Governor-elect Hinojosa calls for a pause on new data-center permits, Sen. Perry files a statewide bill, and Cedar Creek neighbors write to Anthropic

KCBD · Fox News · CBS Austin (KEYE) · Nov 19–20, 2026

The data-center debate moved up to the state level this week, and it matters for every land and power conversation I’m having around Austin. In a Fox News interview Thursday evening, Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa said Texas needs “a moratorium, a ban on new data center permits” until lawmakers pass protections for residents. KCBD notes she acknowledged she can’t stop projects already under construction. The same report says Lubbock Sen. Charles Perry has filed a bill with 16 co-authors. It would apply to new data centers after Sept. 1 of next year, limit them to four water-conserving cooling methods, require annual water reporting, let the PUC set limits on water, light, noise and setbacks (a facility that doesn’t comply couldn’t connect to the grid), and cap local property tax breaks at 50%. The Legislature convenes Jan. 12. Closer to home, CBS Austin reports that 73 neighbors near Earl Callahan Road in Cedar Creek (Bastrop County) signed a letter asking Anthropic to support a public hearing on a planned gas plant and data center being built by Pacifico CCK Energy and developed by BlackChamber. An Anthropic spokeswoman told KEYE the company is in talks but has not signed a lease. Residents believe developers are racing for TCEQ approvals before the new administration; that is their claim, not a confirmed fact. For land sellers and buyers, the takeaway is that permit timing and state rules could change how much data-center demand is worth over the next few months.

San Marcos · Industrial investment

Austin-based Blue Yonder Property Group launches a high-power manufacturing platform with GCM Grosvenor, starting with a San Marcos plant

PR Newswire (company release) · Nov 19, 2026

According to its release, Blue Yonder Property Group (BYPG) has formed a partnership with GCM Grosvenor, which invested an initial $62.5 million. The platform is structured to scale up to $300 million of equity and is aimed at about $480 million of manufacturing buildings that already have power in place. Its first acquisition is 2000 Clovis Barker Road in San Marcos: a 75,276 SF crane-served plant on about 30 acres along I-35, with 2.5 MW of secured power. BYPG says a national industrial pipe fabrication company has signed a long-term lease and will fully occupy the building at closing. Coming the same week as Daimler’s Project Hercules news, it tells me something I’m seeing in my own deals: industrial buildings with power already in place are being priced as hard-to-replace assets.

Pflugerville · Multifamily sale

SPI Advisory buys 280-unit Villas at Grand Avenue for $37.5 million, below its 2016 price

Multi-Housing News · Nov 20, 2026 · Newmark · Nov 19

Multi-Housing News reports that Dallas-based SPI Advisory paid $37.5 million for Villas at Grand Avenue, a 2009-built, 280-unit mixed-income community at 1720 Grand Avenue Parkway in Pflugerville. The seller, Jackson Square Properties, is exiting Texas, and the price came in below what the property sold for in 2016. SPI financed the deal with a $28.3 million Freddie Mac loan and $13.7 million in equity, and it plans about $2.2 million in light interior upgrades. The property was about 95% occupied at closing. It has a 50% tax abatement tied to a CHDO agreement that reserves half the units for households at 60% of area median income. Newmark, which arranged both the sale and the financing, cites absorption of 7,979 units in a recent quarter against 2,893 delivered, with construction starts down about 73% from the late-2023 peak. Buyers are getting back into North Austin apartments at a reset basis.

Northwest Austin · Office leasing

Accesso signs four leases totaling 102,751 SF at the 7700 Parmer campus

Accesso (via Citybizlist) · Nov 17–18, 2026

Accesso reports four new leases at 7700 Parmer, its 911,000 SF Class A campus on 129 acres in Northwest Austin. Vaco by Highspring took 27,346 SF and IWG/Regus took 13,448 SF. An unnamed global professional services firm and an unnamed multinational technology company account for the other 61,957 SF. The campus was recently rezoned for mixed use, which allows future office, retail, multifamily and hospitality. With Tesla’s 82,251 SF lease at 717 E. Parmer earlier this week, the north Parmer corridor is clearly getting real tenant activity while downtown works through subleases.

Hutto · Retail recruitment & sales tax

Hutto and its fire district clash over sales tax, and the city says the current split makes it harder to land big retailers

KXAN · Nov 19, 2026 · WilCo Insider · Nov 20

KXAN reports that Hutto Fire Rescue (WilCo ESD No. 3) collects sales tax in certain annexed areas and shares 60% with the city. Mayor Mike Snyder and the Hutto EDC say that arrangement limits the sales-tax incentives that grocers and other major retailers often expect. A petition backed by the EDC would cut the district’s rate from 2% to 0.25%. It needs signatures from 5% of district voters by Dec. 1, and the earliest it could reach the ballot is May 2027. The fire district says it doesn’t take part in economic development and uses the revenue for staffing, equipment and stations. WilCo Insider reports the council and ESD board will hold a joint workshop at 6 p.m. Dec. 1. For anyone working on retail pads near SH 79 and Blue Origin’s planned Constellation Park, this decides how much incentive money Hutto can actually offer.

Leander · Land & master-planned

The Giddens family ranch in west Leander becomes Weslynn, a 584-acre community planned around its live oaks

Austin American-Statesman · Austin Business Journal · Hoodline · Nov 17–21, 2026

This one is close to my office. The Statesman and ABJ are covering Weslynn, Randolph Texas Development’s master-planned community on the former Giddens ranch near Bagdad Road and San Gabriel Parkway. Hoodline, citing a Crestline Investors announcement, reports a $30 million revolving credit facility for the first phase of infrastructure on the 584-acre site: 75 one-acre lots in phase one, all under contract with builders, and 218 half-acre lots in phase two. ABJ’s preview puts full buildout at about 700 homes. The project site lists a 27-acre land reserve, a 12-acre flagship park and first homes coming in late 2027. Large lots on the west side are supporting land values along the Bagdad and San Gabriel corridor, and they will bring neighborhood retail with them.

I still can’t confirm what Kyle City Council did with Council Member Claudia Zapata’s Nov. 17 item directing staff to draft a citywide data-center ban.
