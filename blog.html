Data centers / state permitting · Statewide, with Austin-area projects in the queue

Today is TCEQ’s deadline to update the governor on the data-center permit freeze — and as of this morning, nothing has been made public

REJournals · Oct 19, 2026 · btw.media · Oct 18, 2026 · Office of the Texas Governor · Sept 21, 2026 · Groundbreak · Oct 17, 2026

This is the checkpoint I’ve been flagging all month. Gov. Abbott’s Sept. 21 directive told TCEQ to halt permits sought by data-center projects until ERCOT completes its audit, and to give his office an update on its compliance by Monday, Oct. 19. When I checked TCEQ’s newsroom and the governor’s news page early this morning, neither had posted anything on it, and I haven’t found any report on what the update says — so I’m not going to guess. REJournals’ Monday piece frames the stakes well: the freeze applies whether or not a project sits in the ERCOT queue, which reaches developers planning to generate their own power, and because it covers permits, amendments and renewals, even projects already vertical can run into it. It quotes JLL’s Curt Holcomb that projects already under construction have most likely received the permits they need, while projects still in the interconnection queue typically won’t close or start without a firm power commitment. btw.media’s weekend explainer adds an important nuance: neither Oct. 19 nor ERCOT’s report planned by Dec. 10 is stated as an automatic end date for the pause. Locally, the freeze is the backdrop for projects like Project Longhorn Building One in Cedar Creek — the 498,165-square-foot, $366 million BlackChamber data center Groundbreak reported as approved on Oct. 17, the first of two buildings on a 2,842-acre campus that includes a grid-independent natural gas plant. Groundbreak’s write-up says the project also cleared the statewide permit pause without detailing how, which is exactly the kind of question TCEQ’s update could clarify for pending applications. REJournals counts three dates that now govern the market: Oct. 19, mid-December, and January, when the 90th Legislature convenes. I’ll report what TCEQ’s update says once it’s public.

Retail / investment sales · Oak Hill, Southwest Austin

Cohen & Steers and Trademark buy the 115,512-square-foot Oak Hill Plaza at the “Y,” with a revitalization plan now that the Oak Hill Parkway rebuild is substantially complete

Groundbreak · Oct 18, 2026

A good weekend retail read for anyone watching Southwest Austin. Groundbreak reports that a joint venture of Cohen & Steers Real Estate Opportunities Fund and Trademark Property Company acquired Oak Hill Plaza, a 115,512-square-foot center on 11.28 acres at 7101 State Highway 71 W — the “Oak Hill Y” where US 290 West meets SH 71 — on Aug. 21. The center was 92.5% occupied at acquisition, with tenants including Wells Fargo, The Picklr, Pluckers, Dollar Tree, AutoZone, Jim’s Restaurant and Golden Chick. Partners Capital (Partners Real Estate) sold, Newmark brokered, and terms weren’t disclosed; Trademark will manage, lease and lead a revitalization. The deal follows substantial completion of TxDOT’s $674 million Oak Hill Parkway project, and it’s Cohen & Steers’ second Austin-area shopping center buy this year after 1890 Ranch in Cedar Park. To me, that’s institutional capital betting on well-located, older suburban retail once the road work is behind it.

Office / tenant build-out · The Domain, North Austin

A 38,380-square-foot “Take2 ATX” office build-out starts today at Domain 7

Groundbreak · Oct 19, 2026

A concrete office-leasing signal in North Austin. Groundbreak reports that Take2 ATX, a 38,380-square-foot tenant build-out inside Domain 7 — a 221,973-square-foot Class A building at 11501 Alterra Parkway — is registered with TDLR at an estimated $3.5 million (about $91 per square foot), with construction starting Oct. 19 and completion expected Feb. 5, 2027. The scope covers demolition, new partitions, ceilings, doors, finishes and MEP updates. Groundbreak notes the project name and scope indicate a build-out for Take-Two Interactive; the filing itself lists Domain Junction 7 LLC as owner of record. Build-outs of this size are a reminder that The Domain keeps drawing tech tenants even with metro office vacancy elevated.

Industrial & land / state filings · Southeast and Northeast Austin

“Project Phantom”: a $60 million, 285,000-square-foot warehouse registered near the airport — plus a $45 million filing for Colony Park’s first roads and utilities

The American Developer · Oct 16–17, 2026

Two state filings worth noting for industrial and land clients. The American Developer reports that VC Holdings QOZ LP registered a $60 million new-construction warehouse with TDLR on Oct. 16 — 285,000 square feet at 11505 1/2 Spirit Drive in southeast Austin near Austin-Bergstrom, named “Project Phantom,” with Portland-based Mackenzie as design firm, an estimated Jan. 11, 2027 start and Oct. 11, 2027 completion, and no tenant named. Separately, the City of Austin registered a $45 million site-infrastructure job on Oct. 15 for Colony Park Phase 1 at 7900 Loyola Lane — clearing, roads, sidewalks, utilities, retaining walls and stormwater ponds across about 208 acres of city-owned land that Catellus/Banbury agreed to develop in 2024 — with a listed March 1, 2027 start and Sept. 1, 2028 completion. As the publication notes, TDLR registrations are accessibility filings, not city building permits, so I watch for the site plans and permits that follow.

Multifamily pipeline / permits & housing finance · City of Austin

Austin’s new residential permits are down 8.2% through August, with just 83 for apartment-scale buildings — even as affordable projects keep moving

The American Developer · Oct 17–18, 2026

The supply story behind Austin’s apartment market. Pulling the city’s own permit data, The American Developer counts 1,706 new residential building permits issued Jan. 1–Aug. 31, 2026, down 8.2% from 1,859 a year earlier; only 83 were for buildings with five or more units. It notes CRE Daily, citing The Real Deal and ABJ figures, put the five-county metro decline at 14% overall and 36% for apartments. A thinner pipeline today means fewer deliveries in 2027 and 2028 — good news for owners working through today’s lease-up. Affordable housing is the exception: on Oct. 16 the city issued a building permit for Foundation Communities’ Crossroads redevelopment at 8801 McCann Drive, a four-story SMART Housing building with Swinerton as contractor (the permit lists 90 units in its first fire area; the city’s funding application describes 110 homes). And on Oct. 22 the Austin Housing Finance Corporation board has five proposed loans totaling $21,093,750 on its consent agenda, including up to $7.92 million for Foundation Communities’ Jordan II and up to $5.2 million for The Maven on East Highland Mall Boulevard — proposals until the board votes.

Aerospace & advanced manufacturing · Hutto, Cedar Park, Williamson County

Behind the deal: how Hutto beat Alabama and Florida for Blue Origin — and why Williamson County’s aerospace cluster keeps growing

Austin Business Journal · Oct 17, 2026 · Office of the Texas Governor · Oct 8, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 16, 2026

ABJ’s weekend “Behind the Deal” traces how Blue Origin’s Hutto project started with a request for information code-named “Project Blue Hub” and moved through weekend calls, site visits and incentive talks that sealed it over competition from Alabama and Florida. The governor’s Oct. 8 announcement set the scale: an advanced manufacturing and operations campus with a $554,780,000 capital investment and more than 2,000 new jobs, backed by an $18,171,000 Texas Enterprise Fund grant; Hutto’s mayor called the city an anchor point on the Highway 79 tech corridor. Pair that with Community Impact’s Oct. 16 look at Cedar Park, where Firefly opened a cleanroom four times the size of its previous one and the Central Texas Spaceport Development Corporation is in talks with developers on sites for a campus that could start construction as early as the first quarter of 2027. For industrial owners and land sellers in Williamson County, aerospace suppliers are becoming a real demand driver.

Mixed use / suburban downtowns · Round Rock, Leander, Cedar Park, Hutto

Suburban mixed-use hubs keep taking shape, led by the 41,525-square-foot retail and dining phase now underway at The District in Round Rock

Austin Business Journal · Oct 16, 2026 · Groundbreak · Oct 17, 2026 · Traded · Oct 16, 2026

Still one of the most relevant reads for my north-metro clients. Groundbreak reports Mark IV Capital broke ground Oct. 15 on the 41,525-square-foot retail and dining phase of The District at I-35 and SH 45 in Round Rock. ABJ’s Oct. 16 look at suburban mixed-use hubs, as summarized by Traded, also covers Leander’s 116-acre Northline nearing full buildout with about 20 acres left (including a planned 30,000-square-foot Partners Real Estate retail project), RedLeaf’s Bell District in Cedar Park, where later multifamily and commercial phases have been slowed partly by apartment oversupply, and MA Partners’ 35-acre Co-Op District in Hutto, which counts ASML as a major office tenant. With my office in Leander, I see these districts as where small-shop retail, medical office and restaurant tenants will compete for space over the next few years.
