Data centers / annexation · Taylor / Williamson County

Taylor decides Thursday on the 664-acre Big Watt–PowerHouse campus — 500 MW of load, on-site power, and developers that don’t own the land yet

Bisnow · Oct 5, 2026 · also KUT · Oct 2 · City of Taylor · Oct 2 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 2

The vote I’ve been flagging all week is this Thursday, Oct. 8. Bisnow’s Oct. 5 recap of Austin Business Journal reporting adds useful texture on the Taylor Technology Campus (formerly Project Mustang) at 3301 FM 112: it’s planned for 500 megawatts of data center load with behind-the-meter, on-site power generation, the unnamed tenant can add another 500 MW, and Big Watt Digital and PowerHouse Data Centers don’t own the site yet. A commissioned report cited by Bisnow projects $1.4 billion in gross tax revenue for Taylor from 2027 to 2046, 320 full-time jobs, and $28.2 million in community benefits, with nearly $5 billion in economic output to Williamson County over two decades. City materials, as KUT reported Oct. 2, call for closed-loop cooling on reclaimed water only, developer-paid utility work, wide setbacks, and noise limits. Bisnow also notes the local backdrop: residents sued the city in July seeking an election on a temporary data-center ban. However council votes, this sets the template for how east Williamson County prices large-acreage power sites.

Data centers / industrial land · Hutto / Williamson County

Skybox plans a February close on Hutto’s 140-acre “Project Sequel,” marketing it as industrial build-to-suit while it waits on grid capacity until 2028

The Real Deal Texas · Oct 5, 2026 · also Groundbreak Austin · Oct 5

Just down the road, the power question is reshaping the Hutto megasite. The Real Deal Texas reports Oct. 5, citing Austin Business Journal, that Skybox Datacenters has secured rezoning for its 140-acre Project Sequel and, according to chief development officer Haynes Strader, plans to close on the property in February. Skybox and Prologis aim to market the land as an industrial build-to-suit while pursuing enough power for two data centers on about half the site, but under the state’s current grid-connection regime following Gov. Abbott’s summer directive to ERCOT, Skybox won’t know its power allocation until 2028. Skybox’s first Hutto project, the 150-acre PowerCampus Austin, has leased its four planned buildings to tenants including Google, per the outlet. Meanwhile Groundbreak Austin reported Oct. 5 that Prologis’ 846,300-square-foot Skybox Hutto 4 at 3399 County Road 132 is approved, with completion expected in late October 2028. For industrial users, a large entitled site with optional data-center upside is an unusual opportunity on the northeast ring.

Entitlements / permitting · City of Austin

Austin’s city manager gives Development Services the final say when departments clash over development rules

Austin Free Press · Oct 5, 2026

This one matters to every site plan in the city. Austin Free Press’ Chad Swiatecki reports Oct. 5 that a Sept. 25 memo from City Manager T.C. Broadnax names Development Services Director Keith Mars as the final department-level authority when departments disagree over how development rules apply, and says some review functions now handled by Watershed Protection and Parks and Recreation will move to Development Services. Mars told the outlet a new Executive Permitting Committee of department leaders will handle conflicts and that specialists will still provide technical analysis; the memo doesn’t yet spell out which functions shift or when. Mayor Pro Tem Chito Vela called it a needed fix for interdepartmental gridlock, while Council Member Marc Duchen said it surprised him and raised concerns about losing specialist expertise. For owners and developers, a single arbiter could shorten the delays that kill deal timelines, so I’ll be watching how the framework is actually implemented.

Office capital markets · Downtown and Northwest Austin

Two office trades, two different stories: Riverside’s value-add Lavaca Plaza buy and the 85%-leased Research Park Plaza V

Connect CRE · Oct 5, 2026 · also REBusinessOnline via Real Estate Trail · Oct 5 · JLL · Aug 4 · Bisnow · Sep 30

Office sales keep showing the split in this market. Connect CRE reports Oct. 5 that private equity investor Riverside bought Lavaca Plaza, the 11-story, 122,042-square-foot tower at 504 Lavaca St. built in 1981, from Unico Properties for an undisclosed price; Commercial Search reports Unico paid $61.4 million in 2015. The building is 46.2% vacant, has 476 parking spaces on floors one through seven with office on the top four, and JLL marketed it as a possible redevelopment play. On the stabilized side, REBusinessOnline’s Oct. 5 coverage (summarized by Real Estate Trail) highlights JLL’s sale of Research Park Plaza V at 12301 Research Blvd., a four-story, 172,854-square-foot Class A building from 2015 that was 85% leased; JLL’s Aug. 4 release says it represented Cousins Properties, with Cadence and Planview among the tenants and the buyer unnamed. For context, Bisnow’s Sept. 30 piece cites JLL data putting Austin office vacancy around 25.8% in Q2, with a third straight quarter of positive net absorption and the construction pipeline down to 1.7 million square feet.

Retail / grocery anchors · San Marcos, Buda, and North Austin

H-E-B opens a 114,000 SF San Marcos store Wednesday as grocery-anchored retail keeps marching down I-35

MySA · Oct 5, 2026 · also MySA · Oct 4 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 5

The southern I-35 corridor has a grocery opening this week. MySA’s Nicholas Hernandez reports Oct. 5 that H-E-B’s newest San Marcos store, a 114,000-square-foot location at I-35 and McCarty Lane (1074 E. McCarty Lane), opens at 6 a.m. Wednesday, Oct. 7. It’s the third H-E-B in San Marcos and includes an H-E-B Fresh Bites with fuel and a car wash plus the city’s first True Texas BBQ. That follows MySA’s Oct. 4 report on H-E-B’s nearly $61.2 million store planned on a former landfill site at 15300 S. I-35 in Buda. On the investment side, Austin Business Journal reported Oct. 5 that two shopping centers north of Austin have new owners amid an increase in retail transactions; the details are behind ABJ’s paywall, so check the story for specifics. Grocery anchors landing this fast along I-35 tell me where shadow-anchored pad and inline demand will follow.

Mixed-use pipeline · South Congress

Congress Views proposes 350 units and 90 feet of mixed use at 6111–6119 South Congress

Groundbreak Austin · Oct 4, 2026

Far South Congress is getting a taller proposal. Groundbreak Austin reports Oct. 4 that Congress Corner LLC has filed Congress Views, a 350-unit mixed-use project rising 90 feet across 6111, 6113, and 6119 South Congress Ave. in 78745, with 42 affordable units (about 12%), a component typically tied to the city’s density bonus programs. It’s the project’s first appearance in tracking records and still needs site plan review and permitting. At that height it would stand above most of that stretch of the corridor, and I read it as a sign that density bonuses are pushing mixed-use further south along a major transit line.

Healthcare development · South Austin

St. David’s is under construction on a $180M, 160,000 SF patient tower at its Ben White campus

Groundbreak Austin · Oct 3, 2026

Medical construction keeps showing up in the pipeline. Groundbreak Austin reports Oct. 3 that St. David’s South Austin Medical Center’s new patient tower at 901 W. Ben White Blvd. has moved from proposed to active construction: a $180 million, four-story addition of 160,000 square feet, designed by Polkinghorn Group Architects. The program includes a 30-bed patient care unit, a 20-bed inpatient rehab unit, four more operating rooms with shell space for future expansion, a new utility powerhouse, and sterile processing, with completion set for the end of 2027. Hospital campus expansion is one of the steadiest generators of nearby medical office and service retail demand along the US 290/71 frontage.

Occupier demand · Austin MSA

Doodle Labs is still hunting Austin space — WeWork for now, hybrid office-plus-testing next, with SoCo in the mix

The Real Deal Texas · Oct 2, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Oct 1

I’m keeping one live tenant requirement on the board. The Real Deal Texas reported Oct. 2 (citing Austin Business Journal) that defense-tech company Doodle Labs has moved its U.S. headquarters from the Los Angeles area to the Austin metro, is working out of a WeWork, and is searching for a hybrid office and testing location, possibly across multiple sites, with the South Congress corridor of particular interest. ABJ’s Oct. 1 piece says local headcount could more than quintuple by the end of 2027. That reads to me as flex and small-to-mid bay demand with a testing overlay, not just another HQ announcement.
