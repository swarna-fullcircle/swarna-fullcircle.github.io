Office leasing · Downtown Austin

Austin Transit Partnership locks in a leaner downtown office expansion on Colorado

Community Impact · Sep 23, 2026

Downtown Class A demand is showing up as public-sector leasing with a shorter leash. Community Impact reports the Austin Transit Partnership board on Sept. 21 approved a $13.5 million, 48-month lease to renew its current office at 203 Colorado St. and expand next door at 201 Colorado St., for a combined about 54,000 square feet as the agency plans to grow from roughly 200 staff to about 280 while Project Connect / Austin Light Rail ramps up. The board also approved about $6 million for finish-out and improvements and $400,000 to extend the current lease through February 2027. That package replaces the earlier, postponed path toward a roughly $32 million, 90-month lease of about 51,600 square feet at 100 Congress Avenue (plus a separate finish-out contract) that Mayor Kirk Watson and others had pressed ATP to rethink on cost. ATP’s Alex Gale said the Colorado option did not exist when the Congress deal was first teed up and that co-locating contractors could save up to about $50,000 a month in third-party space. For me, this is the CRE office read: shorter-term, cost-disciplined downtown absorption still lands square footage — just not the trophy long-term commitment first floated.

Data centers / regulation · Statewide (MSA impact)

Abbott’s TCEQ data-center permit pause is live — and Bastrop’s Anthropic-eyed campus is the first clear pause read

Texas Tribune · Sep 21, 2026 · also Community Impact · Austin Business Journal · Sep 22 · Axios Austin

Digital-infrastructure entitlement just got another statewide brake — and the MSA already has a named project in the crosshairs. The Texas Tribune reports Gov. Greg Abbott on Sept. 21 directed TCEQ Executive Director Kelly Keel to halt new permits sought by data centers until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board finish audits covering electricity use, water use, community impact, ownership, and tax breaks. Community Impact’s Sep. 22 follow-up underscores that the order also hits “behind-the-meter” builds that try to bypass ERCOT connection, because large construction and air/water authorizations still run through TCEQ; Abbott’s letter still asks TCEQ for a compliance update by Oct. 19, 2026, and ERCOT has said it expects to finish its audit in December. Austin Business Journal then flagged what it called the first project clearly impacted: a high-profile Bastrop County gas-powered data-center campus that AI company Anthropic has been in talks to lease appears paused. Axios Austin and The Real Deal had earlier tied Anthropic to talks on a planned about 2,842-acre Cedar Creek campus with roughly 710 megawatts of natural-gas generation supporting data-center load (BlackChamber Partners / Pacifico Energy; land in parcels near Earl Callahan Road), with residents and Lost Pines Watch already pressing TCEQ over air permitting. For CRE around Taylor, Bastrop, Georgetown, and Four Points, I’m reading this as schedule risk that is no longer theoretical — air/water pause plus named east-MSA campus friction even when local land talks looked advanced.

Land / mixed-use · Southeast Austin

Lawsuit seeks to void Dog’s Head development agreement and TIRZ financing

Austin Current · Sep 21, 2026 · also KUT · Austin Business Journal

The MSA’s biggest mixed-use land story is still in court, not just Council chamber. Austin Current and KUT report that Save Our Springs Alliance, PODER, and nearby residents sued the City of Austin and City Council seeking to void both the 45-year Dog’s Head development agreement with Endeavor and the Tax Increment Reinvestment Zone financing that would return more than $1 billion in property and sales taxes to help pay for infrastructure over 35 years. Plaintiffs call the financing an “illegal and unconstitutional use of taxes” and argue the agreement unconstitutionally delegates legislative authority; they also say the site does not meet the state’s “unproductive, underdeveloped, or blighted” TIRZ test given its location beside major highways, Austin-Bergstrom, and Tesla’s Gigafactory. City officials have estimated the roughly 2,600-acre Southeast Austin project (Colorado River, US 183, SH 130) could generate as much as $3.5 billion in tax revenue over 30 years; Endeavor’s first announced tenant remains Amazon’s planned robotics facility. A city spokesperson said Austin had not been served as of Monday evening and would respond through the courts. For me, this is still the CRE risk read: even after unanimous Council action, title-to-delivery on mega mixed-use turns on whether the development agreement and TIRZ survive litigation.

Data centers / adaptive reuse · Northwest Austin

Four Points town hall zeroes in on SoftBank’s former 3M campus — R&D campus or data center?

Community Impact · Sep 18, 2026 · also Austin American-Statesman

Inside city limits, the digital-infrastructure debate still has a very specific address — and Abbott’s TCEQ pause only raises the stakes. Community Impact reports dozens of Four Points residents met Sept. 17 with City of Austin officials and an SB Energy representative about the former 3M campus off River Place Boulevard — a Karlin Real Estate “Highpoint 2222” adaptive-reuse play that Travis County records show sold to SB Energy in early 2026. SB Energy’s Jaime Carlson framed Project Cosmos as renovating the site for U.K. semiconductor maker Graphcore’s U.S. engineering headquarters to research, develop, and test power-dense chips, with no zoning change, noise limits honored, closed-loop chillers aimed at holding water use at or below historic 3M levels, and electric capacity capped at 50 megawatts. Residents still pressed on wildfire risk, vibration, and whether the campus stays R&D or becomes a data center — an Aug. 31 SEC filing cited in coverage lists Cosmos as a data center. Separately, the Austin American-Statesman’s earlier SEC-filing read (SE Cosmos LLC; ~107 acres purchased in April) describes nearly $1 billion in financing to convert the roughly 1.1 million-square-foot office campus into an R&D technology center with about 50 megawatts of critical IT load, plus a SoftBank-affiliate lease expected to generate roughly $2.5 billion for data-center and office uses — all while Austin City Council works toward restricting large and hyperscale data centers in city limits. Paired with the statewide air/water permit pause, this remains the in-city CRE collision: recycled campus inventory, substation upgrades, water benchmarking, and entitlement politics on one of Northwest Austin’s largest tech sites.

Industrial / rail logistics · Taylor

Partners Real Estate plans TaylorPort 3 — a 573,000 SF rail-served building at RCR

Taylor Press · Sep 18, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal

Northern MSA industrial keeps stacking next to Samsung. Taylor Press reports Partners Real Estate is advancing TaylorPort 3 — a planned 573,000-square-foot building with loading docks along the dedicated rail line at RCR Taylor Logistics Park — still in planning and design, with no construction start date disclosed yet. It would join Partners’ existing TaylorPort inventory on about 58 acres: a 183,000-square-foot rail-served building occupied by Tesla and a 366,000-square-foot building for Compal USA Technology. Managing director Mitchell Hanzik called TaylorPort 3 the largest of the three phases and said the newest building will occupy Partners’ last parcel at RCR, with the firm potentially looking for more Taylor-area land. Austin Business Journal separately highlighted the 573,000-square-foot rail-served product (cross-dock configuration, office pods, truck courts, heavy power) and Compal’s lease next door. For me, this is the supplier-and-logistics CRE read beside Samsung’s Taylor fab: speculative big-box inventory racing to catch institutional tenants before the park’s remaining parcels are gone.

Advanced manufacturing · Georgetown

Aalo Atomics plans $25M Georgetown build-out — 204,350 SF for factory-made nuclear

Community Impact · Sep 17, 2026 · also Austin American-Statesman

Northern MSA industrial isn’t only warehouses — it’s also the factories feeding the power story behind data centers. Community Impact reports Austin-based Aalo Atomics plans a $25 million advanced-manufacturing expansion in Georgetown, renovating an existing 204,350-square-foot one-story warehouse at 4813 N. I-35 for lab, machine-shop, and production-floor space, with work estimated from Nov. 15, 2026, through Jan. 30, 2028, per a Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation filing. Aalo designs compact, factory-built nuclear plants aimed primarily at powering AI data centers; the company says it started a self-sustaining reaction with its Critical Test Reactor on July 4 after building the CTR in less than eight months. For me, this is the CRE overlay on the Abbott/TCEQ pause: even as state permitting slows hyperscale interconnects, advanced-manufacturing users that serve the same power demand are still taking warehouse product along the I-35 corridor.

Data centers / land · Taylor

Texas AG opens investigation into Taylor data center site tied to 1999 parkland deed

KUT · Sep 17, 2026 · also Texas Attorney General · Austin Business Journal

Entitlement risk stays loud on one of Taylor’s contested digital-infrastructure sites — especially beside the new statewide permit pause. KUT and a Sept. 17 Texas Attorney General press release report that Ken Paxton’s office is investigating whether a proposed Blueprint Projects AI data center can proceed on land conveyed in 1999 “to be held in trust for future use as parkland.” Coverage traces the chain: a conveyance to the Texas Parks and Recreation Foundation, later transfers to the City of Taylor and the Taylor Economic Development Corporation, then a 2025 sale of the property to NCP Travis TPP Project, LLC for $10 million. Blueprint’s planned facility is described as about 135,000 square feet with an on-site electricity substation; a residents’ lawsuit was previously dismissed for lack of standing and remains on appeal. Austin Business Journal separately flagged the state investigation on Sept. 18. For me, this is the CRE overlay: title history and charitable-trust questions can freeze or reshape a data-center land play even after local economic-development conveyances.
