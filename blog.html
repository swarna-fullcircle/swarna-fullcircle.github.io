Data centers / land use · City of Austin & Taylor

Austin sets a Dec. 3 vote on its first data-center rules, and Inside Climate News takes a hard look at Taylor’s three approved campuses

City of Austin memo (published by KXAN) · Oct 28, 2026 · KXAN via Fox21 · Oct 2026 · Inside Climate News · Nov 1, 2026 · TCEQ · checked Nov 1, 2026

The City of Austin’s Oct. 28 staff memo lays out a two-phase plan. In Phase 1, staff would set non-land-use requirements for large-scale data centers in City Code Title 4 and may add a “data center” definition. The proposal posts to the Council agenda Nov. 20, and Council takes it up Dec. 3. Phase 2 is the zoning piece: data centers become a land use with rules by zoning district. A public draft is due in February or March 2027, followed by Planning Commission in April and Council in April or May. The memo says Austin isn’t seeing hyperscale-type requests, citing limited land and high real estate values. As of Sept. 30, Austin Energy had 13 inquiries from potential data centers seeking 20 MW or more, none above 75 MW, totaling 664 MW over 12 years if every one were built, which staff calls unlikely. In KXAN’s report, Council Member Mike Siegel said he expects a ban on large data centers in December. Just up the road, Inside Climate News reports this morning that Taylor has approved three data centers that together could use an estimated 1.42 gigawatts. They are Blueprint Projects, a 135,000-square-foot, 60 MW complex that starts construction later this year; KDC Project Comal, $2.5 billion and 360 MW, which still needs rezoning; and the 664-acre Taylor Technology Campus, annexed Oct. 8 with construction possible in 2027. Taylor’s new digital-infrastructure code isn’t expected to be ratified until 2027 and wouldn’t apply to projects already approved. Meanwhile, TCEQ’s STEERS page is unchanged this morning: it is still down until mid-November, and the MSGP deadline is still Dec. 28. My read: inside Austin, the window for large powered-land plays is closing. The live opportunities are moving to the suburbs and unincorporated counties, and so is the political risk.

Industrial & advanced manufacturing · Southeast Austin, Manor & Kyle

A vacant Met Center building is 100% preleased before closing, Purchase Corner takes 108,000 SF in Manor, and a new prototyping plant picks South Austin

FORMATION (LinkedIn) · Oct 27, 2026 · Lee & Associates · Oct 29, 2026 · Austin American-Statesman · Oct 29, 2026 · Austin Capital News · Oct 31, 2026 · Transwestern · Oct 27, 2026

FORMATION says that, with New York Life Investors, it closed an off-market purchase of Met Center 5, a 154,000-square-foot building in the Met Center business park near the airport. The building was vacant when FORMATION went into escrow and was 100% preleased to a credit tenant, not named, before closing. First United Bank & Trust provided the financing. FORMATION cites very high power, abundant parking, full HVAC and security as the features that drove demand. Lee & Associates represented Austin-based home décor maker Purchase Corner in a 108,000-square-foot lease at Quarter Crossing, 10111 Hill Lane in Manor. The Statesman reports Advanced Manufacturing Partners (AMP) will open a flagship prototyping plant in South Austin, with production starting in January. Austin Capital News adds that it will open with about $30 million in equipment, in partnership with ACMI; the exact site and headcount haven’t been disclosed. Down I-35, Transwestern Investments and Balcones Real Estate Group have broken ground on Kyle Commerce Center, a 328,256-square-foot, four-building shallow-bay project at 278 Republic Drive with 30 suites from roughly 4,500 to 20,800 square feet, delivering in 2027. My read: powered, move-in-ready buildings and small-bay suites are still leasing fast, even with big-box vacancy elevated.

Retail & mixed-use · Kyle, Cedar Park, Bastrop & Georgetown

Kyle Park starts construction on about 412,000 SF of retail at I-35 and Bebee Road, and it’s largely leased

Transwestern · Oct 27, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 27, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 30, 2026

Transwestern Investments and NewQuest have formed a joint venture and started construction on the retail portion of Kyle Park. It is part of a roughly 101-acre mixed-use project at the northeast corner of I-35 and Bebee Road, and Transwestern Development plans a future multifamily phase. The lifestyle center is planned at about 412,588 square feet of big-box, inline and pad space. Transwestern says it’s largely leased, with anchors and junior anchors including Dick’s Sporting Goods, Burlington, HomeGoods, ULTA Beauty, TJ Maxx, Barnes & Noble, Boot Barn and EOS Fitness. From this week’s permit filings, also in the retail pipeline: Community Impact’s list includes a $4.5 million Fogo de Chão in Cedar Park and a $3.3 million Burlington at Burleson Crossing East in Bastrop. CI also reports a $2.8 million second Tractor Supply in Georgetown. My read: a regional center this size, mostly preleased before vertical construction, says a lot about how much confidence retailers have in Hays County rooftops.

Office · Arboretum, Northwest Austin, Central Austin & downtown

Flex office keeps expanding: Lucid Private Offices opens 22,329 SF at Stonebridge Plaza, while smaller, well-leased buildings keep trading

Realty News Report · Oct 31, 2026 · Lucid Private Offices · Austin Business Journal · Oct 30, 2026 · Commercial Property Executive · Oct 30, 2026 · CRE Daily · Oct 30, 2026

Realty News Report says Lucid Private Offices, a national coworking operator focused on private offices, has opened its first Austin location: 22,329 square feet at Stonebridge Plaza in the Arboretum/Domain area. Lucid’s site lists Suite 900 at 9600 N. MoPac. A second location at Las Cimas III in Westlake is set to open in April 2027. Last week’s office sales round out the picture. ABJ reports a local firm bought Gabriel’s Court, about 45,000 square feet of office and retail at 2905 San Gabriel St. that is about 8% vacant, in its first sale in some 40 years; price wasn’t disclosed. Commercial Property Executive notes that Brick Row lifted Lakeside at Quarry Oaks from 36% to 98% occupancy before selling it to DML Capital. JLL highlights that the building sits next to the planned 300-acre UT Dell Medical Center, set to open in 2030. Downtown, CRE Daily recaps Indeed’s 308,000-square-foot sublease listing and the roughly 456,000 square feet Meta still has to fill. My read: tenants want flexibility and well-amenitized suites, and investors are paying for buildings with a clear leasing story.

Industrial & advanced manufacturing · Bastrop County

Carried over: Musk’s companies start more factory buildings in Bastrop, one with a planned 2.14 million-square-foot footprint

Austin Business Journal · Oct 30, 2026 · Bloomberg · May 21, 2026 · TCEQ air permit record

Yesterday’s lead, condensed. ABJ reports that work has started on more factory buildings at Elon Musk’s Bastrop campus, including one planned with a 2.14 million-square-foot footprint, as part of the plan to launch data centers into space. Bloomberg reported in May that Bastrop County permit filings describe a SpaceX solar-cell facility totaling 10 gigawatts. TCEQ’s database still shows the “Starlink Factory Bastrop” air permit application at 858 FM 1209 as pending. I still haven’t seen a published investment or job figure for the new buildings.

Land & master plans · Georgetown, Round Rock, Granger & Leander

Carried over: Heirloom breaks ground Thursday, plus KB Home’s Salerno expansion, Granger’s 765 lots and Leander’s Oakwood Hills

Community Impact · Oct 29, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 26, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 29, 2026 · Hyperlocal Loop · Oct 29, 2026

Georgetown approved the Heirloom PID and $20.89 million in city bonds for the 620-acre, 3,600-unit project near Williams Drive and Ronald Reagan Boulevard, and it breaks ground Thursday, Nov. 5. Round Rock approved KB Home’s 71.88-acre Salerno expansion, about 330 homes in two phases. Granger approved WBW Development’s 765-lot Lakeside at Granger, and Leander set phasing for the first 183 homes at the 337-acre Oakwood Hills. These rooftops are what eventually justify retail like Kyle Park.
