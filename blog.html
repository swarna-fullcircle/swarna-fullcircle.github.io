Data centers / annexation · Taylor / Williamson County

Vote day in Taylor: council takes up the Big Watt–PowerHouse agreement tonight, with 500 MW, an on-site gas plant, and $28.2 million in pledges on the table

Austin American-Statesman · Oct 7, 2026 · also Axios Austin · Oct 7 · City of Taylor · Oct 2

Today is the day I’ve been flagging all week. Taylor City Council’s regular meeting is at 6 p.m. tonight, Thursday, Oct. 8, in Council Chambers at 400 Porter St., and the agenda includes possible action on the development agreement to annex the 664-acre Taylor Technology Campus (formerly Project Mustang). As of this morning I haven’t seen any reported outcome, so this is a preview. The Statesman’s Oct. 7 story adds detail I hadn’t seen: the agreement calls for 500 megawatts of data center capacity with an option for another 500 MW in a future project, powered by the site’s own natural gas-fired plant that could sell power back to the grid. The developers’ $28.2 million package breaks down to $13 million for parks and quality-of-life priorities, $10.2 million for Taylor ISD programs, $1.5 million in scholarships, $1 million for workforce development, $1 million for animal care, and about $1.5 million for nonprofits. An RCLCO analysis projects about $4.8 billion in economic output and roughly $1.38 billion in city tax revenue through 2046. Axios Austin notes that Gov. Greg Abbott has temporarily halted grid connections and state environmental approvals for new data centers and that it isn’t clear whether that affects this project, while HALT Taylor Data Centers is urging council to reject the deal. I’ll report the result once it’s published.

Data centers · Cedar Creek / Bastrop County

EdgeConneX adds two $600 million buildings in Cedar Creek, pushing its state filings there to $5.84 billion

Community Impact · Oct 7, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Oct 7

Bastrop County keeps stacking up hyperscale numbers. Community Impact’s Amanda Cutshall reports Oct. 7 that two new Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation filings, EDCAUS15 and EDCAUS16, describe two 591,400-square-foot data center buildings at 6402 FM 535, each estimated at $600 million, with construction scheduled from Nov. 2 to May 15, 2028. That brings the six buildings at EdgeConneX’s second Cedar Creek campus to a combined $4 billion, and the company’s nine filings across three planned campuses to about 5.83 million square feet and $5.84 billion. Separately, the Austin Business Journal reported Oct. 7 that the Pacifico/BlackChamber data center Anthropic has been eyeing in Bastrop County could break ground soon. For anyone holding land, industrial, or housing sites east of Austin along SH 71, this is the demand story to watch.

Retail pipeline · Buda, North Austin, Liberty Hill

A $61.25 million H-E-B in Buda, a $36 million Kia dealership on North I-35, and a Walmart Supercenter in Liberty Hill lead September’s retail filings

Austin American-Statesman · Oct 7, 2026

The Statesman’s Travis Webb pulled the largest retail projects from September’s TDLR filings, and they span the whole metro. H-E-B’s long-awaited new Buda store is filed at 132,563 square feet and an estimated $61.25 million, near the existing Buda store, with construction listed from May 1 to June 12, 2028. A Kia dealership near I-35 and Chevy Chase Drive in North Austin (7616 N. I-35 and 7715 and 7745 Chevy Chase Drive) is filed as a 206,601-square-foot retail automotive project at $36 million, slated for March 1 to Sept. 30, 2028. And Walmart plans a 176,246-square-foot Supercenter in Liberty Hill at an estimated $12.2 million, starting Oct. 26, 2026 and finishing Nov. 11, 2027, with a separate fuel station filing. The Statesman notes these filings are preliminary and subject to change. A Walmart Supercenter in Liberty Hill is a meaningful anchor for our side of Williamson County, and I expect pad and inline interest around it.

Downtown retail and office · Congress Avenue

Punch Bowl Social closes on Congress Avenue, freeing up more than 20,000 SF downtown, as Buchalter goes looking for more office space

Austin Business Journal · Oct 7, 2026

Two Congress Avenue items from the same day point in different directions. The Austin Business Journal’s Sahar Chmais reports Oct. 7 that Punch Bowl Social has closed its Congress Avenue location after more than six years, leaving a space of more than 20,000 square feet up for grabs. Separately, ABJ’s Sean Hemmersmeier reports that national law firm Buchalter, whose Austin office is at 515 Congress Ave., has hired all of the attorneys at an established Texas firm focused on business and real estate, and that the search is on for more office space. Both stories are behind ABJ’s paywall. A large eatertainment box downtown is a hard space to backfill, but law firms growing on Congress is the kind of tenant demand that keeps the core moving.

Industrial / flex · Kyle and Pflugerville

Kyle Technology Park delivers its first 75,000 SF of flex industrial, and Brennan buys a Pflugerville outdoor-storage site in a sale-leaseback

Community Impact · Oct 7, 2026 · also REBusinessOnline · Oct 6

Small-bay and IOS keep getting attention. Community Impact reports Oct. 7 that Phase I of Kyle Technology Park, at FM 1626 and Plum Creek Meadows, wrapped in late September: three 25,000-square-foot buildings for multi-tenant warehouse and flex uses on a 41-acre site, built by Cadence McShane with TIG Real Estate Services and Studio 8 Architects. At full build-out the park is planned for up to 11 buildings totaling about 300,000 square feet with office, industrial, showroom, and build-to-suit options. And REBusinessOnline reports Oct. 6 that Chicago-based Brennan Investment Group bought a 13,700-square-foot industrial outdoor storage facility in Pflugerville via a sale-leaseback with an undisclosed seller/tenant; it sits on 3.4 acres and has 24-foot clear heights, nine grade-level doors, two five-ton cranes, and a wash bay. Price wasn’t disclosed. I hear regularly from service and trade businesses looking for exactly this kind of space.

Construction capital · South Congress and Southwest Austin

BGO is the lender behind Intracorp’s $173 million Leland South Congress loan, and OHT is underway on 60 acres in Southwest Austin

Connect CRE · Oct 7, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Oct 7

A follow-up to yesterday’s Leland item, now with the lender named. Connect CRE reports Oct. 7 that BGO provided the $173 million construction loan for Intracorp’s Leland South Congress, with Leifer Properties as co-developer and Northland Properties and Aron Industries as partners. Citing ABJ and the approved site plan, it describes about 635,000 square feet with 265 condos, nearly 22,000 square feet of ground-floor retail, and a 6,000-square-foot outdoor dining paseo in a seven-story building by Andres Construction and Kirksey Architecture, with completion expected in 2029. Also on Oct. 7, ABJ reported that OHT Partners has broken ground on its 60-acre Southwest Austin multifamily project, which ABJ says won’t look like a traditional apartment complex. Lenders are still writing big checks here, just for well-located, differentiated projects.

Entitlements / permitting · City of Austin

Austin is replacing its 20-year-old AMANDA permitting system under nearly $60 million in contracts, on top of a new single arbiter at Development Services

The Daily Texan · Oct 7, 2026 · also Austin Free Press · Oct 5

Permitting reform is turning into a two-part story. The Daily Texan reports Oct. 7 that City Council approved two contracts with Clariti Cloud, Inc. at its Sept. 24 meeting: up to $28.1 million for permitting, licensing, and land management software, including development application review, and up to $31.2 million for enterprise case management covering licensing, permitting, inspection, and enforcement. Together they replace the AMANDA system the city has used for 20 years, and the city says implementation will happen in phases over the next three years. That lands alongside City Manager T.C. Broadnax’s Sept. 25 memo, reported by Austin Free Press on Oct. 5, naming Development Services Director Keith Mars the final department-level authority when departments disagree over how development rules apply. Faster, more predictable reviews would help every deal timeline I work on, so I’m watching how both roll out.
