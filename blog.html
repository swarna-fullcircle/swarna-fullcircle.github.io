Statewide · Data-center permitting

TCEQ resumes data-center permit reviews after the governor’s pause ends, as local fights move to Georgetown Council and Austin’s Dec. 3 rules

Texas Tribune · Nov 17, 2026 · WOAI · Nov 18 · TCEQ · Austin Environmental Commission · KXAN · KVUE

This is the statewide shift I’ve been waiting on, and a correction to my own calendar: I had been reporting STEERS as down, but TCEQ says the system came back online Nov. 9 for TPDES general-permit filings (Construction, Multi-Sector and Concrete Production). The Texas Tribune reports TCEQ has now resumed reviewing data-center permits after Gov. Greg Abbott’s office notified the agency on Friday, Nov. 13, that ERCOT had finished gathering information on community impacts. “The agency has resumed its normal permit review process,” a TCEQ spokesperson said. WOAI notes the notice didn’t share ERCOT’s findings, and advocates at Public Citizen criticized the lack of transparency. The Tribune also points out that Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa campaigned on keeping approvals paused until lawmakers act; ERCOT’s full report goes to the PUC on Dec. 10, with discussion at the Dec. 17 PUC open meeting. Locally, Austin’s Environmental Commission has posted Recommendation 20261118-004 for SB Energy’s COSMOS data center on the former 3M campus, calling for a 50 MW IT / 70 MW total power cap, no potable water for cooling and noise monitoring near three schools (the posted document leaves the vote tally blank). In Georgetown, KXAN confirms P&Z’s unanimous denial recommendation for Inner Loop Exchange, and the developer can still take it to Council in December. In Kyle, I still haven’t seen a report of what council decided on Council Member Claudia Zapata’s ban item; any ordinance would have to return for a formal vote (KVUE).

Office · Downtown sublease

Indeed puts all 308,469 SF at Indeed Tower on the sublease market, even as downtown vacancy dips to about 28%

Austin American-Statesman (via Yahoo) · Nov 18, 2026

The Statesman reports Indeed has listed its entire 308,469 SF at 200 W. Sixth St. for sublease, about 42% of the building, roughly three years after moving in. If it lands a subtenant, Indeed plans to move downtown staff to its existing Domain office. That puts two of Austin’s largest subleases about a block apart: Aquila Commercial counted roughly 520,000 SF of vacant Meta sublease space at Sixth and Guadalupe, on the market for 45 months. The broader numbers are actually improving. Aquila put CBD vacancy at about 28% at the end of September, down from roughly 30%, with direct vacancy at 21.4% and vacant sublease space down to about 955,000 SF. The Domain sat near 5.3%, while East Austin was about 41%, driven largely by an empty Springdale Green. Citywide Class A base rents averaged about $39 per SF (before operating expenses) and Class B about $27. For tenants I advise, a block of quality sublease space downtown means real negotiating leverage.

Downtown · Office pipeline

Manifold is still planning its 47-story Block 16 office tower on Trinity Street

Austin Business Journal · Nov 18, 2026

Austin Business Journal has an update on a plan that’s been in the works for more than a decade: Manifold Development still wants to build an office hub on Trinity Street near the Austin Convention Center site. Current plans call for a 47-story, 723-foot tower of roughly 743,175 SF, designed by Gensler, replacing a P.F. Chang’s and other businesses. Next to the Indeed news, it’s a reminder that developers are still betting on downtown’s long game even with Waterline’s new space mostly unleased (Statesman).

Eastern Travis County · Land & infrastructure

Council considers up to $6.35 million today to oversize utilities for the 260-acre Winding Trails project at FM 973 and SH 130

Texas Construction Hub · Nov 16, 2026

Texas Construction Hub reports Austin City Council is set to consider two cost-participation agreements today, Nov. 19 (consent items 66 and 67), under which the city would pay Z Modular up to $6.35 million to build larger water and wastewater lines than its Winding Trails East and West mixed-use project needs: $1.85 million for water and $4.5 million for wastewater, paid from Austin Water’s capital budget. The roughly 260-acre site is at 7308 N. FM 973 near SH 130. City-funded oversizing like this is what opens up neighboring land along the SH 130 corridor, so I’m watching it closely. Also on today’s agenda: the Davis Lane mini-warehouse rezoning and the Howard Lane and Menchaca Rd. cases (The American Developer).

Kendra Scott at Burleson Tech, Project Juniper’s start, a 308-acre Lockhart site and Kyle Park

Austin Business Journal · Community Impact · REJournals · Bisnow · Nov 16–17, 2026

Kendra Scott is leasing a 124,000 SF warehouse at Holt Lunsford’s Burleson Tech, moving from East Stassney Lane (ABJ).
