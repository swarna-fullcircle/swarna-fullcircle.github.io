City Hall · North Lamar & Austin Energy

Council approves North Lamar DB90 rezoning and up to $695M in Austin Energy bonds

Y’all City Hall · City of Austin Action Notes · Nov 5, 2026

Following up from yesterday’s agenda: per Y’all City Hall’s write-up of Thursday’s Action Notes, Austin City Council approved on all three readings the North Lamar at Beaver Street neighborhood-plan land-use change and matching rezoning (Items 31–32) for property at 8605–8613 N. Lamar Blvd. and 604–706 Beaver Street — the DB90 case staff had framed around roughly 545 apartments and a small retail plaza. On consent, Council also authorized electric utility revenue refunding and/or improvement bonds totaling up to $695 million (to be issued by April 22, 2027, repaid from Austin Energy revenues, not property taxes) and directed the city manager to develop a targeted commercial-space rent buy-down program for eligible local and legacy small businesses, subject to available funding. The 3601 Davis Lane mini-warehouse rezoning was postponed to Nov. 19 at the applicant’s request. My read: another corridor multifamily entitlement cleared, utility capital capacity got a green light, and small-business retail policy got a nudge — while the Davis Lane industrial-flex ask waits two more weeks.

Hospitality & condo · Downtown Austin

Congress Avenue “Project Bolo” rezoning seeks 750–1,200 feet for hotel and condos

Our Austin · Nov 5, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Nov 4, 2026

More detail is out on the hotel-condo tower planned for Congress Avenue at W. 4th. Our Austin reports the Project Bolo rezoning at 402 and 408 Congress Ave. proposes 87 condos and 221 hotel rooms, with a requested height range of 750 to 1,200 feet — tall enough to test Capitol View Corridor limits downtown. ABJ first flagged the parking lot and two-story building as redevelopment targets; the request is still pending city review. I’ll keep watching how height, corridor, and hotel-condo product land with Planning.

Industrial · Semiconductor corridor

Reports: AMD books most of Samsung Taylor Fab 2’s planned 2nm HBM base-die output

TweakTown · Nov 5, 2026 · Maeil Business Newspaper via TweakTown

TweakTown, citing Maeil Business Newspaper, reports Samsung’s planned Taylor Fab 2 has secured customers for its entire planned 2nm HBM base-die output before construction begins, with AMD accounting for most of the volume (NVIDIA also mentioned). The fab is described at about 2.7 million SF — roughly 1.5× Fab 1 — with site prep underway, formal construction reportedly set to begin this month, equipment move-in expected in 2029, and mass production planned for 2030. Samsung has not confirmed which products the fab will make, so treat the customer mix as reported, not official. For CRE, a booked second fab still points to sustained supplier, housing, and industrial demand along the Taylor corridor.

Still on my radar: Northcross’s 1.26M SF site plan and Lincoln’s 294 units at Four Points

Groundbreak · Nov 2–4, 2026

Groundbreak reports Riverside Resources filed a site plan to redevelop Northcross Mall at 2525 W. Anderson Lane into a nine-building, 1.26 million-SF mixed-use project. Data centers · Hutto follow-up

Hutto’s data center campus replat is approved; code rewrite still Dec. 1

OpenTexas · Nov 3, 2026 · Bisnow · Nov 4, 2026

Recap from this week: Hutto P&Z voted 7–0 to approve the replat of Hutto Data Center Campus Subdivision One (97.866 acres into six commercial lots). The commission pushed its Unified Development Code rewrite (Chapters 1–3) to Dec. 1, holding parking and data-center provisions pending a study and asking staff to review Taylor’s new data-center code. Developer/operator still unnamed. Bisnow’s coverage of Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa’s proposed data-center moratorium and tax-break rollback remains the state-policy backdrop.
