Infrastructure · I-35, AUS & Austin Water

$412.2 million to rebuild I-35 north of downtown, a $344 million utility backbone at the airport, and $55.3 million at Hornsby Bend

Texas Construction Hub · TxDOT · Nov 16–21, 2026

This week brought several very large public contracts. Texas Construction Hub reports that Austin Bridge & Road submitted the low bid of $412.2 million, about 0.6% above the engineer’s estimate, to rebuild I-35 from US 290 East to Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard. That is the Capital Express Central University segment. The work includes managed lanes, cross streets at Manor Road and 32nd Street, frontage roads between Concordia Avenue and 40th Street, and a shared-use path. It was the largest item in TxDOT’s $811.4 million November letting. TxDOT’s Nov. 20 commission recap says commissioners awarded $811 million in low-bid contracts for 39 highway projects. At Austin-Bergstrom, the Sundt/Archer Western joint venture now has up to $344 million as construction manager at risk for the campus-wide utility project after a $224 million amendment Council approved 10–0 on Oct. 22. The project covers power, communications, stormwater and water quality for the 32-gate expansion, with completion expected in 2029. And on Nov. 19, Council approved two Matous Construction contracts totaling $55.33 million for Austin Water’s Hornsby Bend biosolids plant; construction dates are not confirmed yet. For my clients along the I-35 corridor and on the east side near the airport, these are the projects that will shape access, construction disruption and future values for years.

San Marcos · Caldwell County · Manufacturing

Daimler’s Project Hercules: San Marcos is the only Texas site in the running, and a Chapter 380 incentive gets its first reading Dec. 1

Texas Construction Hub · City of San Marcos · Austin Business Journal · Nov 19–21, 2026

Texas Construction Hub has more detail on Daimler Truck North America’s proposed plant of about 2 million SF for medium- and heavy-duty trucks. The site is about 611 acres in Caldwell County, generally between SH 80 and Valley Way Drive and between FM 1984 and SH 142. The City of San Marcos says it is one of several sites under consideration and the only one in Texas, and that the company expects to choose a site by the end of the year. Neither the city nor Daimler has published an investment figure, and Council has taken no formal action. A proposed Chapter 380 incentive agreement is set for first reading Dec. 1 and second reading Dec. 15. The city describes it as a performance-based grant paid only if minimum job and capital-investment commitments are met. This is not a done deal, but I’m already getting questions about land and industrial sites along the SH 80 and SH 130 corridors.

Far East Austin & Bastrop · Industrial growth

Far East Austin heats up around the Dog’s Head and ABIA, and Acutronic plans a jet-engine factory in Bastrop

Austin Business Journal · Texas Construction Hub · Nov 17–19, 2026

ABJ’s Nov. 19 feature describes how development on Austin’s eastern fringe is speeding up around the Tesla gigafactory, the Dog’s Head and the airport. It says one of Austin’s top real estate firms is starting a building effort that could add millions of square feet of commercial space and thousands of homes. Separately, ABJ reports that Acutronic Technologies’ parent has secured a $100 million investment to fund a new jet engine factory in Bastrop, and the company expects to more than double its local headcount, with about 100 new hires. Nearby, city records reviewed by Texas Construction Hub tie Link Logistics to Project Juniper, the $67 million, 1.015M SF warehouse on FM 969, which has a planned construction start of tomorrow, Nov. 23. Taken together, the area east of SH 130 keeps producing industrial and land demand.

Data centers · Local rules

Austin’s first data-center rules go to Council Dec. 3, Bastrop drafts its own, and Kyle still hasn’t taken a formal vote

City of Austin · KXAN · CBS Austin · KVUE · Nov 6–20, 2026

Austin Planning says the Dec. 3 package is phase one of two. It would define “data center” and add business registration rules in Title 4. Land-use rules in the Land Development Code are expected to go to Council by May 2027. In Bastrop, KXAN reported that the City Council unanimously asked TCEQ to hold a public meeting on Pacifico CCK Energy’s proposed 48-turbine gas plant in Cedar Creek. The council also held a first reading of rules that would prohibit data centers in general commercial districts and require a conditional use permit in industrial areas; I haven’t seen the result of the Nov. 17 second reading. CBS Austin reports that Anthropic says it is in talks on the related data-center campus but has not signed a lease. In Kyle, KVUE’s coverage shows the Nov. 17 discussion was only about directing staff to draft a ban, with no ban vote yet. For land owners, the rules on where data centers can go are being written city by city right now.

Housing data & builder land · Market context

October data: Austin MSA home sales fall 11.4% and the median drops to $404,802, while builders keep buying land in Georgetown and NW Austin

Unlock MLS · Groundbreak · Oct 29–Nov 11, 2026

Unlock MLS’s October report shows 2,114 closed sales across the MSA, down 11.4% year over year. The median price fell 6.9% to $404,802, which the report attributes partly to a bigger share of lower-priced sales. Inventory is 6.2 months, and pending resale contracts rose 6% even as total pendings fell 4%. Rentals are tighter: the median rent is $2,100 (up 1%), and active lease listings are down 29%. Builders are still buying land. Groundbreak reports that Highland Homes bought 100 acres in Hillwood’s Melina for roughly 400 lots and 80 acres near SH 130 and SH 29 for the 214-home East View Farms, its first self-developed Austin-area communities. Tri Pointe bought 85.6 acres at SH 45 and West Parmer for 410 homes at Pearson Ranch, with sales expected in 2028. Rooftops in these areas lead to neighborhood retail and medical demand, which I’m watching for my clients.

Governor-elect Hinojosa’s call for a pause on new data-center permits and Sen. Perry’s bill (including a 50% cap on local tax breaks) are still the backdrop heading into the Jan. 12 session (KCBD).
