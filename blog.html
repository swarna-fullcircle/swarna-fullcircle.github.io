What I’m watching in CRE

Each morning I gather the latest commercial real estate and business headlines across the Austin MSA — deals, land, leasing, industrial, office, and the infrastructure stories that move our market.

Last updated Thursday, November 26, 2026 · Refreshed daily at 6:00 AM PT

Happy Thanksgiving! It’s a lighter holiday news week, so today’s edition is a lighter refresh: a few genuinely new items plus updates on the threads I’m tracking.

Industrial · SH 130 & I-35 corridors

Balcones plans about 700,000 SF of shallow-bay industrial at CR 152 and SH 130 in Georgetown, with no data centers

Austin Business Journal · Williamson County Sun · IPE Real Assets · Nov 20–25, 2026

The Austin Business Journal reports that Balcones Real Estate is planning its next shallow-bay industrial park in Georgetown, with about 700,000 SF of space. ABJ says local officials will soon consider key approvals and that, despite online rumors, the project would not include any data centers. The Williamson County Sun adds that on Nov. 17, Georgetown’s Planning and Zoning Commission voted 6-1 to recommend rezoning about 46.39 acres at the northeast corner of County Road 152 and SH 130 from Agriculture to Intensive Industrial. The applicant calls the project “Georgetown 152 Manufacturing.” That was the same night P&Z unanimously recommended denial for the Inner Loop Exchange data center, so the commission drew a clear line between the two uses. On the I-35 side, IPE Real Assets reports that GCM Grosvenor has committed an initial $62.5 million, and up to $300 million over time, to a Blue Yonder Property Group platform focused on high-power manufacturing buildings. Its first purchase is 2000 Clovis Barker Road in San Marcos, a 75,276 SF crane-served facility on about 30 acres. Small-bay space for local users and buildings that already have power are two of the most supply-constrained products I see in our market, so both stories make sense to me.

Office leasing · South Austin & defense tech

Defense contractor Noda AI leases the entire 201–207 E. St. Elmo Road office building

Austin Business Journal · LoopNet · Nov 25, 2026

ABJ reports that Noda AI, an Austin defense contractor, signed a lease for all of the 201–207 E. St. Elmo Road office building in South Austin’s St. Elmo district. ABJ calls the deal further evidence of the city’s ongoing defense-industry boom. The property’s former LoopNet listing describes the project as 46,565 SF of new creative office, built in 2026, with built-out private offices and private patios. The listing has been taken down and directed inquiries to CBRE. Lease terms were not in the public summary. When a new-construction creative office building fills with a single tenant, that tells me well-located, move-in-ready space under 50,000 SF is still leasing, even with the broader office market still working through vacancy. Zimmer Biomet at MetCenter and the 7700 Parmer leases I covered earlier this week point the same way.

ABJ: Noda AI in St. Elmo · LoopNet: 201–207 E. St. Elmo · ABJ: Zimmer Biomet (Nov. 24)

Multifamily · Pipeline & trades

Austin’s apartment pipeline fell sharply in Q3, and a 280-unit Pflugerville community just traded

Austin Business Journal (CoStar data) · Matthews · Newmark · Commercial Property Executive · Nov 19–25, 2026

ABJ, citing CoStar’s multifamily team, reports that Austin saw a pronounced fall in its under-construction share in the third quarter. The paper says that’s not necessarily cause for concern and that some developers have been waiting for numbers like these. Matthews’ Q3 report puts the pipeline at 13,543 units, about 3.9% of inventory, after 5,727 completions in Q3, and notes that trailing-12-month absorption held at about 21,200 units. On the deal side, Newmark arranged the sale of Villas at Grand Avenue, a 2009-built, 280-unit community at 1720 Grand Avenue Parkway in Pflugerville. The seller was Jackson Square Properties, the buyer was Dallas-based SPI Advisory, and the property was nearly 95% occupied. Newmark also arranged Freddie Mac acquisition financing. Newmark’s research says Austin construction starts are down about 73% from their late-2023 peak. Taken together with last week’s Yardi rent data, the oversupply cycle looks like it is ending, and I expect value-add buyers to keep moving on older, well-occupied suburban assets.

Suburban mixed use · Senior housing

Suburban mixed-use projects keep moving: senior living at Leander’s Northline and in Dripping Springs, a new Bell District street, and Taylor’s North Village breaks ground

Community Impact · Austin Business Journal · Nov 23–25, 2026

Close to my Leander office, Community Impact reports that Verdot Capital plans Hayden Grove at Northline, a 234-unit senior community for residents 75 and older. It will have 138 independent living units, 72 assisted living units and 24 memory care units, with groundbreaking targeted for early 2027 and opening in mid-2029. The developer cited Northline’s upcoming stores, town square, library and the neighboring St. David’s ER as reasons it chose the site. In Cedar Park, council approved agreements on Nov. 19 for South Parkwest Drive in and next to the 54-acre Bell District. The city will contribute $2.2 million, and Verdot and RedLeaf will cover their own portions. In Dripping Springs, CI reports that Serenova broke ground in September on a 175-unit, 14-acre all-inclusive 55+ community at 201 Headwaters Blvd., with move-ins targeted for fall 2028. And ABJ reports that EVK Homes has broken ground on North Village in Taylor, a 40-acre site with 175 housing units and 10 acres of commercial space. Senior housing is becoming a dependable anchor use in suburban mixed-use town centers, and that helps the retail and medical office around it.

Retail · Permits & pad sites

This week’s top filings include a Fifth Third Bank in Liberty Hill and a Mavis Tire in Georgetown, while two Red Lobster pads go dark in Austin

Community Impact (TDLR filings) · Nov 24–25, 2026

Community Impact’s weekly roundup of the most expensive TDLR filings in the metro includes a 2,400 SF Fifth Third Bank with a two-lane drive-thru at 2863 Kauffman Loop in Liberty Hill, estimated at $3.3 million. It also lists a Mavis Tire and Brakes at 28717 Ronald Reagan Blvd. in Georgetown at $2.5 million, an Ascension Urgent Care infill at 801 W. Wells Branch Pkwy. in Pflugerville at $1.1 million, and Cedar Park’s $4 million Fire Station 4 renovation. Separately, CI reports that Red Lobster closed both Austin restaurants on Nov. 2, at 3815 S. Lamar Blvd. and 109 W. Anderson Ln., leaving Round Rock and San Marcos as the nearest locations. The pattern I keep seeing is that bank branches and auto service are still chasing new rooftops along Ronald Reagan and in Liberty Hill. Meanwhile, second-generation restaurant boxes on established in-town corridors like South Lamar tend to draw strong backfill or redevelopment interest. I’d watch who ends up with those two sites.

Land use · Central Austin

Ahead of the Dec. 3 Muny vote, UT researchers push for protection of Brackenridge Field Lab, and the Environmental Commission asks for a delay

KXAN · Austin Politics · Nov 19–24, 2026

KXAN reports that the proposed agreement would give the city permanent ownership of Muny. It would also raise development allowances on UT’s portions of the Brackenridge Tract from 1.7 million SF to 4.9 million SF across about 190 acres. Researchers support saving Muny but want all 82 acres of the Brackenridge Field Laboratory permanently protected. KXAN says the Austin Environmental Commission unanimously recommended delaying the vote to June 2027. City officials say the deal adds heritage-tree and water-quality protections, and UT says it has no specific development plans for the property. Austin Politics’ guide to the draft Dec. 3 agenda lists Muny as Item 31, which includes street vacations, a regulating plan and a tax increment zone. The data-center license is Item 32. Nearby, KXAN reported Nov. 19 that UT Regents discussed in executive session possible leases of about 5.2 and 4.1 acres for a multipurpose arena and 1.1 acres for student housing on the former Erwin Center site, including a possible operating agreement with Live Nation. No decisions have been announced, and I’m treating that one as unconfirmed for now.

Data centers & manufacturing · Local rules

Kyle’s data-center ban direction is back on the Dec. 1 agenda; Austin’s two-phase rules, Georgetown’s Dec. 8 vote and Daimler’s decision are still pending

City of Kyle agenda (via GridWatch) · Austin Politics · KLBJ · Civic IQ · City of San Marcos · Nov 1–24, 2026

Here is an update on one open thread. Kyle’s posted Dec. 1 council agenda brings back Item 25, which was tabled on Nov. 17. Council Member Claudia Zapata’s item would direct the city manager to bring back an ordinance that defines data centers and prohibits them in all zoning districts. It is only a direction to staff. A ban would still need its own ordinance vote. In Austin, Austin Politics’ read of the city’s Oct. 28 memo describes phase one on Dec. 3 as Title 4 business rules for large data centers. Zoning rules would follow in spring 2027, with Planning Commission review planned for April. Austin Energy has 13 inquiries of 20 MW or more, and none of them is above 75 MW. Austin Water’s large-water-user framework goes to a council committee on Dec. 9. In Georgetown, Council takes up Inner Loop Exchange on Dec. 8. Because P&Z recommended denial, approval would take six of seven votes, KLBJ reports. Bastrop’s second reading of Ordinance 2026-50 still has no posted minutes or vote result that I can find. Daimler hasn’t announced its Project Hercules site yet. San Marcos says the Caldwell County site is the only Texas option still in the running, and the Chapter 380 readings are Dec. 1 and Dec. 15.
