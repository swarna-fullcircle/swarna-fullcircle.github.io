Data centers / power · Taylor / Williamson County

The morning after Taylor’s vote, Big Watt and PowerHouse put numbers on the Taylor Technology Campus: up to 1 gigawatt, behind-the-meter power, first power targeted for the first half of 2028

Big Watt Digital / PowerHouse Data Centers via Business Wire · Oct 9, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Oct 9 · Austin American-Statesman · Oct 9 · KUT · Oct 9

Yesterday I led with Taylor City Council’s unanimous approval; today the developers and the business press filled in the picture. In an Oct. 9 Business Wire announcement, co-development partners Big Watt Digital and PowerHouse Data Centers (a division of American Real Estate Partners) said they secured approval of the development agreement for the roughly 664-acre Taylor Technology Campus, which they describe as having the potential to deliver up to one gigawatt of hyperscale data center capacity using dedicated, behind-the-meter power generation, with first power targeted for the first half of 2028. Their release projects about $1.3 billion in cumulative gross tax revenue to the city from 2027 through 2046, about $4.8 billion in total economic output and roughly 19,000 construction-period FTE jobs across Williamson County during a projected 2027–2029 build, and about 500 FTE jobs at stabilized buildout, including 320 in Taylor. The Austin Business Journal’s Justin Sayers framed it as a key annexation win that came despite objections from many residents, with city officials citing the standards codified in the deal and the tax benefits, and the Statesman noted the more than $28 million in community investments the developers pledged. Those projections are the developers’ own, but for the US 79 corridor the message is clear: this campus is now on a real timeline.

Infrastructure + advanced manufacturing · Hutto–Taylor corridor

East WilCo Highway Segment 2 opens between Hutto and Taylor months ahead of schedule, the same week Blue Origin picked Hutto for its ~1.3 million SF Constellation Park

Community Impact · Oct 9, 2026 · plus Community Impact · Oct 8 · KUT · Oct 8 (Blue Origin)

Roads follow jobs, and this week eastern Williamson County got both. Community Impact’s Jillian Nachtigal reports that the county held an Oct. 8 ribbon-cutting for East WilCo Highway Segment 2, which runs from CR 137 in Hutto to CR 404 in Taylor with one travel lane each way plus a center turn lane. The $53.1 million segment, funded by the 2023 voter-approved road bond, was finished months ahead of schedule, and East WilCo Highway now connects SH 130 in Pflugerville to Chandler Road in Taylor; at full buildout it will reach I-35 in Jarrell. The county also completed the $7.1 million Southeast Loop connection, adding 1.65 miles of new roads and bridges. That lands right after Blue Origin’s Constellation Park announcement, which I covered yesterday: Community Impact reported the company will occupy roughly 1.3 million square feet in the existing industrial park along Hwy. 79 between Innovation Boulevard and Alliance Boulevard, with a nearly $555 million capital investment and more than 2,000 jobs that KUT says are expected over the next 10 years. New east–west capacity is exactly what industrial users and their suppliers look for along this corridor.

Industrial leasing / manufacturing · Georgetown

Solar parts maker Protec PV leases 82,000 SF at Berry Creek Business Park in Georgetown and plans to hire 200 by April

Austin Business Journal · Oct 9, 2026

Another occupier for Berry Creek Business Park, the same Georgetown park where TSS recently took about 380,000 square feet. The Austin Business Journal’s Justin Sayers reports that solar parts manufacturer Protec PV Inc. has leased 82,000 square feet at Berry Creek and plans to hire 200 employees by April. ABJ’s angle is that demand for the company is being driven not just by data centers but by pretty much anything that requires power. Protec’s own site says the company provides encapsulants to the solar industry. For Georgetown’s industrial owners, a mid-size manufacturing lease with a fast hiring ramp is a healthy signal that the I-35 north submarket is drawing more than distribution users.

Institutional / medical · North Austin

UT wants to build another tower in North Austin as part of its AI-native UT Dell Medical Center

Austin Business Journal · Oct 9, 2026 · background from Community Impact · Aug 13, 2026

The Austin Business Journal’s Sean Hemmersmeier reports that the University of Texas plans to build an AI-native medical center in North Austin featuring a new tower designed to symbolize research and innovation, with UT President Jim Davis saying the school is focused on bringing innovative ideas to the build-out. For scale, Community Impact reported in August that the UT System Board of Regents added Phase I of the UT Dell Medical Center to its capital improvement program: a 27-acre complex with a hospital tower, outpatient facilities, an emergency department, and 300–500 beds, in collaboration with UT MD Anderson, inside the 300-acre UT Dell Campus for Advanced Research off MoPac. Phase I is expected to cost up to $1 billion of a roughly $5 billion total, with completion targeted for December 2030. A project of that size is a long-term demand driver for medical office, hospitality, and housing in the MoPac/Parmer area.

Entitlements / policy · City of Austin

Austin explores a Transfer of Development Rights program to aid historic preservation and boost dense development

Austin Business Journal · Oct 9, 2026

A policy item worth flagging for landowners and developers. The Austin Business Journal reports that city staff is evaluating a Transfer of Development Rights program that would let owners of historic properties sell some of their entitled development rights to owners of other sites in the city, preserving landmarks while encouraging denser growth in designated receiving areas. ABJ notes a recent study found current market conditions aren’t optimal for the program’s use, but that improved conditions could make it a key tool. If it moves forward, entitlements themselves could become a tradable asset for some historic owners, which is worth watching on downtown and central-city sites.

Retail / mixed use · Leander

Northline adds The Market District, a 30,000 SF retail and dining hub in Leander leaning toward Asian businesses, targeted for Q1 2028

CultureMap Austin · Oct 9, 2026

Close to home for me in Leander. CultureMap’s Brianna Caleri reports that Northline, the mixed-use development around Leander Station, is adding The Market District, 30,000 square feet of retail and dining space west of The Shops at Northline, with a tenant mix expected to “lean toward Asian businesses.” The developer’s release points to Samsung’s $42 billion semiconductor plant in Taylor as the anchor for a broader wave of Korean investment across Williamson County, and Northline Leander Development Company founder Alex Tynberg said the goal is to bring retailers, restaurants, and services not offered anywhere else in the area. No leases have been announced; Partners Real Estate is leading tenant recruitment for this first phase, and The Shops at Northline, anchored by Sprouts Farmers Market, is set to open later in 2026. Northline’s plan also includes Leander’s new City Hall, a town square, a boutique hotel, and a library, cultural center, and museum. Opening is expected in the first quarter of 2028.

Residential pipeline · South Congress and Northwest Austin

Leland South Congress officially breaks ground with 265 condos and 225 hotel rooms, and Tri Pointe plans 410 homes at Pearson Ranch alongside JPI’s 342 apartments

Groundbreak · Oct 9, 2026 · also Community Impact · Oct 9

Two pipeline updates. Groundbreak reports that construction began Oct. 1 on Intracorp Homes’ Leland South Congress at 2105 S. Congress Ave., the former Ego’s Bar site: a seven-story, 635,000-square-foot, 90-foot building on 2.8 acres with 265 condos, 225 hotel rooms, 22,000 square feet of ground-floor retail and restaurant space, and a 500-plus-space garage, backed by a $173 million BGO construction loan, with 45% of units presold and completion expected by the end of 2029; Shop Companies handles retail leasing. Separately, Community Impact’s Hamera Shabbir reports that Tri Pointe Homes plans 410 homes on 85.6 acres in the 156-acre Pearson Ranch development at the northeast corner of SH 45 and Parmer Lane, with construction expected to start in early 2027 and sales in 2028, while developer JPI is planning 342 apartments on 9.3 acres in the same development. Rooftops and units like these are what future neighborhood retail along Parmer and SH 45 will be underwritten on.

Market data · Office and industrial, Q3 2026

Still my reference point this week: CBRE’s Q3 numbers show office vacancy at 23.6% after Waterline delivered, while industrial leasing tops 9.77 million SF year to date

CBRE Research · Oct 8, 2026

I’m keeping CBRE’s Austin Q3 2026 figures on the page one more day because they frame almost everything above. On office, net absorption was 253,000 square feet (577,000 SF year to date), but vacancy rose 40 basis points to 23.6% and availability rose to 28.5%, driven by Waterline Tower delivering downtown with more than 700,000 SF of vacant Class A space; CBRE says that excluding that building conditions were steady, the pipeline is at its lowest level in more than 10 years, and tenant demand reached 5.1 million SF, up 45% year over year. On industrial, net absorption reached 1.22 million SF against 918,000 SF of deliveries, year-to-date leasing hit 9.77 million SF, already above full-year 2024 and 2025 totals, and average asking rents fell 2.1% year over year. Leases like Protec PV’s in Georgetown are part of that industrial leasing story.
