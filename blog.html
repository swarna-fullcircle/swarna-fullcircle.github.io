Office investment sales · Downtown Austin

Lavaca Plaza trades to Riverside — JLL arranges sale of 122,042 SF CBD tower with redevelopment upside

JLL newsroom · Sep 30, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Sep 30

Downtown Austin just logged another office trade — this one with an explicit redevelopment lens. JLL Capital Markets announced Sep. 30 that it arranged the sale of Lavaca Plaza, an 11-story, 122,042-square-foot office building at 504 Lavaca Street (Fifth and Lavaca), to Austin-based Riverside. Originally built in 1981, the asset puts parking on floors one through seven — among the highest parking ratios downtown — with four stories of office suites above. JLL highlights a Walk Score® of 99 and surrounding density of more than two million square feet of retail, about 6,700 hotel keys, and roughly 4,300 residential units. Managing Directors Ryan Stevens and Drew Fuller represented the seller; price was not disclosed. Stevens called it a cash-flowing core property with “exceptional redevelopment opportunity.” Austin Business Journal’s Sep. 30 framing adds that the decades-old tower is “prime for redevelopment” and could accommodate a major skyline addition. I’m pairing this with 515 Congress below: two CBD office trades in a week tell me capital is back in the core — some buying leased towers to refresh, others buying older bases to reinvent.

Office investment sales · Downtown Austin

515 Congress still on the board — Aquila / Glendon / Palmatum buy ~268K SF as CBD pricing tests a floor

CRE Daily · Sep 29, 2026 · also CRE Market Beat · Yahoo Finance / Austin Business Journal · Sep 24

Yesterday’s national CRE brief remains the leased-tower counterpart to Lavaca. CRE Daily frames the joint venture of Aquila Commercial, Glendon Capital Management, and Palmatum Ventures acquiring 515 Congress — a 26-story, about 268,000-square-foot tower at Congress and Sixth — from KBS for an undisclosed price. County appraisal is cited at $117 million in 2026 (down from $150 million in 2022); an Aquila spokesperson put the tower at about 80% leased, with roughly 66,000 square feet available and tenants including Austin Business Journal, Boeing, Cloudera, and WebAI. CRE Daily pairs the deal with Hines’ $151 million buy of 405 Colorado in July. Two different downtown bets in the same week: well-leased Class A at a reset basis versus an older, high-parking asset marketed for reinvention.

Industrial investment sales · Southeast Austin / Del Valle

Dalfen lists Airport Logistics Center — ~800K SF, 100% leased to Four Hands, JLL on the sale

Connect CRE · Sep 30, 2026 · citing Austin Business Journal

Big-box industrial just moved from lease-up to exit. Connect CRE reports (citing Austin Business Journal) that Dalfen Industrial has put Airport Logistics Center at 6106 Ross Road up for sale after furniture maker Four Hands expanded to just over 800,000 square feet across two buildings, taking the 2024-delivered project to 100% occupancy. A JLL team of Witt Westbrook, Trent Agnew, Rob Ellwood, and Kyle Mueller is marketing the fee-simple logistics center; Four Hands’ weighted-average lease term is cited at 5.1 years. For me, this is today’s industrial capital-markets story: when a credit occupier fills a new park near the airport, institutional sellers test the bid — a useful read on where Austin logistics pricing sits after the supply wave.

Retail investment sales · Central Austin

Casis Village still shows grocery-anchored liquidity — Randalls center, 92% leased on 3.4 acres

REBusinessOnline · Sep 29, 2026

Neighborhood retail remains a bright spot when the tenancy is right. REBusinessOnline reports JLL negotiated the sale of Casis Village in Austin’s Tarrytown neighborhood. Square footage was not disclosed; the site spans 3.4 acres. Grocer Randalls anchors the center, which was 92 percent leased at sale, with other tenants including Estilo Boutique, Starbucks, Tarrytown Pharmacy, Zed’s, and Face Foundrie. Chris Gerard and Shea Petrick of JLL represented the seller — a joint venture between Ivy Cos. and 35 South Capital; the buyer was not named. I’m keeping this next to the CBD office trades: grocery-anchored urban retail is still clearing while office capital sorts leased towers from redevelopment bases.

Industrial delivery · Kyle / Hays County

Kyle Technology Park Phase I — REBusinessOnline confirms Cadence McShane / TIG’s 75,000 SF delivery

REBusinessOnline · Sep 30, 2026 · also San Antonio Business Journal · Sep 29 · REJournals · Sep 28

Southern MSA small-bay industrial keeps picking up ink. REBusinessOnline’s Sep. 30 note confirms Cadence McShane completed a 75,000-square-foot Phase I at Kyle Technology Park for TIG Real Estate Services — three 25,000-square-foot buildings with 35-foot clear heights on a 41-acre site at FM 1626 and Plum Creek Meadows, designed by Studio 8 Architects for multi-tenant use. Full buildout is planned at about 11 buildings and roughly 300,000 square feet. SABJ’s Sep. 29 corridor piece and REJournals’ Sep. 28 completion note add I-35 / ABIA access context and infrastructure work (earthwork, utilities, drainage, new crossover). I’m pairing Hays County flex delivery with Dalfen’s airport big-box list — two industrial clocks: lease-and-exit at the airport versus new small-bay supply south of town.

Multifamily entitlement · Southwest Austin

Community Impact deepens Stratus Circle C Tract 110 — 1,000 units cleared; traffic and sponsor questions remain

Community Impact · Sep 30, 2026 · also The Real Deal · Sep 28 · FOX 7 Austin · Sep 24

Southwest Austin’s biggest near-term multifamily entitlement got a fresh Sep. 30 local deep dive. Community Impact reports City Council cleared a 1,000-unit housing plan (including 100 affordable units) on a more than 67-acre portion of Tract 110 at 11010 ½ S. MoPac — Stratus Properties / Circle C Land Corp. land previously limited under a 2002 development agreement to about 651,000 square feet of office plus limited retail and housing. Council’s Sep. 24 votes also struck the South Bay Lane extension requirement; a TIA is still required at site plan, and the development team “highly anticipates” funding a Dahlgreen signal if studies warrant it. Impervious cover on the redevelopment portion is limited to about 16 acres; 10,000 square feet of commercial remains, with none of the originally envisioned offices. Residents continue to flag school-zone traffic near Kiker and Valor, and some note Stratus’s planned dissolution leaves delivery uncertain. Corridor density won 10-0-1; aquifer compliance, access, and who builds will still price the schedule.

Data centers / land entitlement · Caldwell County

PowerHouse’s nearly 900-acre Uhland site leaves city ETJ — Caldwell County now holds the data-center path

Austin Business Journal · Sep 28, 2026

The southern corridor’s biggest entitlement geography story is still live. Austin Business Journal reports PowerHouse Data Centers — fully owned and operated by Virginia-based American Real Estate Partners — is moving a nearly 900-acre Caldwell County project near Uhland forward without city oversight after landowners petitioned to release the property from the City of Uhland’s extraterritorial jurisdiction under a state law that allows them to do so. ABJ’s framing: the huge site has been “released into Caldwell County purview,” paving a path for a data center under county rather than municipal ETJ control. Earlier Bisnow and Data Center Dynamics coverage (May 2026) had tracked PowerHouse’s push for a roughly 500-acre campus inside the broader Caldwell Valley Technology Park corridor. For me, this still sits next to Abbott’s statewide TCEQ permit pause: leaving city ETJ is not the same as clearing state air and water authorizations — CRE owners along that corridor should underwrite both clocks.

Data centers / regulation · Statewide (MSA impact)

Abbott’s TCEQ data-center permit pause still frames every campus clock — including Caldwell

Texas Tribune · Sep 21, 2026 · also TechTarget · Sep 24 · Community Impact · Austin Business Journal · Sep 22

Digital-infrastructure entitlement is still on a statewide brake — and the Uhland ETJ release makes the sequencing risk sharper, not softer. The Texas Tribune reports Gov. Greg Abbott on Sept. 21 directed TCEQ Executive Director Kelly Keel to halt new permits sought by data centers until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board finish audits covering electricity use, water use, community impact, ownership, and tax breaks. TechTarget’s Sep. 24 follow-up quotes TCEQ’s Ryan Vise confirming the agency has paused air and water permits and other authorizations for infrastructure directly supporting data centers, will fold ERCOT findings into its evaluations, and still owes Abbott a compliance update by Oct. 19; developer-facing dates now include Oct. 12 for ERCOT’s State and Community Impact RFI responses and Dec. 10 for ERCOT’s planned report to the PUC. Community Impact and Austin Business Journal had already flagged “behind-the-meter” builds still needing TCEQ air/water authorizations. For CRE around Taylor, Bastrop, Georgetown, Four Points, and Caldwell/Uhland, schedule risk remains live as those mid-October checkpoints approach.
