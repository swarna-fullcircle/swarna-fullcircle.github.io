Data centers / industrial land · Hutto / Williamson County

Hutto’s Skybox–Prologis megasite is being rewritten by power — ABJ flags an industrial pivot while Taylor and DCD track the next data-center phases

Austin Business Journal · Oct 2, 2026 · also Taylor Press · Oct 2 · Data Center Dynamics · Oct 2

The northeastern MSA’s biggest power-and-land story took a sharper turn overnight. Austin Business Journal’s Justin Sayers reports Oct. 2 that a Hutto data-center plan tied to Skybox Datacenters and Prologis is pivoting toward industrial uses, with the piece framing the shift around ERCOT grid access growing “more challenging” and a possible 2028 green light for the next phase. Local coverage fills in the entitlement side: Taylor Press reports that at Hutto City Council’s Oct. 1 meeting, Skybox Chief Development Officer Haynes Strader said ERCOT conditions have become more challenging and that if the team can secure power — which may take another two years — construction would start on the next phase of the campus, described as the fifth and sixth data centers in the Megasite. Council approved rezoning a 140-acre parcel with pre-approval for a data center and power station even though no tenant is in place yet, with stipulations for closed-loop cooling, Hutto land-development codes, and local sales taxes on construction and power generation. The same day Data Center Dynamics reported that Skybox and Prologis filed with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation for Skybox Hutto 4 at 3399 County Road 132 — an 846,300-square-foot single-story core-and-shell building, with construction slotted for January 2027 through October 2028 and a $550 million investment figure, on a PowerCampus planned for 600 MW of IT capacity across 3.88 million square feet. I’m reading these three notes together: power timelines are now pricing land use as much as rack demand, and Hutto is where that trade-off is showing up first.

Occupier demand · Austin MSA

Doodle Labs’ Austin HQ hunt gets a CRE follow — WeWork for now, hybrid office-plus-testing space next, with SoCo in the mix

The Real Deal Texas · Oct 2, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Oct 1

Yesterday’s defense-tech HQ move now has a leasing brief. The Real Deal Texas reports Oct. 2 (citing Austin Business Journal) that Doodle Labs — which designs long-range connectivity gear for unmanned systems — has moved its U.S. headquarters from the Los Angeles area to the Austin metro and is working out of a WeWork while it searches for a hybrid office and testing location. Co-CEO Amol Parikh told ABJ the firm picked Austin after shopping several U.S. markets, citing the U.S. Army’s local research presence, defense and aerospace clusters in places like Buda and Cedar Park, real-world testing options, and a central geography for meeting other companies. Brokers are in the mix; the company will take multiple sites if one unicorn space does not open up, and the South Congress corridor is of particular interest. ABJ’s Oct. 1 piece still supplies the scale signal: local headcount could more than quintuple by the end of 2027. TRD also notes peer expansions — Allen Control Systems’ 191,000-square-foot north Austin factory and Aeon Industrial tripling space in Cedar Park near Firefly Aerospace. For me, this is active small-to-mid bay and flex demand with a testing overlay, not just another HQ press release.

Industrial / cleanroom · North Austin

Prudential Overall Supply breaks ground on a 100,000 SF northside cleanroom plant — $50M+ and 150+ jobs, ops targeted for 2028

Textile Rental Services Association · Oct 2, 2026 (groundbreaking Oct 1)

Owner-user industrial just put a shovel in the ground on Austin’s north side. The Textile Rental Services Association reports that public and private officials attended an Oct. 1 groundbreaking for a new 100,000-square-foot cleanroom and industrial plant for Irvine, California–based Prudential Overall Supply — the chain’s 38th plant and a backup to its Round Rock sister facility. President Chris Welch put the capital commitment at more than $50 million with over 150 full-time jobs; the plant is expected to begin operating in 2028 and to process 82 million pounds in two years, supporting controlled-environment customers in semiconductors, pharmaceuticals, bioscience, aerospace, and electronics. Austin City Council member Mike Siegel and Greater Austin Chamber vice president of membership Chad Wicks spoke at the event. I’m filing this next to the cleanroom and packaging leasing we have seen on the northeast industrial belt: specialized process space is still getting built even while broader warehouse vacancy digests.

Office investment sales · Downtown Austin

Lavaca Plaza still anchors the CBD read — Riverside’s 122,042 SF buy at 46.2% vacant, with SB 840 conversion in the conversation

Commercial Property Executive · Oct 1, 2026 · also JLL newsroom · Sep 30 · Austin Business Journal · Sep 30

I’m keeping the downtown office trade on the board while Hutto and occupier demand dominate today’s wire. Commercial Property Executive reports Oct. 1 that private-equity investor Riverside acquired Lavaca Plaza — an 11-story, 122,042-square-foot office building — from Unico Properties for an unspecified price. Yardi Matrix puts the property at 46.2% vacant and notes Unico bought it for $61.4 million in 2015. The 1981 tower’s parking ratio (3.9 per 1,000 square feet across floors one through seven) and SB 840’s Sep. 1 effective date — allowing more market-rate housing on most commercially zoned property — are why conversion keeps coming up even though CPE says the buyer has not revealed conversion plans. JLL’s Ryan Stevens and Drew Fuller represented Unico; metro office vacancy was cited at 24.5% in July. Capital is still pricing CBD bases for more than one exit path.

Industrial infrastructure · Lockhart / Caldwell County

Lockhart is chasing industrial users with a $1.7M federal grant aimed at utility projects

Austin Business Journal · Oct 1, 2026

South of the metro, small-city industrial site readiness is getting a federal assist. Austin Business Journal reports Oct. 1 that Lockhart aims to build more industrial capacity with a $1.7 million federal grant, with the money going to utility projects meant to lure new investment as housing costs push residents and companies farther down the corridor. The city’s EDC director told ABJ Lockhart wants manufacturers and other employers that bring lasting jobs and capital investment and that fit the community. I’m pairing this with the Caldwell/Hays data-center and ETJ stories from earlier this week: the southern MSA is competing as much on wet utilities and power as on land price.

Retail investment sales · Central Austin

Casis Village’s 1031 buyer stays on the board — 46,564 SF, 92% leased Tarrytown grocery-anchored retail

Lincoln Property Company · Oct 1, 2026 · also REBusinessOnline · Sep 29

Infill grocery-anchored retail is still clearing into exchange capital. Lincoln Property Company announced Oct. 1 that it advised a high-net-worth family office on a 1031 acquisition of Casis Village at 2727 Exposition Boulevard — 46,564 square feet, 92% leased, Randalls-anchored in Tarrytown. REBusinessOnline’s Sep. 29 note had named JLL’s Chris Gerard and Shea Petrick for the Ivy Cos. / 35 South Capital seller JV and put the site at 3.4 acres. I’m keeping this next to Lavaca: two different bids for Austin’s built stock in the same week — conversion-optional CBD office versus leased neighborhood retail.

Entertainment / flex office · Georgetown

The Junction still targets a November opening in Georgetown — 60K SF entertainment plus 30K SF flex office

Community Impact · Oct 1, 2026

Northern-MSA experiential product is on a delivery clock. Community Impact reports Oct. 1 that The Junction at 210 Blue Springs Boulevard — Cooley Capital Cos.’ entertainment concept — is approaching a November opening, partner Matt Marshall told the paper. The 60,000-square-foot venue is pitched for food, drinks, live music, watch parties, pickleball, and private events, with an additional 30,000 square feet across three buildings for flexible office space. Named dining operators include Sweet Lemon Kitchen, Dough-Go Pizza, King’s Chicken Wings, Taconmaye, JABS Smashburger, and Shawarma Point. I’m still watching the flex-office stack as much as the pickleball courts — Georgetown is trying to lease daytime occupancy on top of a night-and-weekend draw.
