Industrial delivery · Kyle / Hays County

Cadence McShane delivers Kyle Technology Park Phase I — 75,000 SF of flex warehouse south of Austin

REJournals · Sep 28, 2026

The southern MSA industrial corridor just put vertical product on the board. REJournals reports Cadence McShane Construction completed Kyle Technology Park Phase I for client TIG Real Estate Services — three warehouse buildings totaling 75,000 square feet (25,000 SF each) with 35-foot clear height, designed for multi-tenant warehouse and flex industrial users. The 41-acre site at FM 1626 and Plum Creek Meadows sits for I-35, Austin-Bergstrom, West Austin, and San Antonio access; Cadence McShane also finished earthwork, utilities, drainage, and a new crossover road, with Studio 8 Architects as architect of record. Full buildout of the master-planned park is framed at up to 11 buildings and roughly 300,000 square feet for office, industrial, showroom, and build-to-suit users. For me, this is Monday’s freshest CRE industrial read: Hays County is not waiting on CBD office narratives — small-bay flex is still delivering where population and last-mile demand already are.

Multifamily entitlement · Central Austin

Council clears Rosedale School rezoning — 435-unit path still blocked by a deed-restriction lawsuit

Community Impact · Sep 25, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Sep 24

Central Austin just got a zoning win that still isn’t a construction calendar. Community Impact reports Austin City Council gave final approval Sept. 24 — after three postponements since May — to rezone the former AISD Rosedale School site at 2117 W. 49th St. from single-family to multifamily, clearing one of two hurdles for OHT Partners’ proposed 435-unit, six-story apartment plan on about 4.62 acres with a 75-foot height limit. Because neighbors filed a valid petition, the case needed a supermajority; Council also adopted a District 7 amendment directing staff to consider street impact fees for traffic-safety work, including at 49th and Burnet. AISD agreed in 2025 to sell the closed campus to OHT for $26 million as the district works a budget shortfall, but Community Impact stresses the sale still depends on AISD winning its Travis County lawsuit against Rosedale homeowners over a 1938 deed restriction — no trial date set yet. Neighbors’ Play Fair With Rosedale group has floated a smaller roughly 150–225-unit alternative, and Council Member Mike Siegel said mediation talks that looked close later fell apart. For me, this is still the CRE land-to-delivery read: zoning can move while title risk and deed covenants still hold the shovel.

Multifamily entitlement · Southwest Austin

Stratus wins Circle C Tract 110 rezoning — ~1,000 apartments next to Kiker, traffic fight unresolved

FOX 7 Austin · Sep 24, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Sep 25

Southwest Austin’s biggest near-term multifamily entitlement just crossed Council. FOX 7 reports City Council approved items tied to Stratus’s Circle C Tract 110 near Kiker Elementary — rezoning land originally framed for office into multifamily for about 1,000 apartment units (including affordable), plus removing a prior requirement that the developer build a South Bay Lane construction extension. Residents and the Protect Circle C Coalition pressed hard on school-zone traffic and a proposed Dahlgreen Avenue access point they say sits roughly 500 feet from the elementary campus; parents want a traffic study now, not later in site permitting. Stratus CEO Beau Armstrong’s statement, carried by FOX 7, counters that of roughly 190 acres the firm is developing only about 16 acres at just over 8% impervious cover, with about 174 acres preserved as permanent open space; he argues residential peak traffic would be lighter than a full office build-out under existing zoning, that the city required a secondary access point, and that a full TIA comes at site development. Austin Business Journal’s Sep. 25 write-up frames the same MoPac-adjacent Circle C rezoning as unlocking a huge multifamily project. For me, this is still the MSA housing-pipeline CRE overlay: corridor density is winning votes, but school-access and open-space politics will still price the schedule.

Office investment sales · Downtown Austin

KBS exits 515 Congress — Palmatum / AQUILA / Glendon buy ~268K SF; ~80% leased CBD tower

CRE Market Beat · Sep 24, 2026 · also Yahoo Finance / Austin Business Journal · Sep 24 · Commercial Search Week of Sep 28

Downtown office liquidity is still finding buyers for the right capital plan — and the deal is already landing in this week’s Southwest transaction roundups. CRE Market Beat reports KBS Real Estate Investment Trust III sold 515 Congress — a 26-story Class A tower totaling 267,956 square feet at Congress Avenue and Sixth Street — to a joint venture of Palmatum Ventures, AQUILA, and Glendon Capital Management, with JLL Capital Markets (Mike McDonald, Jonathan Napper, Ryan Stevens, and Drew Fuller) representing the seller. Sale price was not disclosed; Yahoo Finance / Austin Business Journal report Travis County appraised the asset at $117 million this year (down from $150 million in 2022), and an AQUILA spokesperson put occupancy around 80%, with office tenants including Austin Business Journal, Boeing, Cloudera, and WebAI. During its hold, KBS invested more than $15.5 million in lobby, lounge/conference, and elevator modernization; AQUILA managing principal Ben Tolson said the JV sees room to enhance the experience for existing tenants and companies it hopes to attract. Commercial Search’s Week of September 28 Southwest deal sheet also flags the close. For me, this is still the investment-sales counterpart to the mid-month CoStar vacancy/pipeline snapshot: institutional capital will underwrite refreshed CBD product even while tenants still shop hard on concessions.

Industrial pipeline · Taylor / Williamson County

Partners pushes TaylorPort C into permitting — ~572,640 SF rail-served spec near Samsung

Partners Real Estate · Sep 23, 2026

Northeast corridor industrial is still scaling with the semiconductor story. Partners Real Estate announced TaylorPort C — a roughly 572,640-square-foot speculative, cross-dock, rail-served industrial building on FM 3349 in Taylor — has entered the permitting process after lease-up of the park’s first two phases. The building sits on about 35.34 acres with 40-foot clear height, 3,000 amps of expandable power, ESFR sprinklers, and about 5.1 acres of industrial outdoor storage for trailer staging, containers, or fleet parking. Partners Development is developing; John Colglazier, SIOR, John Simons, and Kyle Kennan are exclusive leasing agents. Colglazier’s statement, carried in the release, frames rail-served boxes of this size as rare in Central Texas and ties demand to manufacturers and suppliers putting down roots near Samsung’s Taylor investment. Paired with today’s Kyle Technology Park delivery farther south, I’m reading a two-speed industrial MSA: small-bay flex completing in Hays County while large-bay, rail-served spec still queues in Williamson.

Office market snapshot · Austin MSA

CoStar’s mid-September office read — vacancy 15.7%, rents inching up, pipeline near cycle lows

Austin Office Space · Sep 15, 2026 · CoStar data as of Sep 16

Before another CBD tower trade lands in a regional deal sheet, I’m resetting to the mid-month CoStar picture Austin Office Space published from data as of Sept. 16. Vacancy sits at 15.7% — down 0.7 points from a year ago and holding the spring level — with about 2.5 million square feet of trailing-twelve-month net absorption (roughly 1.8 million so far in 2026) and average asking rents near $44/SF full-service, up about 1.7% year over year. The under-construction pipeline is about 1.1 million square feet, framed as the smallest in years, with Waterline’s 715,000 SF downtown called out as the last big speculative tower of the cycle. Class A asks about $56/SF with 20.5% vacancy; downtown still leads asking rents near $60/SF with roughly a fifth vacant, while Northwest Austin absorbed more space over the past year than any other submarket and Round Rock is flagged as the tightest major submarket at under 5% vacancy. Signature 2026 leases cited include Jackson Walker and Virtu Financial taking a combined 170,000 SF at The Republic downtown, Renesas 95,000 SF at San Clemente in the Southwest, and Qualcomm 66,000 SF at Parmer Business Park — plus July sales of One Eleven Congress for $208 million and 405 Colorado for $151 million (about $733 a foot). For me, this is still the office CRE backdrop for 515 Congress and every other CBD conversation on today’s board: tenants still have leverage above 15% vacancy, but a thinning quality pipeline through 2027 is the quiet risk landlords will underwrite against.

Data centers / regulation · Statewide (MSA impact)

Abbott’s TCEQ data-center permit pause gets sharper edges — air/water freeze, Oct. 12–Dec. 10 calendar

Texas Tribune · Sep 21, 2026 · also TechTarget · Sep 24 · Community Impact · Austin Business Journal · Sep 22

Digital-infrastructure entitlement is still on a statewide brake — and the sequencing risk just got clearer. The Texas Tribune reports Gov. Greg Abbott on Sept. 21 directed TCEQ Executive Director Kelly Keel to halt new permits sought by data centers until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board finish audits covering electricity use, water use, community impact, ownership, and tax breaks. TechTarget’s Sep. 24 follow-up quotes TCEQ’s Ryan Vise confirming the agency has paused air and water permits and other authorizations for infrastructure directly supporting data centers, will fold ERCOT findings into its evaluations, and still owes Abbott a compliance update by Oct. 19; developer-facing dates now include Oct. 12 for ERCOT’s State and Community Impact RFI responses and Dec. 10 for ERCOT’s planned report to the PUC — even as TCEQ has not fully clarified how the freeze applies to projects outside ERCOT’s ≥25 MW interconnection review. Community Impact and Austin Business Journal had already flagged “behind-the-meter” builds still needing TCEQ air/water authorizations, and ABJ named a high-profile Bastrop County gas-powered campus Anthropic has been in talks to lease as an early pause read (Axios Austin / The Real Deal earlier tied Anthropic to talks on a planned about 2,842-acre Cedar Creek campus with roughly 710 megawatts of natural-gas generation). For CRE around Taylor, Bastrop, Georgetown, and Four Points, schedule risk remains live — and the next six weeks of audit paperwork may matter as much as any land closing.
