Data centers / annexation · Taylor / Williamson County

Taylor City Council approves the Big Watt–PowerHouse annexation agreement 5-0, clearing the way for the 664-acre Taylor Technology Campus

KUT · Oct 9, 2026 · also CBS Austin · Oct 8 · City of Taylor

The outcome I’ve been waiting on all week is in. KUT’s Kailey Hunt reports that Taylor City Council voted unanimously Thursday night to approve the agreement with Big Watt Digital and PowerHouse Data Centers to annex their planned 664-acre Taylor Technology Campus (formerly Project Mustang), and CBS Austin puts the vote at 5-0. The approval came before an overflow crowd and over the objections of many residents in attendance, including members of HALT Taylor Data Centers, who raised concerns about water, infrastructure, and over-industrialization. Council members and staff said annexation gives the city authority to regulate water use, noise, air quality, lighting, and setbacks on a project that would otherwise sit in unincorporated Williamson County. The developers’ estimated $28.2 million in community investments includes more than $10 million for Taylor ISD and local students, and CBS Austin notes officials estimate about $1.38 billion in gross city tax revenue over 20 years. For the US 79 corridor between FM 112 and Taylor, this settles months of uncertainty: a large data center and on-site power campus is now moving forward under city oversight, and I expect it to shape land pricing and industrial interest east of town.

Advanced manufacturing / industrial · Hutto / US 79

Blue Origin picks Hutto for Constellation Park, a roughly 1.3 million SF, ~$555 million satellite manufacturing campus with 2,000+ jobs

Community Impact · Oct 8, 2026 · also KUT · Oct 8 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 8

This is the biggest occupier headline of the week for our side of the metro. Community Impact’s Claire Shoop reports that Jeff Bezos’ Blue Origin will move into approximately 1.3 million square feet in the existing industrial park along Hwy. 79 between Innovation Boulevard and Alliance Boulevard in Hutto. The campus, called Constellation Park, will be a production hub for satellite and communications technology, and Gov. Greg Abbott’s office describes a nearly $555 million capital investment and more than 2,000 jobs; KUT adds that Blue Origin expects to create those jobs over the next 10 years. The company received a Texas Enterprise Fund grant of more than $18 million, and Williamson County, the city of Hutto, and the Hutto EDC have also extended incentives whose terms won’t be released until they’re executed. The Austin Business Journal frames the facility as bigger than Lakeline Mall. As Hutto Mayor Mike Snyder put it to KUT, between Tesla, ASML, and now Blue Origin, Hutto is becoming an anchor on the Highway 79 tech corridor. A user this size absorbing existing industrial space is exactly what the northeast submarket needed, and I’d expect supplier, housing, and retail demand to follow.

Market data · Office and industrial, Q3 2026

CBRE’s Q3 numbers: office absorbs 253,000 SF as Waterline nudges vacancy to 23.6%, while industrial leasing tops 9.77 million SF year to date

CBRE Research · Oct 8, 2026

CBRE published its Austin Q3 2026 figures Oct. 8, and both reports read as a market working through supply rather than losing demand. On office, net absorption was 253,000 square feet (577,000 SF year to date), but vacancy rose 40 basis points to 23.6% and availability rose 150 bps to 28.5%, driven by the delivery of Waterline Tower downtown with more than 700,000 SF of vacant Class A space. CBRE says that excluding that one building conditions were steady, Class B tightened, the pipeline is at its lowest level in more than 10 years, and tenant demand reached 5.1 million SF, up 45% year over year, even as quarterly leasing slowed to 590,000 SF from 1.06 million in Q2. On industrial, net absorption reached 1.22 million SF against 918,000 SF of deliveries (nearly 95% delivered vacant), year-to-date leasing hit 9.77 million SF, already above full-year 2024 and 2025 totals, eight buildings totaling 1.21 million SF broke ground, and average asking rents fell 2.1% year over year. For tenants, that still means leverage on rate; for owners, the leasing volume is the encouraging signal.

Data centers · Cedar Creek / Bastrop County

Pacifico Energy and BlackChamber file Project Longhorn Building One: a nearly 500,000 SF, $366 million data center in Cedar Creek

Community Impact · Oct 8, 2026

A follow-up to yesterday’s Bastrop County item, now with the filing details. Community Impact’s Amanda Cutshall reports that Pacifico Energy and BlackChamber filed plans Oct. 7 with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation for Project Longhorn—Building One, a one-story data center near Earl Callahan and Walter Hoffman roads, about 7 miles southeast of EdgeConneX’s three Cedar Creek campuses. The nearly 500,000-square-foot building is estimated at $366 million, with construction scheduled from Nov. 30 to Jan. 31, 2028. It is the first of two planned buildings on the 2,842-acre property, which also includes a natural gas-fired power facility developed by Pacifico. Community Impact notes the developers withdrew earlier Bastrop ISD and county tax abatement requests, nearby residents have pushed back, and Gov. Abbott in September directed state agencies to pause regulatory approvals for data centers while ERCOT and others audit proposed projects. The SH 71 corridor keeps adding hyperscale filings, but the state pause is the variable I’m watching on timing.

Office leasing · North Austin / Domain–Arboretum

York Space Systems opens a new North Austin office near The Domain, and Lucid Private Offices takes a full floor at StoneBridge Plaza II

Austin Business Journal · Oct 8, 2026 · also SpaceNews · Oct 8 · Community Impact · Oct 8

Two smaller North Austin office moves from the same day. The Austin Business Journal reports that space and defense contractor York Space Systems has opened a North Austin office near The Domain and expects to grow its local workforce by 10% to 15% over the next year, and SpaceNews says York is moving its Austin software development group into a 780-square-meter office (roughly 8,400 SF) near Quarry Lake. Separately, Community Impact’s Grant Crawford reports that Lucid Private Offices is pre-leasing a coworking space that will occupy the entire ninth floor of StoneBridge Plaza II at 9600 N. MoPac, between The Domain and The Arboretum, with 83 private offices, 12 team suites, and four conference rooms; a TDLR filing shows a $1.8 million renovation, and opening is expected in November. Aerospace and defense growth plus flexible-office operators taking whole floors fit right in with CBRE’s rising demand numbers.

Multifamily · Round Rock and Southwest Austin

Knightvest closes on a 777-unit Round Rock portfolio at La Frontera, and OHT’s 402-unit The Barton starts construction off Hwy. 71

Groundbreak · Oct 8, 2026 · also Community Impact · Oct 8

Multifamily capital is still moving in both directions: buying stabilized assets and breaking ground on new ones. Groundbreak reports Oct. 8 that Knightvest has completed its acquisition of the Silicon Hills Portfolio, the Enclave at La Frontera and Lakeside at La Frontera at 2800 La Frontera Blvd. and 941 Hesters Crossing Road in Round Rock, with the confirmed unit count revised to 777 from the 1,027 initially reported; Groundbreak didn’t report a price. And Community Impact reports that construction on The Barton, the 402-unit OHT Partners project on more than 60 acres near Southwest Parkway and West Hwy. 71, began Oct. 6, with first units expected in early 2028 and full completion at the end of 2028. That’s the project ABJ flagged as breaking ground earlier this week; now we have the unit count and timeline.

Entitlements · City of Austin

Austin City Council approves two DB90 density-bonus rezonings, including 1120 S. Capital of Texas Hwy. over a valid petition, plus $33 million in bonds for Elm Ridge Apartments

City of Austin council action notes · Oct 8, 2026

From Thursday’s Austin City Council meeting, per the city’s tentative action notes: council approved, as amended, rezoning 1120, 1120 1/2, 1122, and 1220 South Capital of Texas Highway from LO and LR to LR-V-CO-DB90 for owner AREIT City View LLC (agent Drenner Group), even though a valid petition had been filed in opposition. Council also approved DB90 zoning for the 1900 Rosewood Block (1155 Chicon St., 1900–1906 Rosewood Ave., and 1152 Poquito St.) in East Austin, represented by Armbrust & Brown. And the Austin Housing Finance Corporation authorized up to $33 million in multifamily housing revenue bonds for Elm Ridge Preservation to acquire and rehabilitate Elm Ridge Apartments near 1190 Airport Blvd. The city notes these action notes are tentative until minutes are approved. DB90 approvals on a West Austin corridor and an East Austin block show the density bonus tool is still getting used on real sites.
