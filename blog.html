Office · Downtown Austin & Northwest Austin (Quarry Lake)

Downtown’s sublease picture in one day: Meta lands a fourth subtenant at Sixth and Guadalupe while Indeed’s 308,000 square feet becomes the second-largest block on the market

Austin Business Journal · Oct 29, 2026 · The Real Deal (via Yahoo Finance) · Oct 29, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 28, 2026 · JLL · Oct 28, 2026

The Austin Business Journal reports that public filings tie InKind to about 32,000 square feet inside Meta’s 589,000-square-foot office lease at the Sixth and Guadalupe tower, which would make it Meta’s fourth subtenant. Meta never occupied the space, and it has been Austin’s largest sublease offering since it hit the market in November 2022. The Real Deal, summarizing ABJ’s coverage, says Meta’s available space is now just over 456,000 square feet, with The Malin, PwC and Weaver and Tidwell already in the building. The same day, Indeed’s full 308,000-square-foot listing at Indeed Tower, 200 W. Sixth St., became the second-largest downtown sublease opportunity. The Real Deal says Indeed told ABJ it would consolidate at The Domain if a subtenant takes the space. TRD also points to other recent downtown sublease deals, including Digital Realty on the top six floors of Google’s Sail Tower and xAI taking 112,300 square feet at Seaholm from Athenahealth. Away from downtown, carried over from yesterday: DML Capital bought the 98%-leased Lakeside at Quarry Oaks (123,272 square feet) from Brick Row Holdings in a JLL-brokered deal with life-company financing. My read: sublease space is moving, but slowly, and with roughly three-quarters of a million square feet sitting between these two blocks, tenants shopping downtown still have real negotiating room.

Land & master plans · Georgetown, Granger & Williamson County

Georgetown clears the 620-acre Heirloom project to start, with a Nov. 5 groundbreaking, and tiny Granger approves a 765-lot neighborhood

Community Impact · Oct 29, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 29, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 28, 2026

Community Impact reports Georgetown City Council unanimously approved the special assessment ordinance and service and assessment plan for the Heirloom public improvement district on Oct. 27, and authorized $20.89 million in city bonds. That money funds a 2-mile collector road, water and wastewater lines and trails in a future city park. Heirloom is a planned 3,600-unit development near Williams Drive and Ronald Reagan Boulevard, zoned for a corporate campus, multifamily, big-box retail and smaller commercial space, plus a future Georgetown ISD school site. Columnar Investments says first-phase work is targeted for completion in May or June, homebuilders are secured, and a groundbreaking is set for Nov. 5. (Its earlier talk of a Trader Joe’s-type grocer was, in its words, aspirational.) Farther east, ABJ reports Granger City Council approved WBW Development’s 765-lot Lakeside at Granger, which would nearly triple the small city’s population. Carried over: Serenity Ranch, a 571-unit neighborhood in Georgetown’s ETJ, is advancing. For retail and land clients, these rooftops along the SH 130 and Ronald Reagan corridors are what eventually justify neighborhood retail and service pads.

Residential & mixed-use · South Central Waterfront

Related launches sales at One Lady Bird Lake, an 18-story, 101-condo tower on the south shore with about 6,000 square feet of ground-floor restaurant space

Related Companies · Oct 29, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 29, 2026

Related Companies, with Austin-based Christopher Investment Company and Wheelock Street Capital, opened sales Thursday at One Lady Bird Lake, 125 S. First St., on the former Hyatt Regency parking lot. The KPF-designed tower has 101 one- to four-bedroom condos, nearly 30,000 square feet of amenities, and roughly 6,000 square feet of ground-floor retail anchored by a destination restaurant and café, with a public lakeside park and a future boardwalk extension. Rogers-O’Brien is the general contractor, Moreland Properties has the exclusive listing, and completion is scheduled for the first quarter of 2028. ABJ reports prices start at $1 million. Financial partners listed in the release include Northwestern Mutual and Avela Real Estate Partners. Why it matters for the commercial side: it’s one of the first vertical pieces of the South Central Waterfront district, and a for-sale luxury launch in this market is a confidence signal for the lake-adjacent retail and hospitality around it.

Data centers / permitting · Hays County, Taylor & statewide

No change overnight: STEERS stays offline until mid-November, Hays County could adopt its rules Nov. 10, and ERCOT is set to present its audit Dec. 10

TCEQ · checked Oct 30, 2026 · Hays County meeting record · Oct 27, 2026 · Magica · Oct 29, 2026 · The Cooldown · Oct 30, 2026 · City of Austin

A quieter update than yesterday’s. TCEQ’s STEERS e-filing portal is still down while the agency reworks it so staff can review data-center-related applications before anything is authorized, and TCEQ still expects that in mid-November. The Multi-Sector General Permit renewal deadline remains extended to Dec. 28, and the episodic waiver still lets applicants who can’t wait email their MSGP, construction general permit and concrete-production filings. In Hays County, Tuesday’s hearing on the rewritten Development Regulations ended with no action, and staff said adoption could come Nov. 10 or Dec. 1, with a Jan. 1, 2027 effective date in the draft. Magica’s Oct. 29 recap says ERCOT will present its data-center audit findings to the PUC on Dec. 10. In Taylor, The Cooldown recaps CBS Austin’s reporting on the 664-acre Taylor Technology Campus tied to Big Watt Digital and PowerHouse, whose agreement the council approved Oct. 8, including 700-foot setbacks on its southern edge and reclaimed water for cooling. And in Austin, a city staff update confirms the first phase of data-center land-use rules is due by year-end, with a public open house and a second phase in spring 2027. My advice is unchanged: anyone with an industrial or construction project needing stormwater coverage this fall should plan for the manual process.

Industrial · Southeast Austin & metro-wide

Carried over: Amazon’s 1.2 million-square-foot Project Toaster has foundation permits, and Q3 was industrial’s best quarter in at least a year

Texas Construction Hub · Oct 28, 2026 · ECR · Oct 27, 2026 · Lee & Associates – Austin · Q3 2026 · Newmark · Q3 2026

Condensed from earlier this week. Texas Construction Hub reports Amazon’s proposed robotics manufacturing building at 475 Patton Ave. in southeast Austin, about 1.2 million square feet, holds city foundation permits, with a $100 million core-and-shell registration filed for Nov. 16, 2026 through July 13, 2027 and roughly 300–500 projected jobs. The site sits within the wider Dog’s Head development, which faces challenges from environmental groups and residents. On the market side, ECR measured about 2.6 million square feet of Q3 net absorption, Lee & Associates had vacancy down to 19.26%, and Newmark listed Tesla’s 538,720-square-foot Mustang Ridge lease as the quarter’s largest. Rents haven’t turned yet, so occupiers with 2027 needs still have leverage.

Mixed-use, retail & zoning · Manor, Oak Hill, Arboretum & Airport Boulevard

Manor Town Center closes its financing, Oak Hill Parkway wraps up, and Planning Commission takes up an Airport Boulevard mixed-use assembly

Business Wire (Hunt Real Estate & Infrastructure) · Oct 27, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 28, 2026 · The American Developer · Oct 28, 2026 · CultureMap Austin · Oct 27, 2026

Condensed from this week. Manor and Hunt Real Estate & Infrastructure reached financial close on the Manor Town Center civic campus (City Hall, the city’s first public library and a gathering lawn) inside an 84-acre master development, targeting a Nov. 14 infrastructure groundbreaking. TxDOT has substantially finished the $677 million Oak Hill Parkway, though Community Impact notes some Oak Hill retailers say foot traffic is still below pre-construction levels. OHT Partners’ 402-unit The Barton is under way nearby. Asana Partners plans a roughly 14,000-square-foot retail and dining building at The Arboretum. New today: The American Developer reports Austin’s Planning Commission took up a six-parcel Airport Boulevard assembly near Koenig Lane on Oct. 27 that seeks a shift from commercial to mixed use, with a planned development agreement whose conditions will define what can be built. No commission outcome had posted, and any vote there is only a recommendation to Council.
