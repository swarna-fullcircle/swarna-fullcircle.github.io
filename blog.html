What I’m watching in CRE

Economic development · Bastrop County

Bastrop County’s backwoods boom is being fueled largely by Elon Musk

Austin Business Journal · Sep 13, 2026

The CRE story east of Austin keeps widening beyond warehouse parks. Austin Business Journal’s Colin Pope reports that Bastrop County is growing notably — largely due to Elon Musk — with wartime startup Bravo Ordnance recently opening a major headquarters facility in the Cedar Creek area, where it 3D-prints warheads. Paired with the data-center acreage stories nearby, I’m watching Bastrop as a mixed industrial and digital-infrastructure corridor where land, power, and manufacturing demand are stacking on the same map.

Data centers · Bastrop County

Data centers swarm Cedar Creek as neighbors push back

Austin Business Journal · Sep 12, 2026

The digital-infrastructure map keeps stretching southeast of Austin. Austin Business Journal reports that rural Cedar Creek in Bastrop County is among the hardest-hit pockets of Texas’ data-center boom, with the paper tabulating more than 5,500 acres of projects in the area — some nearing completion, others just starting, and more still in planning — while neighbors raise concerns. EdgeConneX already has a facility there and plans for additional capacity nearby. For me, this sits next to the Hutto pipeline: AI load is chasing power and land in the ETJ and rural counties even as Austin debates large-load rules inside city limits.

Data centers · Hutto

Colovore files for $280M, ~193K SF AI data center in Hutto

Community Impact · Sep 11, 2026

Digital infrastructure is still choosing the northern industrial parks. Community Impact reports Colovore — a California-based AI data center developer — plans a one-story, 192,694-square-foot facility at 2401 Innovation Boulevard in Hutto, with about $280 million in investment, 40 megawatts of capacity, and liquid cooling. Construction is slated to start Dec. 1, 2026, and finish Dec. 1, 2028, per a Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation filing. The site sits next to land that could host Blue Origin. Paired with Cedar Creek, I’m reading a clear pattern: AI colocation keeps landing outside Austin’s city limits while the core debates large-load rules.

Retail · Central Austin

Ivy and 35 South Capital buy 2825 Hancock for Highland Market

CultureMap Austin · Sep 10, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal Sep 9

Neighborhood retail is getting a fresh Central Austin bet. Ivy Companies and 35 South Capital purchased 2825 Hancock Drive — between Rosedale and Allandale, across from Austin Memorial Park and accessible from MoPac — and plan to renovate it into Highland Market, about 18,900 square feet for roughly 12 tenants spanning retail, local restaurants, and neighborhood services. Local firm Firmah is on design; Ivy already owns Casis Village in Tarrytown. Austin Business Journal separately notes leasing is underway for retail, restaurant, and service-oriented businesses. For me, this is the kind of small-box, walkable repositioning I’m watching as capital looks past trophy corridors for everyday retail demand.

Industrial · Manufacturing

Allen Control Systems leases 192,000 SF for North Austin manufacturing

Commercial Real Estate Direct · Sep 9, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal

Defense tech is absorbing industrial space north of the core. Allen Control Systems signed a lease for 192,000 square feet at 15700 Long Vista Drive in North Austin, with plans to rapidly scale production of its Bullfrog autonomous weapon station. Per Austin Business Journal’s coverage, the company also holds an option to expand by another 150,000 square feet, and the owner had repositioned a long-vacant building for advanced manufacturing. I’m reading this as another user-driven industrial lease — the kind that keeps north-corridor product relevant even when speculative absorption is uneven.

Industrial · Taylor

Partners Real Estate breaks ground on 573,000 SF speculative building in Taylor

Austin Business Journal · Sep 9, 2026

The eastern industrial pipeline keeps extending. Partners Real Estate broke ground on a 573,000-square-foot speculative, rail-served industrial building in Taylor — next to the firm’s already-leased Compal USA Technology building — with a cross-dock configuration, office pods, truck courts, ample parking, and heavy power. For me, this is the Samsung-adjacent corridor thesis in motion: large-bay, power-ready product still pencils when rail and manufacturing demand sit nearby.

Investment · Opportunity Zones

Austin-area sites nominated for Opportunity Zone 2.0, including Dog’s Head and Taylor

Austin Business Journal · Sep 9, 2026 · also Texas Today

Capital-stack incentives are back on the metro map. Texas nominated 605 census tracts statewide for Opportunity Zone 2.0 — submitted to the U.S. Treasury on Sept. 4, with federal certification expected by Nov. 28 and designations effective Jan. 1, 2027. Austin Business Journal and follow-on reporting highlight Austin-area nominees that include the Dog’s Head corridor between downtown and the airport (where Endeavor is advancing a large mixed-use plan), parts of downtown and North Austin, and Taylor-area land that includes the Samsung chip-factory vicinity. For CRE clients, I’m watching how OZ 2.0 could reshape hold periods and equity bids on large land and industrial plays once Treasury certifies the map.

Industrial · Cedar Park

Aeon Industrial nearly triples Cedar Park footprint to 138,000 SF

Austin Business Journal · Sep 8, 2026

Northwest suburban industrial is picking up more defense-tech demand. Austin Business Journal reports Aeon Industrial Inc. is nearly tripling its Cedar Park footprint to 138,000 square feet as it moves its headquarters from Austin, with the founder and CEO saying the expansion was already needed based on customer demand. This sits alongside the Allen Control Systems lease as another signal that advanced manufacturing users are taking sizable blocks in the Round Rock–Cedar Park–Georgetown corridor — the part of the MSA where flex and industrial product still sees mission-driven absorption.
