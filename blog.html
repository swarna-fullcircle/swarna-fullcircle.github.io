Data centers · State policy

Sen. Huffman files SB 293 to repeal Texas’ data-center sales tax exemptions

Texas Senate · Nov 9, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Nov 7, 2026

This is the story I’m watching most closely for our data-center pipeline. On Monday, the first day bills could be filed for the 90th Legislature, Senate Finance Chair Joan Huffman filed Senate Bill 293 to repeal the sales and use tax exemptions for qualifying data centers and large data center projects (Tax Code Sections 151.359 and 151.3595). Her office says a majority of senators signed on as joint authors. As filed, existing exemptions would end on the bill’s effective date and applications still pending with the Comptroller would be denied. Her release says the exemption cost about $14.6 million in its first biennium after 2013, versus a Comptroller estimate of roughly $3.3 billion in forgone revenue for the coming biennium. ABJ flagged over the weekend that this credit could become a 2027 target; now it’s on paper. It’s a filed bill, not law, and the session convenes in January, but anyone underwriting Taylor, Hutto, Bastrop, or Round Rock data-center land should be pricing in this risk.

Data centers · Regulation & infrastructure

Hays County could adopt its new Development Regulations today; STEERS is still down

The Texas Dispatch · Hays County · TCEQ · City of Austin memo

Tuesday is the earliest date Hays County Commissioners Court could adopt its rewritten Development Regulations. The Texas Dispatch’s read of the 225-page draft is worth knowing: a “high-intensity use” is a project averaging at least 100,000 gallons of water a day (or peaking at 250,000), drawing 20 MW or more, or using mechanical cooling for data centers or high-density computing. To qualify for county incentives, those projects would need air-cooled, hybrid, or closed-loop cooling, and incentives may be withheld unless on-site generation or storage covers at least half of peak demand; 50 MW-plus projects would also need dispatchable power and grid-coordination plans. The county’s pause on discretionary approvals for large water users still runs through Dec. 31. I rechecked TCEQ this morning: STEERS remains shut down for data-center reviews, with the fix still expected mid-November and the MSGP renewal deadline extended to Dec. 28. Austin’s Phase 1 data-center rules remain slated for a Nov. 20 agenda posting and Dec. 3 Council vote.

Industrial · Deliveries and leasing

Southeast Crossing delivers near the airport, and Tesla adds a Buda lease

Hoodline · Nov 9, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Nov 9, 2026 · Partners Real Estate

Hoodline reports Bridge Logistics Properties and Clarion Partners completed Southeast Crossing, a six-building park at 7200 Burleson Road of roughly 366,400 SF per CoStar (Bridge’s own post says 364,529 SF), which Stream’s Sam Owen described as 70% pre-leased back in August. That’s a good sign for a Southeast submarket that Partners still shows at 23.9% vacancy. ABJ also reports Tesla signed a new industrial lease at Buda Commerce Center, with job postings hinting at an electric semi service center; I haven’t seen a square footage published. Context from Monday’s post: Partners has Q3 metro industrial vacancy at 15.5% with 2.21 million SF absorbed.

Georgetown · Land and master plans

Heirloom breaks ground and a former quarry could become 1,376-acre 4T Ranch

Austin Business Journal · Nov 9, 2026 · Connect CRE · City of Georgetown records

Georgetown had a big Monday in the headlines. ABJ reports dirt started turning Nov. 5 at Heirloom, planned for 3,000 residential units, 100 acres of commercial development, and a school campus over roughly a decade of buildout. ABJ also reports the Robinson family is pursuing 4T Ranch, a 1,376-acre mixed-use plan on a former quarry; the city’s record shows an in-city MUD proposal under review that would finance infrastructure through a district rather than the general fund. And Connect CRE notes Council gave conditional approval to Serenity Ranch, whose plan includes 300 multifamily units and 30,000 SF of commercial space. For retail and service users, that’s a lot of future rooftops along the I-35 corridor north of Austin.

Taylor & Round Rock · Industrial pipeline

Taylor’s 2.2 million SF Gradiant Technology Park and Dell’s Round Rock lab work

Austin American-Statesman via Yahoo · Nov 5, 2026 · Taylor Press · Oct 7, 2026 · The American Developer

Samsung supplier iMarketAmerica is investing more than $50 million in Gradiant Technology Park, a 212-acre, 2.2 million SF manufacturing, R&D, and commercial campus in Taylor, with a first phase of at least 325,000 SF that must start construction by Dec. 31. The Statesman describes 60% abatements per phase running up to 10 years; Taylor Press reports Council approved the reinvestment zone and repayment structure on Oct. 1, tied to a $34.5 million water main partly funded by a $15 million zero-interest loan from the company. Carrying over: Dell registered a $12.0M server-rack and MEP project at 701 E. Parmer Lane in Round Rock, per The American Developer.
