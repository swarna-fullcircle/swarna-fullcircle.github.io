Office investment sales · Downtown Austin

Lavaca Plaza gets a national follow — Unico sold the 122,042 SF CBD tower at 46.2% vacant, with conversion now in the mix

Commercial Property Executive · Oct 1, 2026 · also JLL newsroom · Sep 30 · Austin Business Journal · Sep 30

Yesterday’s downtown office trade picked up a fuller capital-markets write-up. Commercial Property Executive reports Oct. 1 that private-equity investor Riverside acquired Lavaca Plaza — an 11-story, 122,042-square-foot office building — from Unico Properties for an unspecified price. Yardi Matrix puts the property at 46.2% vacant and notes Unico bought it for $61.4 million in 2015. The 1981 tower has 476 parking spaces on floors one through seven (a 3.9-per-1,000-square-foot ratio, among downtown’s highest) and four stories of office above; CPE also flags LEED Gold, an on-site café, conference rooms, showers, and a bike locker. JLL Managing Directors Ryan Stevens and Drew Fuller represented Unico; Stevens called it an “exceptional redevelopment opportunity,” though CPE notes the buyer has not revealed conversion plans. The same piece puts metro Austin office vacancy at 24.5% in July (third-highest nationally, behind San Francisco and Seattle) and year-to-date office investment at $1.4 billion through July, seventh nationally — then points to Senate Bill 840, effective Sep. 1, which allows more market-rate housing on most commercially zoned property. JLL’s Sep. 30 release and Austin Business Journal still supply the location (504 Lavaca at Fifth) and Walk Score® 99. I’m reading this as the conversion overlay on last week’s CBD bid: capital is back in the core, and older high-parking bases are being priced as more than office.

Occupier demand · Austin MSA

Doodle Labs moves its U.S. HQ to Austin from California — looking for space, and local headcount could more than quintuple by 2027

Austin Business Journal · Oct 1, 2026

Office demand from actual occupiers is the other half of yesterday’s CBD pricing story. Austin Business Journal reports Oct. 1 that defense-tech manufacturer Doodle Labs — a drone-radio company — has moved its U.S. headquarters from California to Austin, with plans to add employees and real estate quickly. The company’s local headcount could more than quintuple by the end of 2027, ABJ says, and it is looking for real estate in the Austin market and could occupy several locations as it scales. Square footage and a signed address were not disclosed. For me, this is the user-side counterpart to Lavaca and 515 Congress: if HQ and defense-tech users are shopping multiple sites, the flight-to-quality bid for amenitized space still has a pulse even while vintage vacancy stays elevated.

Retail investment sales · Central Austin

Casis Village’s buyer is now on the record — Lincoln advised a family-office 1031 into 46,564 SF of Tarrytown grocery-anchored retail

Lincoln Property Company · Oct 1, 2026 · also REBusinessOnline · Sep 29

The grocery-anchored trade that showed up last week now has a buyer-side confirmation and a size. Lincoln Property Company announced Oct. 1 that it advised a high-net-worth family office on a 1031 exchange acquisition of Casis Village at 2727 Exposition Boulevard — a 46,564-square-foot, 92% leased center in Tarrytown anchored by Randalls, with Starbucks, Tarrytown Pharmacy, Estilo Boutique, and Benchmark Bank among the tenant mix. Lincoln grouped the Austin retail buy with two Colorado assets (office in Cherry Creek and industrial in Aurora) totaling nearly 290,000 square feet across the three deals. REBusinessOnline’s Sep. 29 note had already named JLL’s Chris Gerard and Shea Petrick for the Ivy Cos. / 35 South Capital seller joint venture and put the site at 3.4 acres; square footage and the buyer were not disclosed then. I’m keeping this next to the CBD office trades: infill grocery-anchored retail is still clearing into 1031 capital while office buyers sort leased towers from redevelopment bases.

Industrial investment sales · Southeast Austin / Del Valle

Dalfen lists Airport Logistics Center — ~800K SF, 100% leased to Four Hands, JLL on the sale

Connect CRE · Sep 30, 2026 · citing Austin Business Journal

Big-box industrial is still sitting on the bid desk. Connect CRE reports (citing Austin Business Journal) that Dalfen Industrial has put Airport Logistics Center at 6106 Ross Road up for sale after furniture maker Four Hands expanded to just over 800,000 square feet across two buildings, taking the 2024-delivered project to 100% occupancy. A JLL team of Witt Westbrook, Trent Agnew, Rob Ellwood, and Kyle Mueller is marketing the fee-simple logistics center; Four Hands’ weighted-average lease term is cited at 5.1 years. For me, this remains the industrial capital-markets read: when a credit occupier fills a new park near the airport, institutional sellers test where Austin logistics pricing sits after the supply wave.

Entertainment / flex office · Georgetown

The Junction targets a November opening in Georgetown — 60,000 SF of food, music, and pickleball plus 30,000 SF of flex office

Community Impact · Oct 1, 2026

Northern-MSA experiential retail is getting a delivery date. Community Impact reports Oct. 1 that The Junction — an entertainment venture by Georgetown-based Cooley Capital Cos. at 210 Blue Springs Boulevard — is approaching a November opening, Cooley partner Matt Marshall told the paper. The 60,000-square-foot facility is pitched as a destination for food, drinks, live music, watch parties, pickleball, private events, and gatherings, with three pickleball courts, private cabanas, multiple full-service bars, a live-music stage, a grand dining hall with food vendors, and covered decks. Named dining operators include Sweet Lemon Kitchen, Dough-Go Pizza, King’s Chicken Wings, Taconmaye, JABS Smashburger, and Shawarma Point. The project also includes an additional 30,000 square feet across three buildings for flexible office space. Construction updates in a company release: pickleball-court beams in, Mockingbird Bar tile finished, interior walls and ceilings nearing completion, exterior masonry 95% complete, cabanas going in at The Overlook. I’m watching the flex-office stack as much as the pickleball — Georgetown is trying to lease daytime occupancy on top of a night-and-weekend draw.

Data centers / land entitlement · I-35 south corridor

ABJ maps a data-center battleground between Austin and San Antonio — Caldwell and Hays, with Uhland still in the frame

Austin Business Journal · Oct 1, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Sep 28 · Texas Tribune · Sep 21

The southern corridor’s data-center fight got a fresh Oct. 1 frame. Austin Business Journal’s Justin Sayers writes that Caldwell and Hays counties are among those feeling the wave of investment in Central Texas, with “fertile fields south of Austin near Uhland… yielding large data centers.” The Data Center Action Coalition, ABJ reports, says communities are saying no even as projects keep moving forward; developers say they are trying to earn trust through transparency, and the clash is not expected to wind down anytime soon. That sits on top of ABJ’s Sep. 28 note that PowerHouse Data Centers (fully owned by Virginia-based American Real Estate Partners) is moving a nearly 900-acre Caldwell County project near Uhland forward after landowners petitioned to leave the City of Uhland’s ETJ. And the statewide clock has not moved: the Texas Tribune reported Sep. 21 that Gov. Greg Abbott directed TCEQ to halt new data-center permits until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board finish audits; TechTarget later put developer-facing dates at Oct. 12 (ERCOT RFI responses), Oct. 19 (TCEQ compliance update), and Dec. 10 (ERCOT report to the PUC). Leaving city ETJ is still not the same as clearing state air and water authorizations — those mid-October checkpoints are now ten days out.

Downtown occupancy · Pflugerville

Pflugerville is recruiting small businesses downtown — 2,000–5,700 SF city-owned spaces as City Hall relocates

Community Impact · Oct 1, 2026

Northern-MSA Main Street inventory is coming to market as a city occupancy problem, not a private listing. Community Impact reports Oct. 1 that Pflugerville is looking for small businesses to open, expand, or relocate downtown, following a Sep. 30 city news release. Upcoming city-owned spaces range from 2,000 to 5,700 square feet, with potential for colocation, coworking, and shared retail; the call covers retailers and restaurants plus office-based, food-and-beverage, entertainment, and creative-services users. Several city departments are preparing to leave downtown facilities for the new City Hall, slated to open to the public Jan. 4. An interest form is on the city’s website with no deadline, though early names go on a contact list for when spaces become available. A 2025 community survey had flagged downtown quality as a key improvement area. For brokers working Pflugerville and Hutto, this is a municipal landlord bringing small-bay space to the street as civic users vacate.

Office investment sales · Downtown Austin

515 Congress still on the board — Aquila / Glendon / Palmatum buy ~268K SF as CBD pricing tests a floor

CRE Daily · Sep 29, 2026 · also CRE Market Beat · Yahoo Finance / Austin Business Journal · Sep 24

The leased-tower counterpart to Lavaca is still the other downtown bid. CRE Daily frames the joint venture of Aquila Commercial, Glendon Capital Management, and Palmatum Ventures acquiring 515 Congress — a 26-story, about 268,000-square-foot tower at Congress and Sixth — from KBS for an undisclosed price. County appraisal is cited at $117 million in 2026 (down from $150 million in 2022); an Aquila spokesperson put the tower at about 80% leased, with roughly 66,000 square feet available and tenants including Austin Business Journal, Boeing, Cloudera, and WebAI. CRE Daily pairs the deal with Hines’ $151 million buy of 405 Colorado in July. Two different downtown bets in the same week: well-leased Class A at a reset basis versus an older, high-parking asset now being read for conversion under SB 840.
