City policy / infrastructure · Austin-Bergstrom, Southeast & North Austin

Council approves the $224 million airport utility contract increase on consent — plus an airport-area industrial zoning case, $21 million in housing loans and a Parmer Lane resolution

City of Austin action notes · Oct 22, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 22, 2026 · City of Austin backup (Item 27)

Following up on the item I flagged yesterday: the city’s posted action notes show Item 27 — the amendment adding $224 million to the Sundt/Archer Western contract for the airport’s Utility Infrastructure Campus-Wide project, for a new ceiling of up to $344 million — was approved on consent. Staff backup says the money comes from Austin Aviation’s capital budget, covers larger storm-drainage trunk lines, an expanded water-quality pond system and more medium-voltage power redundancy, and that any delay would ripple into the Central Utility Plant, Arrivals & Departures Hall and Concourse B schedules. The Airport Advisory Commission had recommended it 11-0. A few other votes matter for our market: the airport-overlay zoning case at 1019 Dalton Lane (HPI Acquisition, requesting limited industrial) was approved, while the Phoenix Motor Works limited-industrial request at 1125 and 1127 West 6th Street was postponed indefinitely at the applicant’s request. The AHFC board approved all five loans — Community Impact counts 261 planned units across Rowen Vale, The Lenora, The Maven, Jordan II and Austin Art Village. And Council approved a resolution pushing back on CAMPO’s recommendation to convert Parmer Lane east of MoPac into a four-lane highway; Community Impact notes CAMPO’s recommendations aren’t final and there’s no public timeline. The city cautions that action notes are tentative until minutes are approved, and vote tallies aren’t posted yet. For clients near the airport, the takeaway is simple: the utility backbone for the terminal expansion is funded and moving.

Data centers / state permitting · Statewide, including Bastrop County

17 data-center stormwater approvals moved to “suspended” as TCEQ’s STEERS portal stays down

404 Media · Oct 22, 2026 · San Antonio Report · Oct 22, 2026 · TCEQ · KXAN · Oct 21, 2026

The permitting story is still evolving. 404 Media reports that before TCEQ took STEERS offline, it viewed 17 data-center-related permits that had been moved from “active” to “suspended” in the agency’s central registry — all construction stormwater approvals, which FracTracker researcher Sam Law describes as “a key, and sometimes final” step for any Texas construction project. Law also worries contractors used to automatic approvals may not realize the suspensions exist. The San Antonio Report quotes TCEQ’s Ryan Vise that the permits “have been suspended and communication was sent to the applicants,” and that applications must now be physically provided to the agency. TCEQ’s own STEERS page says the system is “temporarily down” and lists program-by-program email contacts in the meantime — including swpermit@tceq.texas.gov for construction and multi-sector stormwater. Closer to home, KXAN covered a Lost Pines Watch town hall on the Project Longhorn data center and Pacifico gas plant near Cedar Creek; Bastrop County has already asked TCEQ for a public meeting, and no date has been set. My practical note is the same as yesterday, and more urgent: if a non-data-center project needs a stormwater NOI or other automatic authorization, plan on manual filing and extra time.

Multifamily investment sale · North Austin / Uptown ATX

Brandywine sells its 341-unit Solaris tower near The Domain for $136 million

Austin Business Journal · Oct 22, 2026 · Commercial Real Estate Direct · Oct 22, 2026

A nine-figure apartment trade in North Austin. ABJ reports Brandywine Realty Trust sold Solaris, its 341-unit tower at Uptown ATX, for $136 million — CRE Direct works that out to about $398,827 a unit — as part of Brandywine’s push to sell up to $300 million of real estate this year. The buyer wasn’t disclosed in what I could read; the full ABJ story is paywalled. A pricing point near $400,000 a door for a new Domain-area tower is a useful comp for anyone underwriting North Austin multifamily right now.

Office leasing · The Domain & Downtown

Flex’s cloud-and-power spinoff, Axiom Solutions, lists Domain Tower 2 as its headquarters; Jackson Walker’s 110,000 SF at The Republic gets fresh coverage

Austin Business Journal · Oct 22, 2026 · SEC Form 10 filing · REBusinessOnline · Oct 22, 2026 · Jackson Walker

Two more data points for the “quality wins” office story. ABJ reports Axiom Solutions — the Flex Ltd. spinoff focused on high-density digital infrastructure for utilities and AI data centers — is leasing space at Domain Tower 2. Its Form 10 registration with the SEC lists 10025 Alterra Parkway, Suite 1900, as its principal executive offices and names Nasdaq as the intended exchange. ABJ adds that Domain Tower 2 is now 93% leased; I haven’t seen the square footage. Downtown, REBusinessOnline reported Oct. 22 that Jackson Walker signed roughly 110,000 square feet across three mid-level floors at The Republic, the 48-story tower at 401 W. 4th St., with CBRE representing the firm. To be clear, this lease was first announced Aug. 19 by DivcoWest and Lincoln, and Jackson Walker says it plans to move in spring 2028 — it’s an older deal getting new coverage, not a new one.

Industrial / advanced manufacturing · Leander, Pflugerville, Southeast Travis County

A stealth manufacturer takes 106,000 SF in Leander, Pflugerville launches a defense-manufacturing hub, and Terafab’s Austin research fab sets a 2027 test-chip goal

Austin Business Journal · Oct 22, 2026 · Reuters · Oct 20, 2026

This was a busy day for advanced-manufacturing headlines in the suburbs. Here in Leander, ABJ reports Resurgent Industries — a stealth Austin startup building automated production facilities — leased 106,000 square feet and plans to start production next year. ABJ’s story is illustrated with Freehill Development’s Hero Way West project, but I couldn’t confirm the exact building beyond the paywall. In Pflugerville, ABJ reports the city is partnering with Q-Branch on an accelerator for defense-manufacturing startups; I could only see headline-level detail, so I’m not repeating specifics. And ABJ reports Tesla’s research-and-development chip fab near Giga Texas continues to progress, though the building’s size has been pegged anywhere from 400,000 to 1.2 million square feet. Reuters reported that on an Oct. 20 SpaceX call, Musk said Terafab aims to have a first SRAM test chip working at the Austin research facility around May or June 2027, with volume production by Q3 2028. He called those dates “very aggressive” and put “big error bars” on them. Taken together, it’s steady demand for flex and light-industrial space up the SH 130 and US 183A corridors.

Land / mixed-use · Georgetown, Williamson County

Today: Southwestern University and Georgetown break ground on the 560-acre mixed-use plan east of campus

Southwestern University 560 · Williamson County Sun · Oct 6, 2026 · Georgetown Chamber

This one is in my own backyard. This morning at 9, Southwestern University and the City of Georgetown hold the groundbreaking for Southwestern University 560, the long-planned development on the 560 acres east of campus. The site is entered from Smith Creek Road, the event is free and open to the public, and organizers ask for closed-toe shoes because it’s an active construction site. The Williamson County Sun reports the first building is the city’s new Customer Service Center, approved in the 2023 bond, with grading already underway this month. Phase one sits southwest of the Old Smith Creek Road and Smith Creek Road intersection along University Avenue. A civic anchor going in first is usually a good signal for the commercial pads that follow.
