Data centers / permitting · Hays County, Williamson County (Taylor) & statewide

Hays County’s data-center incentive hearing is today — and Taylor’s 664-acre campus shows what city-negotiated guardrails look like while TCEQ’s portal stays dark

Hays County · checked Oct 27, 2026 · The Texas Dispatch · Oct 21, 2026 · CBS Austin · Oct 26, 2026 · TCEQ · checked Oct 27, 2026 · Texas Politics · Oct 26, 2026 · KTTZ · Oct 23, 2026

Today is the day I flagged all last week. At 11 a.m. this morning, Hays County Commissioners Court holds its public hearing in San Marcos on the final draft of its Development Regulations, and the county’s own page now says “Adoption of the Final Draft may follow.” As The Texas Dispatch summarized the 225-page draft, projects using at least 100,000 gallons of water a day on average, drawing 20 megawatts or more at peak, or using mechanical cooling for data centers, crypto mining or high-density computing would be “high-intensity uses” that must meet water and power conditions — closed-loop or air-cooled systems, and on-site generation or storage — to qualify for county incentives. These are incentive conditions, not bans. The hearing hadn’t happened when I wrote this, so I’ll report the outcome tomorrow. New this morning: CBS Austin’s Oct. 26 report on the Big Watt Digital/PowerHouse Data Centers “Taylor Technology Campus,” a 664-acre site just outside Taylor city limits. Taylor’s council approved the development agreement Oct. 8; annexation is still a future step. The developers describe dedicated on-site gas generation, closed-loop cooling with reclaimed water, 300-foot setbacks (700 feet on the residential south side for data centers, power generation and battery storage), and a 65-decibel property-line noise limit. TCEQ told CBS Austin it has received Big Watt’s air-permit application for the generators but hasn’t begun technical review, and Williamson County said it can’t zone or prohibit data centers. Statewide, TCEQ’s “STEERS is temporarily down” page is still up this morning with no return date, so filings for every applicant are being handled manually. Texas Politics reports Democratic gubernatorial nominee Gina Hinojosa criticized the shutdown, while TCEQ maintains that the 17 suspended items were automated stormwater notices, not operating permits. FracTracker’s researcher told KTTZ it’s unclear whether site work has actually stopped on the suspended sites. My takeaway for clients: in unincorporated land, the rules a project lives under increasingly come from a negotiated city agreement or a county incentive policy, not from zoning — and the state permitting queue is slow for everyone right now.

Multifamily investment · Uptown ATX / The Domain & North Burnet

The Solaris House buyer is revealed: Weidner Apartment Homes, financed through Nationwide — and the Domain supply story keeps building

JLL · Oct 26, 2026 · CRE Direct · Oct 22, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 20, 2026

Last week the buyer of Brandywine’s 341-unit Solaris House was undisclosed. JLL’s Oct. 26 release fills that in: Weidner Apartment Homes bought the 13-story Class A tower in Uptown ATX, JLL represented seller Brandywine Realty Trust, and JLL’s debt team arranged acquisition financing through Nationwide. It’s Weidner’s fifth Austin-area community, bringing its local portfolio to 1,560 units. The property delivered in 2024 with units averaging 945 square feet, next to a new CapMetro Red Line station. JLL’s release doesn’t state a price; the $136 million figure comes from earlier reporting (CRE Direct, citing the Austin Business Journal). JLL frames the deal around the Domain’s employer base and a moderating development pipeline, which matches what I wrote about last week. Community Impact reported HPI is building the 396-unit The Kent at 2631 Kramer Lane (first units late 2028), against Austin Investor Interests data showing first-half 2026 apartment starts down 35%. Institutional capital is still buying new, well-located Austin product — a useful comp for owners weighing a 2027 sale.

Retail · Oak Hill (West Austin) & Downtown

Trademark and Cohen & Steers buy 92.5%-leased Oak Hill Plaza; a celebrity-chef Italian concept picks The Republic

REBusinessOnline · Oct 26, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 26, 2026

Two retail items from Monday. REBusinessOnline reports Newmark brokered the sale of Oak Hill Plaza, a 115,512-square-foot shopping center on 11.3 acres at 7101 State Highway 71, which was 92.5% leased. Tenants include The Picklr, Dollar Tree, AutoZone, Jim’s Restaurant, Wells Fargo, Pluckers Wing Bar and Golden Chick. Seller Partners Real Estate had owned it since 2021; the buyer is a joint venture of Fort Worth-based Trademark Property Co. and Cohen & Steers. No price was disclosed. Downtown, the Austin Business Journal reports Scopa Italian Roots, owned by chef Antonia Lofaso, plans an 8,300-square-foot restaurant at The Republic tower (401 W. 4th St.) in 2027. That adds ground-floor activity to a tower where Jackson Walker and Virtu have signed office leases. A well-leased, service-heavy neighborhood center trading to institutional money tells me investors still like Austin’s daily-needs retail.

Housing & community development · East Austin

East Austin groundbreakings: 80 supportive-housing units on FM 969, a 33-acre RBI Austin campus on Loyola Lane, and $21M in city gap financing

National Urban League · Oct 26, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 26, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 22, 2026

A cluster of East Austin projects moved forward. The National Urban League announced the groundbreaking of Urban Empowerment Zone I at 6400 FM 969 in District 1: a four-story, 80-unit permanent supportive housing building developed turnkey by Capital A Housing for the Austin Area Urban League, with about $19.9 million in total development cost, 80 project-based vouchers and 42 years of affordability. Community Impact reports RBI Austin broke ground Oct. 23 on a 33-acre youth sports and education campus at 7401 Loyola Lane, expected to open in late 2027. The nonprofit has raised $7.2 million of an estimated $15.1 million. And on Oct. 22, Council, acting as the Austin Housing Finance Corp. board, authorized $21.09 million in loans for five affordable projects, including Foundation Communities’ 66-unit Jordan II, DMA’s 83-unit The Maven at 618 E. Highland Mall Blvd. and O-SDA’s 64-unit Rowen Vale in Travis Heights (Community Impact). For investors and builders in the eastern crescent, these are the civic anchors that shape the next decade of land values.

Economic development / mobility · Austin-Bergstrom, COTA & Austin Tech Week

Austin signs a five-year tech partnership with Tampere, Finland, as Archer’s east-side air-taxi plan and the AUS build-out keep moving

KXAN · Oct 26, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 26, 2026 · CBS Austin · Oct 23, 2026 · Austin-Bergstrom International Airport · Oct 19, 2026

New: KXAN reports leaders from Tampere, Finland, signed a five-year memorandum of understanding with the City of Austin on Oct. 23, focused on robotics, AI and advanced manufacturing. A Finnish “Physical AI” delegation visited Apptronik and Plug and Play’s Cedar Park offices and is attending Austin Tech Week this week. Plug and Play’s Kevin Farjallah told KXAN Texas’ “tax incentives, the land and the labor costs” help companies scale. Carried over, condensed: the Austin Business Journal’s Oct. 26 piece notes the stops for Archer Aviation’s planned Austin air-taxi routes center on the east side of the metro. CBS Austin reported the proposed network would link AUS, Austin Executive Airport and a COTA helipad, with no launch date set and regulatory approval still needed. Meanwhile, AUS’s utilities infrastructure project, which broke ground Oct. 19 and is expected to finish in 2029, supports the new Arrivals & Departures Hall and Concourse B. Soft-landing demand from foreign tech firms tends to show up first in flex, R&D and small-suite office.

Office · Metro-wide, Southwest Austin & Zilker

Still my office read of the week: Class A rents up about 10%, a thin pipeline, and quality buildings filling up

ECR · Oct 20, 2026 · Hoodline · Oct 22, 2026 · Realty News Report · Oct 24, 2026

Carried over, condensed. ECR reports Class A asking rents rose from $36.01 to $39.61 per square foot over the past year, Class A sublease space is down about 40%, and just 227,707 square feet of office is under construction, versus roughly 2 million a year ago. Domain Class A vacancy is 4.2%, but downtown Class A vacancy is still 23.6%, and about 6.1 million square feet of Class B sits vacant. The deals match that read: Humann’s 12,745-square-foot lease left Uplands II at 5301 Southwest Parkway fully leased (Hoodline, citing Realty News Report), and SolarWinds’ 100,000-square-foot headquarters lease at Zilker Point (JLL for the tenant, Aquila for the landlord) is moving a headquarters into a long-vacant tower. Tenants with 2027–2028 expirations in quality space should start early.
