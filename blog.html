What I’m watching in CRE

Each morning I gather the latest commercial real estate and business headlines across the Austin MSA — deals, land, leasing, industrial, office, and the infrastructure stories that move our market.

It’s the day after Thanksgiving and the news wire is quiet, so this is a light refresh. I’ve added a few new stories from Nov. 24–26 and carried forward the threads I’m still tracking. I rechecked every pending vote and decision this morning, and none of them has a new public result yet.

Advanced manufacturing · Georgetown & SH 130

Georgetown says its manufacturing pipeline is the biggest it has ever been: more than $600 million and 3,000 jobs projected this year, with Balcones lining up about 700,000 SF of new industrial space

Community Impact · Austin Business Journal · Williamson County Sun · Nov 20–26, 2026

In a Nov. 26 report, Community Impact quotes Georgetown Economic Development Director Cameron Goodman as saying more than $600 million in investment and 3,000 new jobs could come to Georgetown from advanced manufacturing projects this year alone. “Just in terms of projects we’re working on now, it’s the biggest our pipeline’s ever been,” he said. The names CI lists are Compal and Pegatron, Taiwanese electronics makers that picked Georgetown for their first U.S. facilities, after the city competed against 15 locations nationwide. Compal expects to finish construction by February 2027, and Pegatron by May 2027. Baer Manufacturing is in temporary space for now and plans to move into a 606,000 SF facility next summer. Aalo Atomics leased a 204,350 SF building and expects to start using it early next year, for metalworking only and with no nuclear materials on site, according to the company. On the supply side, ABJ reported Nov. 25 that Balcones Real Estate is planning about 700,000 SF of shallow-bay industrial in Georgetown and that the project would not include data centers. The Williamson County Sun reports that P&Z voted 6-1 on Nov. 17 to recommend Intensive Industrial zoning for about 46.39 acres at CR 152 and SH 130. Council action on that rezoning hasn’t been posted yet. My read: when big tenants are landing like this, their suppliers come next, and those suppliers will need small-bay space with good power. That’s a thin slice of supply in Williamson County today.

Institutional land · UT Austin

UT regents authorize ground leases for a 6,000-seat Bluebonnet Hill Arena and student-athlete housing, and ABJ looks at what UT’s medical build-out means for Austin

Community Impact · Austin Business Journal · KXAN · Nov 19–26, 2026

Here’s an update on the UT item I had listed as unconfirmed. Community Impact reports that on Nov. 19 the UT System regents voted to authorize ground leases with the 2033 Higher Education Development Foundation for a roughly 6,000-capacity Bluebonnet Hill Arena on 4.1 acres and student-athlete housing on 1.1 acres, along with related financing. Both sites are north of East MLK Boulevard, between the Moody Center and the former Erwin Center site. CI says the arena could include street-facing retail, and the housing is set to open in fall 2029. UT also agreed to move forward on a long-term arena operating agreement with Live Nation. The exact sites, the development partner and the lease terms have not been made public. Separately, a Nov. 26 ABJ special report argues that UT’s new academic medical center and medical school should support two emerging economic sectors in the region. In my experience, academic medical anchors pull in medical office, lab and workforce housing demand for years, so I’ll be watching how much of that lands near the Central Austin campus. Muny and the Brackenridge Tract are a separate matter, and that vote is still set for Austin City Council on Dec. 3 (more below).

Infrastructure · Utilities & I-35 mitigation

Austin agrees to cost-share up to $6.35 million in oversized utility lines for the 260-acre Winding Trails project at FM 973 and SH 130, and a $25 million Lady Bird Lake boardwalk moves ahead

Texas Construction Hub · City of Austin · Austin Politics · Community Impact · Nov 16–25, 2026

City backup documents describe Z Modular’s Winding Trails East and West as about 260 acres at 7308 N. FM 973, in Austin’s ETJ. The plans call for about 1,254 multifamily units, 195,000 SF of office, 205,000 SF of retail, 87,500 SF of restaurant space, a 16,500 SF event center, 115 hotel rooms and 14 acres of sports fields. Items 66 and 67 let Austin Water reimburse the developer for oversizing lines to serve neighboring land, up to $1.85 million for water and $4.5 million for wastewater. Both were on the Nov. 19 consent agenda. Austin Politics reports that no items were pulled and consent was adopted in one vote, but the official minutes are still pending. Downtown, Community Impact reports that Council voted on Nov. 19 to authorize a $25 million TxDOT-funded boardwalk over Lady Bird Lake. It will run between South First and South Congress, next to the Hyatt Regency and One Lady Bird Lake, as mitigation for the I-35 Capital Express project. Some residents objected to where the money is going. For me, the Winding Trails item is the bigger CRE signal. When the city pays to oversize trunk lines along SH 130, it is preparing the surrounding land for future development, and that is good news for east Travis County landowners. Bisnow has reported that utility availability is one of the main reasons I-35 projects get delayed.

Office leasing · Carried forward

Defense contractor Noda AI leases the entire 201–207 E. St. Elmo Road office building

Austin Business Journal · LoopNet · Nov 23–25, 2026

This one is carried over from earlier this week. ABJ reports that Noda AI leased all of the 201–207 E. St. Elmo Road office building in South Austin. ABJ calls the deal further evidence of Austin’s defense-industry boom. The property’s former LoopNet listing described about 46,565 SF of new creative office. Lease terms weren’t in the public summary. Put this together with Zimmer Biomet at MetCenter and the four tenants totaling more than 100,000 SF at 7700 Parmer, and it’s clear that move-in-ready space with a clear tenant story is still leasing.

Multifamily & suburban mixed use · Carried forward

The apartment pipeline keeps shrinking, while senior housing and town-center projects move ahead in Leander, Dripping Springs and Taylor

Austin Business Journal · Matthews · Newmark · Community Impact · City of Taylor · Nov 23–26, 2026

These items are carried over from earlier this week. ABJ, citing CoStar, reports a pronounced Q3 drop in Austin’s under-construction share. Matthews puts the pipeline at 13,543 units (about 3.9% of inventory), and Newmark says starts are down about 73% from their late-2023 peak. Newmark also arranged the sale of the 280-unit Villas at Grand Avenue in Pflugerville. In the suburbs, Community Impact reports Verdot Capital’s 234-unit Hayden Grove at Northline senior community in Leander and Serenova’s 175-unit 55+ community in Dripping Springs. ABJ reports that EVK Homes has broken ground on North Village in Taylor, which includes 10 acres of commercial. One new Taylor item: the city announced on Nov. 24, and ABJ followed up Nov. 26, about $1.1 million in Samsung Spark! gifts for Fannie Robinson Park, Murphy Park’s mini golf and a fire department hazmat trailer. Investment in a city’s quality of life tends to come before the retail and housing that follow it.

Land use · Central Austin · Dec. 3

Muny/Brackenridge goes to Austin Council on Dec. 3, with UT entitlements rising from 1.7 million SF to 4.9 million SF

KXAN · Austin Politics · Nov 19–24, 2026

There’s nothing new on this one since my last update. KXAN reports that the deal would give the city permanent ownership of Muny and would raise development allowances on UT’s Brackenridge Tract land from 1.7 million SF to 4.9 million SF across about 190 acres. Researchers want all 82 acres of the Brackenridge Field Laboratory protected, and the Environmental Commission unanimously recommended delaying the vote to June 2027. Austin Politics lists the Muny package as Item 31 on the draft Dec. 3 agenda and the data-center license as Item 32.

Data centers & manufacturing · Still pending

No new outcomes yet: Kyle’s ban direction (Dec. 1), Austin’s phase-one rules (Dec. 3), Georgetown’s Inner Loop Exchange vote (Dec. 8), Bastrop’s ordinance and Daimler’s site choice are all still pending

City of Kyle agenda (via GridWatch) · KLBJ · Civic IQ · Trucking (TT) · City of San Marcos · Nov 17–24, 2026

I rechecked each of these this morning, and none has a new public result. Kyle: Item 25 on Dec. 1 would direct staff to draft an ordinance banning data centers in all zoning districts. It is direction to staff only, not a ban. Georgetown: Council takes up Inner Loop Exchange on Dec. 8. Because P&Z recommended denial, approval needs six of seven votes, according to KLBJ. Bastrop: Civic IQ describes the Nov. 17 second reading of Ordinance 2026-50, which would ban data centers in General Commercial and require a conditional-use permit in Industrial. I still can’t find posted minutes or a vote result, so I’m treating the outcome as unconfirmed. Daimler Truck (Project Hercules): Transport Topics and San Marcos’ FAQs say the 611-acre Caldwell County site is still a contender but no final site decision has been announced. The Chapter 380 readings are Dec. 1 and Dec. 15. Project Juniper, the 1 million SF, $67 million warehouse at 11902 FM 969, listed a TDLR start of Nov. 23. I haven’t seen confirmation that it has broken ground or who the tenant is.
