Data centers · Hutto

Hutto P&Z unanimously approves the 97.866-acre data center campus replat

OpenTexas meeting record · Nov 3, 2026 · Bisnow · Nov 4, 2026

Following up from yesterday: per the OpenTexas summary of Tuesday’s meeting, Hutto’s Planning and Zoning Commission voted 7–0 to approve the replat of Hutto Data Center Campus Subdivision One, nearly 98 acres north and east of CR 132 split into six commercial lots. A resident asked whether a lot sold off later would need new city approvals before another data center could go up, and staff confirmed it would. The commission also pushed its Unified Development Code rewrite (Chapters 1–3) to Dec. 1, holding out parking and data-center provisions pending a study and asking staff to review Taylor’s new data-center code. The developer and operator still haven’t been publicly named. This lands the same week Bisnow reports Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa’s plan for a data-center development moratorium, stricter water and power rules, and an end to industry tax breaks. My read: local entitlements are moving, but state policy risk is now the bigger variable for powered land in eastern Williamson County.

Mixed-use & multifamily · North and Northwest Austin

Northcross Mall redevelopment would total 1.26 million SF, and Lincoln files 294 units on Four Points

Groundbreak · Nov 2–4, 2026

Groundbreak reports Riverside Resources filed a site plan to redevelop Northcross Mall at 2525 W. Anderson Lane into a nine-building, 1.26 million-SF mixed-use project. Separately, Lincoln Property Company filed plans for a 294-unit, four-story apartment building at 11040 Four Points Drive in Northwest Austin, with completion set for May 2029. In retail, Katz’s Deli is under construction at 620 W. Sixth St., bringing the 24-hour brand back to West Sixth in a 22,620-SF space targeting a late spring 2027 opening.

Industrial · Northeast Austin

Brivo signs a roughly 55,000-SF full-building manufacturing lease

Austin Business Journal · Nov 3, 2026 · Hoodline · Nov 3, 2026

ABJ reports security technology company Brivo signed a roughly 55,000-SF, full-building lease in Northeast Austin that will replace its 12,000-SF manufacturing space in Southwest Austin, more than four times the size, with dozens of new jobs expected. Hoodline notes the address, landlord and lease terms weren’t disclosed, and cites a Matthews report showing 2.64 million SF of Q3 industrial absorption with vacancy at 14.0% and asking rents of $14.22/SF. It’s more evidence that mid-size manufacturing users are backfilling Austin’s newer flex and industrial stock.
