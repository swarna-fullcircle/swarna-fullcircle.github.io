Industrial · Metro-wide, Williamson County, Southeast Austin & Buda

Austin industrial’s spec space is starting to fill: the Q3 reports show the strongest quarter in at least a year, led by AI hardware and Tesla’s orbit

ECR · Oct 27, 2026 · Lee & Associates – Austin · Q3 2026 · Newmark · Q3 2026

The third-quarter industrial reports landed this week, and they’re the best news I’ve read for warehouse and flex owners in a while. ECR calls it the strongest quarter in at least a year, with about 2.6 million square feet of net absorption. Lee & Associates, which measures differently, counted 1.62 million square feet of industrial absorption, enough to outpace the roughly 1.55 million square feet delivered, and vacancy fell to 19.26%, its second straight quarterly decline. Lee says North Williamson County (+634,797 SF) and Hays County (+477,655 SF) led the way, while vacancy is concentrated in newer 100,000–200,000-square-foot rear-load buildings. The tenant list tells the story. Newmark reports Tesla signed the quarter’s largest lease, 538,720 square feet at Mustang Ridge in the southeast, and ECR notes it sits next to a building Tesla supplier Xinquan already occupies. Flex took 310,366 square feet at 610 Blue Springs Blvd. in Georgetown as its data-center business grows, AI Prefab Solutions leased 196,523 square feet at 2100 Limmer Loop in Hutto for a factory and headquarters, and Lee lists Ambrosia Energy at 146,216 square feet in Del Valle. In Buda, ECR says PepsiCo-owned Siete now leases a full 396,000-square-foot building. On the sale side, Lee reports EastGroup bought the 95%-leased, 386,000-square-foot Harris Ridge Business Center from TA Realty. ECR puts about 7.1 million square feet under construction, less than half the 15.5 million of a year ago, and expects vacancy to keep falling. Rents haven’t moved yet, so tenants still have leverage, but ECR thinks landlords may push in 2027. Its main caution is Gov. Abbott’s September pause on new data-center permits, because so much supplier demand traces back to data centers. My advice for occupiers needing 2027 space: lock in now while concessions are still on the table.

Data centers / permitting · Hays County, Williamson County (Taylor) & statewide

Hays County held its data-center incentive hearing yesterday; I haven’t seen an outcome yet, and TCEQ’s permit portal is still dark

Hays County · checked Oct 28, 2026 · The Texas Dispatch · Oct 21, 2026 · TCEQ · checked Oct 28, 2026 · CBS Austin · Oct 26, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 8, 2026

Hays County Commissioners Court held its 11 a.m. public hearing in San Marcos yesterday on the final draft of its Development Regulations, and the county’s page said adoption “may follow.” As of this morning I haven’t found an official or published report on whether the court adopted, amended or postponed the draft, so I’m not reporting an outcome; I’ll keep checking. Earlier coverage noted any adopted rules would take effect no sooner than Jan. 1, 2027. As a reminder, The Texas Dispatch summarized the draft as tying county incentives for “high-intensity uses” (100,000 gallons of water a day, 20 megawatts of peak power, or mechanical cooling for data centers and similar uses) to closed-loop or air-cooled systems and on-site generation or storage. These are incentive conditions, not bans. Statewide, TCEQ’s “STEERS is temporarily down” notice was still posted when I checked this morning, saying the system is offline “to ensure that all applications related to data centers are reviewed by TCEQ staff,” with no return date. Carried over: CBS Austin reported TCEQ has received, but not begun technically reviewing, the air-permit application for the 664-acre Big Watt Digital/PowerHouse Taylor Technology Campus. In Cedar Creek, Community Impact reported earlier this month that Pacifico Energy and BlackChamber filed state plans for Project Longhorn’s first building, nearly 500,000 square feet at an estimated $366 million, with a listed Nov. 30 start. Whether those dates hold depends on how the state permitting review shakes out.

Retail · Northwest Austin (Great Hills), Leander & Oak Hill

Asana Partners lays out its Arboretum makeover, Leander Union opens 155,000 square feet, and Oak Hill Plaza trades to institutional money

CultureMap Austin · Oct 27, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 27, 2026 · The Real Deal via Yahoo Finance · Oct 27, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 27, 2026 · REBusinessOnline · Oct 26, 2026

New this morning: Asana Partners, which bought The Arboretum at 10000 Research Blvd. in February, has finalized plans to add a new retail and dining building of roughly 14,000 square feet (TRD, citing the Austin Business Journal, puts it at 13,600) along Great Hills Trail. Construction is set for spring 2027, with tenants opening by fall 2028. CultureMap reports the plan also adds dining patios, kids’ play areas, more seating, new signs and better paths to the courtyard, plus year-round programming starting in 2027. Per TRD, the plans still need city approval, and Jetset Pilates and skincare brand Peachy have already signed leases; Michael Hsu Office of Architecture is the designer. Up in Leander, Community Impact reports Topo Development Group’s Leander Union, a 13-acre, 10-building, 155,000-square-foot project just east of Crystal Falls Parkway and 183A, celebrated its completion Oct. 21. It includes about 65,000 square feet for retail and dining, 50,000 for office and 40,000 for Northpoint Austin Church. Carried over, condensed: Newmark brokered the sale of the 92.5%-leased, 115,512-square-foot Oak Hill Plaza to a Trademark Property Co. and Cohen & Steers joint venture (REBusinessOnline; no price disclosed). Repositioning established centers into all-day destinations is where I see the most retail energy right now.

Multifamily & residential land · West Campus, The Domain & Round Rock

A 30-story, 300-unit student tower is headed for West Campus, Round Rock approves KB Home’s 72-acre Salerno addition, and Weidner’s Solaris buy stands as a fresh comp

Austin Business Journal · Oct 27, 2026 · Hyperlocal Loop · Oct 27, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 26, 2026 · JLL · Oct 26, 2026

The Austin Business Journal reports Somerset Partners and University Realty are developing a 300-unit student housing tower on Rio Grande Street in West Campus. Hyperlocal Loop, working from state permit filings, lists it at 2307 Rio Grande St., 30 stories, with a Feb. 1, 2027 start, July 2028 completion and an estimated $97.3 million construction cost. The same filings list a $4.5 million Fogo de Chão in Cedar Park, a Burlington at Burleson Crossing East in Bastrop and a P. Terry’s in Liberty Hill. One item I flagged as open last week is now resolved: Community Impact reports Round Rock approved changes to a 71.88-acre tract that lets KB Home expand its Salerno subdivision by about 330 homes, in two phases of 181 and 149. Carried over, condensed: JLL confirmed Weidner Apartment Homes bought Brandywine’s 341-unit Solaris House in Uptown ATX, its fifth Austin-area community (price not stated by JLL; $136 million in earlier reporting). Purpose-built student housing near campus remains one of the few multifamily niches still breaking ground at scale.

Office · Metro-wide, Downtown & Zilker

My office read still holds: Class A tightening, a thin pipeline, and The Republic adding a ground-floor restaurant

ECR · Oct 20, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 26, 2026

Carried over, condensed. ECR reports Class A asking rents rose from $36.01 to $39.61 per square foot over the past year, Class A sublease space is down about 40%, and only 227,707 square feet of office is under construction. Domain Class A vacancy is 4.2%, while downtown Class A vacancy is still 23.6% and about 6.1 million square feet of Class B sits vacant. At The Republic (401 W. 4th St.), where Jackson Walker and Virtu have signed office leases, the Austin Business Journal reports chef Antonia Lofaso’s Scopa Italian Roots plans an 8,300-square-foot restaurant in 2027. Tenants with 2027–2028 expirations in quality space should start early.
