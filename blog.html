Office repositioning · West Austin and downtown

CityView office complex on Loop 360 wins approval for a 475-unit mixed-use redevelopment, while Google’s Sail Tower gets a $25 million office and amenity renovation downtown

Groundbreak · Oct 11, 2026 · Groundbreak · Oct 11 (Sail Tower) · context from CBRE Research · Oct 8

My first read this Monday is an office story told two ways. Groundbreak reports that the CityView Mixed-Use Redevelopment has been approved at 1120 and 1122 South Capital of Texas Highway in West Austin, a site that holds the CityView 1–3 office complex and a parking garage today. Rezoning case C14-2025-0089 moved the 15.518-acre site from Limited Office and Neighborhood Commercial to LR-V-CO-DB90, allowing roughly 475 residential units in a mixed-use building capped at 70 feet: a 40-foot base plus 30 feet through the DB90 density bonus, which requires 12% of rental units at 60% MFI, or 10% at 50% MFI, for 40 years. AREIT City View LLC is the owner and applicant, with Drenner Group as agent; the Zoning and Platting Commission recommended approval 6–3 in July, and a valid petition was filed in opposition. Downtown, Groundbreak also reports a $25 million renovation of 68,875 square feet of office and amenity space at Sail Tower, 601 W. 2nd Street, under an Aug. 11 TDLR filing, with work that began Aug. 14 and is expected to wrap in May 2027. Google signed a full-building lease there in 2019 that runs through 2038, and Cousins Properties bought the 35-story, roughly 804,000-square-foot tower for $521.8 million in December 2024. With CBRE putting metro office vacancy at 23.6% last week, I read these two together: older suburban office along Loop 360 is being entitled out of the office inventory, while a long-term tenant in trophy space downtown keeps reinvesting.

Week ahead / zoning · North Lamar

On Tuesday’s Planning Commission agenda: a roughly 545-unit mixed-use plan on North Lamar at Beaver Street, near a planned light rail station

City of Austin Planning Commission staff report · meeting Oct 13, 2026

One item I’m watching tomorrow. According to the City of Austin staff report for case NPA-2026-0026.01, applicant 8609 N Lamar LLC, represented by Drenner Group, is proposing approximately 545 multifamily rental units, some meeting DB90 affordability requirements, with ground-floor commercial along North Lamar Boulevard. The plan amendment covers 1.0275 acres on Beaver Street and would change the future land use from Single Family to Mixed Use, alongside related zoning case C14-2026-0030 seeking CS-V-CO-DB90-NP on the Lamar-facing land and LO-DB90-NP on the Beaver Street lots. The applicant’s presentation describes 390 units on Tracts 1–3 and 155 on Tract 4, a 60-foot base height along Lamar that DB90 would take to 90 feet, and 70 feet stepping down toward single-family homes on Beaver Street. The site is used today for auto repair and storage and sits about 0.10 miles from the planned Fairfield light rail station. Staff supports the Mixed Use designation; the Council date is still to be determined. It’s another example of DB90 reshaping older commercial frontage along transit corridors.

Data centers / state policy · Taylor and statewide

With Taylor’s campus approved, the next dates to watch are state ones: TWDB’s Oct. 14 report, TCEQ’s Oct. 19 update to the governor, and ERCOT’s Dec. 10 audit filing

CBS Austin · Oct 12, 2026 · Office of the Texas Governor · Sept 21 · Latham & Watkins · Oct 5 · Utility Dive · Aug 21

I’ve covered the Big Watt–PowerHouse vote in Taylor all week, so today I’m looking at what comes next. CBS Austin’s Oct. 12 recap says the 664-acre Taylor Technology Campus agreement includes $28.2 million in community investments, a closed-loop cooling system using reclaimed water, and limits on noise and building setbacks, and that a developer-commissioned study estimates $1.38 billion in tax revenue for Taylor over 20 years and nearly 19,350 temporary construction jobs. The bigger variable for every Central Texas data center deal now sits with state agencies. In a Sept. 21 letter, Gov. Greg Abbott directed TCEQ to pause all permits related to data center projects until ERCOT completes its review, and asked the agency for a compliance update by Monday, Oct. 19. A Latham & Watkins client alert notes that the Texas Water Development Board is expected to report on its data center water-survey enforcement by Oct. 14, and Utility Dive reported that ERCOT is targeting a Dec. 10 filing on its audit of roughly 300 large data center proposals ahead of a PUC open meeting that month. For landowners and brokers along US 79 and in Bastrop and Caldwell counties, timelines on these projects now run through state regulators as much as through city councils.

Condos + retail / construction financing · South Congress

Leland South Congress is underway on a $173 million BGO loan, with 45% of its 265 condos already pre-sold

Austin American-Statesman · Oct 11, 2026

I noted the groundbreaking here last week, and the Statesman’s Julianna Duennes Russ adds the detail I was looking for: demand. She reports that construction on Intracorp Homes’ seven-story Leland South Congress is officially underway thanks to a $173 million construction loan from BGO, and that the project has already pre-sold 45% of its residences, with prices from $400,000 studios to penthouses above $3 million; Intracorp said the pre-sold units have trended toward the higher end of that range, though not by a wide margin. The ground floor will have 22,000 square feet of retail and restaurant space, including 6,000 square feet set aside for an outdoor dining paseo meant as a pedestrian “gateway” to South Congress. Andres Construction is the general contractor, Kirksey is lead architect, Michael Hsu Office of Architecture is handling interiors, and completion is estimated for 2029. Pre-sales at that level are a useful read on the for-sale market in the urban core, and the paseo will be leasable restaurant space to keep an eye on.

Infrastructure + growth · Georgetown

Williamson County breaks ground on a $27 million Ronald Reagan Boulevard segment in Georgetown, as Southwestern University’s 560-acre plan sets an Oct. 23 groundbreaking

Community Impact · Oct 9, 2026 · Williamson County Sun · Oct 6 · Community Impact · Oct 8

Ronald Reagan Boulevard runs right past my office in Leander, so this one is close to home. Community Impact reports that Williamson County broke ground Oct. 7 on Segment D1 of the Ronald Reagan Boulevard expansion, a 2.4-mile, roughly $27 million project that adds two southbound lanes from Williams Drive to CR 245, with the existing road becoming the northbound lanes; completion is expected by summer 2028. Commissioner Valerie Covey said neighborhoods like Sun City, Parmer Ranch, Highland Village and Somerset Ranch will keep making the area a magnet for residential and commercial growth, and the full corridor is planned as an eight-lane divided highway with frontage roads across about 30 miles from Cedar Park to Georgetown. Elsewhere in Georgetown, the Williamson County Sun reports that Southwestern University’s long-anticipated development on the 560 acres east of campus will officially begin with an Oct. 23 groundbreaking, starting with the city’s 2023 bond-approved Customer Service Center, and Community Impact reports that Taylor Morrison and M/I Homes broke ground in late August on Chisholm Trails, about 800 homes near SH 130 opening in late 2027. Roads and rooftops like these are what retail and service users wait for before they commit to a corridor.
