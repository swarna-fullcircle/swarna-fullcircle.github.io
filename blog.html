Data centers / land use · Hutto, City of Austin & statewide

Hutto takes up a replat of its data-center campus tomorrow, and Austin’s memo shows a data-center wastewater request just outside the city

City of Hutto P&Z agenda · Nov 3, 2026 meeting · City of Austin memo · Oct 28, 2026 · Our Texas Times · Nov 1, 2026 · Capital Highlights · Nov 2, 2026 · FracTracker Alliance · Oct 2026 · TCEQ · checked Nov 2, 2026

Hutto’s Planning and Zoning Commission meets Tuesday, Nov. 3, at 7 p.m., and its agenda includes a public hearing and possible action on a replat of the Hutto Data Center Campus Subdivision One: about 97.866 acres, six commercial lots, north and east of CR 132. Splitting a campus into lots like that is the kind of step I watch for before vertical construction or a sale. Back in Austin, I went back through the city’s Oct. 28 data-center memo. Beyond the Dec. 3 Council vote on Phase 1 rules, it says Austin Water has received one administratively complete service extension request, for wastewater only, for a proposed data center near 16301 Cameron Road. That site is outside Austin’s city limits and ETJ but inside Austin Water’s wastewater service area. Our Texas Times stresses that the 13 Austin Energy inquiries (20 MW or more each) are not approved projects, and none is ready to sign a facilities extension agreement. Statewide, this morning’s Capital Highlights column recaps the FracTracker Alliance and Public Citizen finding of 35 TCEQ permitting actions on data centers since the governor’s Sept. 21 directive. The governor’s office maintains that “no permits have been approved.” TCEQ’s STEERS page is unchanged today: still down until mid-November, with the MSGP renewal deadline extended to Dec. 28. My read: the action is shifting to suburban plats and utility service areas outside city limits, so diligence on who serves water and wastewater matters as much as who serves power.

Office · Market-wide, CBD & Arboretum

Avison Young: a second straight quarter of positive office absorption, but vacancy holds at 25% after Waterline

Avison Young · Q3 2026 · Realty News Report · Oct 31, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 30, 2026

Avison Young’s Q3 report counts 289,913 square feet of positive net absorption, bringing the year-to-date total to 447,557 square feet. Leasing hit 944,247 square feet, just 4% below the five-year quarterly average, and the CBD took nearly 40% of it, helped by Jackson Walker and Virtu Financial at The Republic. xAI’s 112,297-square-foot occupancy drove much of the sublease absorption, but small and midsize tenants, especially in class B, contributed too. Vacancy stayed at 25.0% because Waterline’s mostly vacant 715,005-square-foot office component delivered. Sublease availability fell to 2.9 million square feet. The pipeline is down to 195,707 square feet, all of it at the largely preleased 5th & Walsh, due in late 2028. Smaller deals fit the picture: Lucid Private Offices opened 22,329 square feet at Stonebridge Plaza, and a local firm bought the 45,000-square-foot Gabriel’s Court near UT, per ABJ. My read: with almost nothing new coming, every quarter of absorption chips away at the vacancy, and owners of well-located class B space should feel that first.

City Council · Nov. 5 agenda

Thursday’s Council agenda: a 545-unit North Lamar rezoning, up to $695 million in Austin Energy bonds, and a storage project staff oppose

Austin Politics · Oct 29, 2026 · City of Austin · Nov 5, 2026 meeting page

Council’s first post-election meeting is Thursday, Nov. 5, and Austin Politics has walked through the draft agenda. Items 31 and 32 would rezone 8605–8613 North Lamar Blvd. and 604–706 Beaver Street in District 4 to DB90, allowing up to 90 feet. The staff report describes about 545 apartments and a small retail plaza, and staff and the Planning Commission recommend approval. Item 3 would authorize up to $695 million in Austin Energy revenue bonds to pay off commercial paper, fund new electric system projects and possibly refinance 2017 bonds. Item 33 would rezone 2.89 acres at 3601 Davis Lane for a mini-warehouse. Staff recommend denial, though the Zoning and Platting Commission voted 8–1 to approve it with extra prohibited uses. Also on the agenda are a commercial rent buy-down resolution and sending part of the Dog’s Head tax zone revenue to housing and parks. Speaker registration opens today at 10 a.m. The agenda is a draft and can change.

Industrial & advanced manufacturing · Southeast Austin, Manor & Kyle

Amazon’s 1.2 million-SF robotics plant is filed to start Nov. 16, and Presidium’s 285,000-SF warehouse follows in January

Texas Gazette · updated Nov 1, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 20, 2026 · The Real Deal · Oct 22, 2026 · FORMATION · Lee & Associates · Transwestern · Oct 2026

The state accessibility filing for Amazon’s “Project Toaster” at 475 Patton Ave. puts the core-and-shell building at 1.2 million square feet and $100 million. The record lists construction from Nov. 16 to July 13, 2027, and that figure doesn’t appear to include interior build-out or equipment. ABJ has reported 300 to 500 jobs at the Dog’s Head plant. The Real Deal ranked Presidium’s “Project Phantom,” a $60 million, 285,000-square-foot warehouse at 11505½ Spirit Drive in The Row, as Texas’ most expensive new filing for its week. It’s set to start in January and finish by October 2027. Last week’s deals still frame the market: FORMATION and New York Life Investors bought Met Center 5 (154,000 SF), 100% preleased to an unnamed credit tenant before closing. Purchase Corner took 108,000 SF at Quarter Crossing in Manor, and Kyle Commerce Center’s 328,256 SF of shallow-bay space is underway. My read: big users are still committing in Southeast Austin, and spec builders are betting on smaller, powered space.

Retail & restaurants · Kyle, Leander & Pflugerville

Gringo’s picks Kyle Park and Leander’s Northline for its Austin debut, and Ramen Tatsu-Ya heads to Pflugerville

What Now Austin (citing ABJ) · Oct 28, 2026 · The Austin Report · weekly roundup · Transwestern · Oct 27, 2026

Houston’s Gringo’s Mexican Kitchen is coming to the Austin area, as reported by ABJ and recapped by What Now. It plans a roughly 9,500-square-foot restaurant at Kyle Park, targeting November 2027, and is buying property in Leander’s 116-acre Northline for another 9,500-square-foot location targeting mid-2028. The COO says each location typically hires 125 to 150 people. Kyle Park is the roughly 412,588-square-foot, largely leased lifestyle center Transwestern and NewQuest just started at I-35 and Bebee Road. In Pflugerville, Ramen Tatsu-Ya has filed for a 2,952-square-foot restaurant at 18725 FM 685 off SH 130, with a $1.4 million build-out and a summer 2027 opening. Snooze A.M. Eatery has filed for a $1.2 million restaurant in the same development. My read: regional and local operators are following rooftops up and down I-35 and SH 130, which keeps suburban pad and inline space tight.

Land & master plans · Georgetown, Round Rock, Granger & Leander

Carried over: Heirloom breaks ground Thursday, plus KB Home’s Salerno expansion, Granger’s 765 lots and Leander’s Oakwood Hills

Community Impact · Oct 29, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 26, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 29, 2026 · Hyperlocal Loop · Oct 29, 2026

Georgetown approved the Heirloom PID and $20.89 million in city bonds for the 620-acre, 3,600-unit project near Williams Drive and Ronald Reagan Boulevard, and it breaks ground Thursday, Nov. 5. Round Rock approved KB Home’s 71.88-acre Salerno expansion, about 330 homes in two phases. Granger approved WBW Development’s 765-lot Lakeside at Granger, and Leander set phasing for the first 183 homes at the 337-acre Oakwood Hills. These rooftops are what eventually justify retail like Kyle Park and Northline.
