Data centers / land entitlement · Caldwell County

PowerHouse’s nearly 900-acre Uhland site leaves city ETJ — Caldwell County now holds the data-center path

Austin Business Journal · Sep 28, 2026

Tuesday’s freshest CRE infrastructure read sits just southeast of the MSA core. Austin Business Journal reports PowerHouse Data Centers — fully owned and operated by Virginia-based American Real Estate Partners — is moving a nearly 900-acre Caldwell County project near Uhland forward without city oversight after landowners petitioned to release the property from the City of Uhland’s extraterritorial jurisdiction under a state law that allows them to do so. ABJ’s framing is blunt: the huge site has been “released into Caldwell County purview,” paving the way for a data center once the land sits under county rather than municipal ETJ control. Earlier Bisnow and Data Center Dynamics coverage (May 2026) had already tracked PowerHouse’s push for a roughly 500-acre campus inside the broader Caldwell Valley Technology Park corridor and the strategy of preferring county jurisdiction after local pushback. For me, this is the southern I-35 corridor land story that now sits next to Abbott’s statewide TCEQ permit pause: entitlement geography can move even while air/water authorizations stay on a brake — and CRE owners along that corridor should underwrite both clocks.

Industrial delivery · Kyle / Hays County

Cadence McShane delivers Kyle Technology Park Phase I — 75,000 SF of flex warehouse south of Austin

REJournals · Sep 28, 2026

The southern MSA industrial corridor still has vertical product landing. REJournals reports Cadence McShane Construction completed Kyle Technology Park Phase I for client TIG Real Estate Services — three warehouse buildings totaling 75,000 square feet (25,000 SF each) with 35-foot clear height, designed for multi-tenant warehouse and flex industrial users. The 41-acre site at FM 1626 and Plum Creek Meadows sits for I-35, Austin-Bergstrom, West Austin, and San Antonio access; Cadence McShane also finished earthwork, utilities, drainage, and a new crossover road, with Studio 8 Architects as architect of record. Full buildout of the master-planned park is framed at up to 11 buildings and roughly 300,000 square feet for office, industrial, showroom, and build-to-suit users. I’m keeping this on today’s board next to the Caldwell data-center entitlement fight: Hays County small-bay flex is delivering where population and last-mile demand already are, while power-heavy digital infrastructure still fights for its path a few miles farther east.

Multifamily entitlement · Southwest Austin

The Real Deal revisits Stratus’s Circle C Tract 110 win — ~1,000 units, aquifer politics, and a dissolving sponsor

The Real Deal · Sep 28, 2026 · also FOX 7 Austin · Sep 24 · Austin Business Journal · Sep 25

Southwest Austin’s biggest near-term multifamily entitlement is still drawing national CRE ink a few days after Council. The Real Deal’s Sep. 28 write-up frames the Austin City Council rezoning that could put about 1,000 residences on Stratus-tied land at 11010 ½ South MoPac Expressway — a roughly 67-acre Circle C tract previously zoned for office, with about 16 acres candidates for redevelopment and 10% of units framed as affordable per ABJ. TRD flags two open questions the vote didn’t settle: the site sits in the Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone (Save Our Springs Alliance opposes redevelopment there), and Austin-based Stratus announced plans to dissolve earlier this year, leaving uncertainty over who ultimately delivers the project. FOX 7’s earlier coverage captured the school-zone traffic fight near Kiker Elementary and Stratus CEO Beau Armstrong’s case that residential peak traffic would be lighter than a full office build-out, with a full TIA still ahead at site development. For me, this remains the MSA housing-pipeline CRE overlay: corridor density won a vote, but aquifer, access, and sponsor continuity will still price the schedule.

Multifamily entitlement · Central Austin

Council clears Rosedale School rezoning — 435-unit path still blocked by a deed-restriction lawsuit

Community Impact · Sep 25, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Sep 24

Central Austin’s other big entitlement win is still not a construction calendar. Community Impact reports Austin City Council gave final approval Sept. 24 — after three postponements since May — to rezone the former AISD Rosedale School site at 2117 W. 49th St. from single-family to multifamily, clearing one of two hurdles for OHT Partners’ proposed 435-unit, six-story apartment plan on about 4.62 acres with a 75-foot height limit. Because neighbors filed a valid petition, the case needed a supermajority; Council also adopted a District 7 amendment directing staff to consider street impact fees for traffic-safety work, including at 49th and Burnet. AISD agreed in 2025 to sell the closed campus to OHT for $26 million as the district works a budget shortfall, but Community Impact stresses the sale still depends on AISD winning its Travis County lawsuit against Rosedale homeowners over a 1938 deed restriction — no trial date set yet. Neighbors’ Play Fair With Rosedale group has floated a smaller roughly 150–225-unit alternative. For me, this stays on the board next to Circle C: zoning can move while title risk and deed covenants still hold the shovel.

Office investment sales · Downtown Austin

KBS exits 515 Congress — Palmatum / AQUILA / Glendon buy ~268K SF; ~80% leased CBD tower

CRE Market Beat · Sep 24, 2026 · also Yahoo Finance / Austin Business Journal · Sep 24

Downtown office liquidity is still finding buyers for the right capital plan. CRE Market Beat reports KBS Real Estate Investment Trust III sold 515 Congress — a 26-story Class A tower totaling 267,956 square feet at Congress Avenue and Sixth Street — to a joint venture of Palmatum Ventures, AQUILA, and Glendon Capital Management, with JLL Capital Markets (Mike McDonald, Jonathan Napper, Ryan Stevens, and Drew Fuller) representing the seller. Sale price was not disclosed; Yahoo Finance / Austin Business Journal report Travis County appraised the asset at $117 million this year (down from $150 million in 2022), and an AQUILA spokesperson put occupancy around 80%, with office tenants including Austin Business Journal, Boeing, Cloudera, and WebAI. During its hold, KBS invested more than $15.5 million in lobby, lounge/conference, and elevator modernization; AQUILA managing principal Ben Tolson said the JV sees room to enhance the experience for existing tenants and companies it hopes to attract. For me, this remains the investment-sales CBD read of the week: institutional capital will underwrite refreshed product even while tenants still shop hard on concessions.

Industrial pipeline · Taylor / Williamson County

Partners pushes TaylorPort C into permitting — ~572,640 SF rail-served spec near Samsung

Partners Real Estate · Sep 23, 2026

Northeast corridor industrial is still scaling with the semiconductor story. Partners Real Estate announced TaylorPort C — a roughly 572,640-square-foot speculative, cross-dock, rail-served industrial building on FM 3349 in Taylor — has entered the permitting process after lease-up of the park’s first two phases. The building sits on about 35.34 acres with 40-foot clear height, 3,000 amps of expandable power, ESFR sprinklers, and about 5.1 acres of industrial outdoor storage for trailer staging, containers, or fleet parking. Partners Development is developing; John Colglazier, SIOR, John Simons, and Kyle Kennan are exclusive leasing agents. Colglazier’s statement, carried in the release, frames rail-served boxes of this size as rare in Central Texas and ties demand to manufacturers and suppliers putting down roots near Samsung’s Taylor investment. Paired with Kyle’s small-bay flex delivery farther south, I’m still reading a two-speed industrial MSA: Hays County flex completing while large-bay, rail-served spec queues in Williamson.

Data centers / regulation · Statewide (MSA impact)

Abbott’s TCEQ data-center permit pause still frames every campus clock — including Caldwell

Texas Tribune · Sep 21, 2026 · also TechTarget · Sep 24 · Community Impact · Austin Business Journal · Sep 22

Digital-infrastructure entitlement is still on a statewide brake — and today’s Uhland ETJ news makes the sequencing risk sharper, not softer. The Texas Tribune reports Gov. Greg Abbott on Sept. 21 directed TCEQ Executive Director Kelly Keel to halt new permits sought by data centers until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board finish audits covering electricity use, water use, community impact, ownership, and tax breaks. TechTarget’s Sep. 24 follow-up quotes TCEQ’s Ryan Vise confirming the agency has paused air and water permits and other authorizations for infrastructure directly supporting data centers, will fold ERCOT findings into its evaluations, and still owes Abbott a compliance update by Oct. 19; developer-facing dates now include Oct. 12 for ERCOT’s State and Community Impact RFI responses and Dec. 10 for ERCOT’s planned report to the PUC — even as TCEQ has not fully clarified how the freeze applies to projects outside ERCOT’s ≥25 MW interconnection review. Community Impact and Austin Business Journal had already flagged “behind-the-meter” builds still needing TCEQ air/water authorizations, and ABJ named a high-profile Bastrop County gas-powered campus Anthropic has been in talks to lease as an early pause read. For CRE around Taylor, Bastrop, Georgetown, Four Points, and now Caldwell/Uhland, schedule risk remains live — leaving city ETJ is not the same as clearing state environmental permits.
