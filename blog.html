Data centers / state permitting · Statewide, including Bastrop County

TCEQ takes its automated permit-filing system offline and suspends the data-center stormwater permits watchdogs flagged

The Texas Tribune · Oct 21, 2026 · TCEQ · Oct 19, 2026 · Inside Climate News · Oct 19, 2026

The data-center freeze took another turn yesterday. The Texas Tribune reports that TCEQ has shut down its automated electronic filing system, STEERS, after Public Citizen and FracTracker Alliance said the agency had issued multiple construction stormwater permits for data-center projects despite Gov. Abbott’s directive. The agency says it suspended the specific data-center permits the groups identified this week, and on Wednesday announced the system “used for automatic authorizations” was being temporarily disabled. “To ensure all applications are thoroughly reviewed by agency staff to determine if they are related to data centers, applications must be physically provided to TCEQ,” spokesman Ryan Vise said. The Tribune notes STEERS handles a wide range of filings, including air and water permits, and the portal was inaccessible Wednesday. My read for clients: this isn’t only a data-center story. If you have any project that relies on an automatic TCEQ authorization — a construction stormwater notice of intent is the common one — plan for paper filings and staff review, and budget extra time until the agency says otherwise. TCEQ’s Oct. 19 letter to the governor still gives no restart date for data-center permits.

Office leasing · CBD South / South Lamar

SolarWinds signs 100,000 square feet at Zilker Point to relocate its global headquarters

Connect CRE · Oct 21, 2026 · AQUILA Commercial (property page)

The biggest office lease headline of the week. Connect CRE reports SolarWinds will move its global headquarters from its existing Austin office to Zilker Point, the Class A midrise at 218 South Lamar owned by Generation Commercial Properties. The 100,000 square feet is just over half of the building’s roughly 195,000 square feet, and move-in is expected in fall 2027. The company has more than 400 employees in Austin. JLL’s Russell Young and Mallory Lewis represented SolarWinds; AQUILA Commercial’s Bart Matheney and Chad Barrett represented ownership. It lines up with what I shared yesterday from ECR’s research: well-amenitized, well-located Class A space close to downtown keeps winning the large requirements, and the quality end of the market is getting tighter.

Infrastructure / airport · Austin-Bergstrom, Southeast Austin

AUS breaks ground on its campus-wide utility project as Council weighs a $224 million contract increase today

Austin-Bergstrom International Airport · Oct 19, 2026 · The Austin Bulletin · Oct 18, 2026 · City of Austin agenda · Oct 22, 2026

The airport celebrated the groundbreaking of its Utilities Infrastructure Campus-Wide project on Oct. 19: new aircraft-rated stormwater and water-quality trunk lines and ponds, redundant medium-voltage power, telecom duct banks and hydronic piping to support the new Central Utility Plant, the Arrivals & Departures Hall and Concourse B. Completion is expected in 2029, and Mayor Watson framed the expansion around 30 to 35 million annual passengers. On today’s Council agenda, Item 27 would add $224 million to the Sundt/Archer Western contract, taking the ceiling from $120 million to up to $344 million; The Austin Bulletin notes staff attributes the increase to Concourse B and the Arrivals and Departures Hall growing in size and scope. I’m not reporting an outcome until Council votes. Paired with Amazon’s Dog’s Head plant and the warehouse filings at The Row, the airport corridor remains the metro’s most active industrial and logistics story.

Data centers / county policy · Hays County

Hays County’s draft development rules would tie incentives for data centers and other heavy users to water and power standards

The Texas Dispatch · Oct 21, 2026

Counties can’t zone, so Hays County is using the tools it has. The Texas Dispatch reports that a 225-page draft of the county’s Development Regulations, dated Oct. 15, defines a “high-intensity use” as a project averaging at least 100,000 gallons of water a day (or peaking at 250,000), drawing at least 20 megawatts at peak, or using mechanical cooling for data centers, crypto mining or high-density computing. To qualify for county incentives, high-water users would need air-cooled, hybrid or closed-loop systems, with tight limits on drinking-quality water, and incentives “may be withheld or denied” unless on-site generation or storage covers at least half of peak demand or provides full backup; projects of 50 megawatts or more would also need dispatchable generation or a firm contract for it. As written, these are conditions for incentives, not bans. The Commissioners Court holds a public hearing at 11 a.m. Oct. 27, and no adoption date has been set. Anyone looking at I-35 corridor land between Austin and San Antonio for power-heavy users should read the draft.

Industrial leasing · Georgetown, Williamson County

TSS’s 380,000-square-foot lease brings Fidelis’ Berry Creek Business Park in Georgetown to 100% leased

Groundbreak · Oct 21, 2026 · TSS, Inc. release · Oct 6, 2026

Close to home, and a good marker for AI-related demand that isn’t a data center itself. TSS, a Nasdaq-listed data-center services and rack-integration company, signed a seven-year lease for roughly 380,000 square feet across Buildings 1 (126,722 square feet) and 3 (253,164 square feet) at Berry Creek Business Park, 3000 N. I-35 in Georgetown. Groundbreak reports the three-building, 520,571-square-foot park on 57 acres is now fully leased; TSS took occupancy of Building 1 this month, with Building 3’s buildout finishing in 2027. The new space is nearly four times its current 97,000-square-foot Round Rock warehouse and less than a mile from its Georgetown integration facility. HPI represented TSS and CBRE represented Fidelis. The deal was announced Oct. 6; I’m including it now because the full lease-up is newly reported.

Land / mixed-use · Lockhart, Caldwell County

A 535-acre hospitality and creative hub is floated for Lockhart

Austin Business Journal · Oct 21, 2026

The southern edge of the MSA keeps drawing big land plans. ABJ reports that Lacey Wickline’s 535-acre Lockhart Commercial District could bring a hotel, a sports complex, retail and maker space for creatives, with about 1,000 jobs, by 2029. The full story is behind ABJ’s paywall, so I’m limiting myself to those headline details — treat it as a proposal, not an approved project. Retail / downtown · Red River Cultural District

Mayor Watson forms a task force to save the Red River Cultural District after a string of venue closures

Austin Business Journal · Oct 21, 2026 · CultureMap Austin · Oct 21, 2026

A downtown street-level retail story worth watching. CultureMap reports Mayor Kirk Watson and Council Member Zo Qadri assembled a task force led by Downtown Strategy Officer Jonathan Stone, with Austin Economic Development, the planning department, the Downtown Austin Alliance, the Music Venue Alliance, Waterloo Greenway and others, to “preserve a quality cultural district and encourage economic vitality in the area” and deliver actionable directives. The district’s September open letter cited closures including Koriente, Feels So Good, Empire Control Room & Garage, Marlow and Valhalla, and flagged Elysium and The 13th Floor as at risk after their building was listed for sale. With Waterloo Greenway and the convention center redevelopment nearby, how the city supports — or doesn’t — these small-format tenants will shape values on that edge of downtown.

ABJ · CultureMap Austin

Industrial / advanced manufacturing · Dog’s Head, Southeast Austin

Update: Amazon’s Dog’s Head filing is “Project Toaster,” one 1.2 million-square-foot building with factory, casting, office and utility-plant areas

Texas Construction Hub · Oct 21, 2026 · Austin American-Statesman · Oct 20, 2026 · The Real Deal · Oct 21, 2026

A follow-up to yesterday’s lead industrial item. Texas Construction Hub reports the Oct. 19 state registration names the project “Project Toaster – Core and Shell,” with Amazon.com Services LLC as owner, and describes a single building at 475 Patton Ave. organized into four areas, a $100 million estimate and a Nov. 16, 2026 start with a listed July 13, 2027 completion. It stresses that the $100 million covers site work and core and shell, not equipping the plant, and that filed dates are planning targets. The Statesman previously reported up to 500 manufacturing and engineering jobs. Separately, The Real Deal recapped Endeavor’s $210 million fund — Endeavor is the master developer of Dog’s Head.
