Williamson & Hays · Data-center policy

Tomorrow night: Georgetown staff give Inner Loop Exchange a split review, and Kyle weighs a citywide ban

KVUE · Nov 11–13, 2026 · Texas Data Center Watch · Nov 12, 2026 · Connect CRE · Nov 13, 2026

Tuesday, Nov. 17 is the data-center night I’ve been flagging all week, and the Georgetown staff review is worth reading before the 6 p.m. Planning and Zoning hearing. KVUE reports that city staff found the Inner Loop Exchange request meets three of the five criteria for both a zoning amendment and a Planned Unit Development, and raised concerns about the project’s size and how it fits the surrounding neighborhood. Texas Data Center Watch, citing the official packet, counts zero written comments in favor and 32 opposed, and notes the PUD requires closed-loop cooling. Presidents of the Churchill Farms and Riverhill HOAs told KVUE their concern is location, next to homes and schools. Developer Phillip Niels points to protections the current General Commercial zoning doesn’t require: 100- to 150-foot building setbacks, a 50-foot landscaped buffer with a 6-foot berm, backup generators limited to outages and brief testing, and a sound study before construction. Power would come from Oncor, not Georgetown Utility Systems. The request would take the allowed data-center size on the roughly 28 acres from 5,000 SF to 290,000 SF for a 68 MW building (Connect CRE puts capital investment near $1 billion). The same evening, Kyle’s council considers Council Member Claudia Zapata’s item directing the city manager to draft an ordinance that defines data centers and bans them in every zoning district. I’ll report both outcomes here Wednesday.

Industrial · Advanced manufacturing

“SCADA” tenants now lease 3.7 million SF here, and Aalo Atomics is eyeing another 606,000 SF in Georgetown

The Real Deal (citing Austin Business Journal) · Nov 13–15, 2026

This weekend’s Real Deal roundup puts a name on the tenant base driving so much of our industrial absorption: SCADA, short for space, chips, advanced manufacturing, defense and AI. Citing Austin Business Journal reporting by Kathryn Lundstrom, it says companies in that group lease 3.7 million SF of office and industrial space in the Austin metro, up from 200,000 SF in 2015, and that the ones needing big factory space are looking to Cedar Park, Hutto and Georgetown. A good example: The Real Deal, citing ABJ, reports nuclear startup Aalo Atomics will occupy the 205,000 SF CrossPoint Building 2 at 4815 N. I-35 in Georgetown, part of Jackson Shaw’s 224-acre CrossPoint Business District, following an Aug. 31 state filing for a $25 million lab, machine shop and production build-out. Aalo is also reportedly in talks for the 606,000 SF spec building next door at 4805 N. I-35, which LoopNet lists as available in March 2027. Lease terms weren’t disclosed. For owners of North I-35 and SH 130 land, this is the demand story to watch.

Industrial · Southeast Austin

Amazon’s 1.2 million SF Project Toaster hits its filed start date today

TDLR TABS record · Texas Gazette · Texas Construction Hub · Nov 2026

Today, Nov. 16, is the filed start date for Amazon’s Project Toaster core and shell. The state accessibility record lists Amazon.com Services LLC as owner of a 1.2 million SF new-construction project at 475 Patton Ave. in Southeast Austin, with an estimated cost of $100 million and completion projected for July 13, 2027. The scope covers civil site work and one building with four areas: office, factory, casting and a utility plant, designed by WSP USA. A filed start date is the registrant’s projection, not confirmation that crews have mobilized. It sits in the same eastern corridor as Project Juniper, the 1,015,000 SF, $67 million warehouse at 11902 FM 969 whose filed start is Nov. 23. Texas Construction Hub notes that site matches Link Logistics’ 2024 Juniper Crossing plan, though no connection to the current owner entity has been confirmed and the tenant is still unnamed.

Multifamily · Affordable housing

Rosewood Courts reopens, Cairn Point Cameron opens, and Waverly North is underway

Hoodline (citing KXAN, KVUE, the Statesman) · Nov 13–14, 2026

Three income-restricted projects moved forward last week. Rosewood Courts in East Austin, which opened in 1939, has reopened with 184 new affordable rental homes for households at or below 60% of area median income, plus eight restored original bungalows and 12 nearby ownership homes (Hoodline, citing KXAN). City leaders cut the ribbon on Vecino Group’s Cairn Point Cameron at 1500 E. St. Johns Ave., with 150 units for seniors 55 and older at or below 50% AMI, 100 of them permanent supportive housing; KXAN put the cost at about $42 million. In North University, O-SDA Industries is building Waverly North at 3710 Cedar St., 76 units at 30% to 80% AMI targeted for winter 2028, backed by a Council-approved incentive of up to $864,210 over 10 years. These deliveries matter for nearby retail and service tenants, and for how the market absorbs new supply.

Late last week · Condensed

Muny moves to Dec. 3, a Burleson Road warehouse renovation, Dell in Round Rock, Rooms To Go in Leander, and Springdale Green’s record C-PACE

Austin Politics · The American Developer · Hoodline · Commercial Property Executive · Commercial Observer · Nov 12–14, 2026

Council’s vote on the UT agreement for Lions Municipal Golf Course is now set for Dec. 3. Staff slides show the roughly 190 acres UT could develop going from about 1.7 million SF to about 4.9 million SF of floor area (Austin Politics). A $10 million renovation of a 124,313 SF warehouse at 7051 Burleson Road is labeled “Kendra Scott Warehouse,” but the tenant field reads “Not Assigned,” so treat the name as a label (The American Developer). Dell filed an $8.0 million server and MEP renovation of Building 3 at 701 E. Parmer Lane (The American Developer). Rooms To Go plans a roughly 49,182 SF showroom at Northline in Leander (Hoodline). Woodside Health bought the 55,000 SF Hills Medical Portfolio in West Lake Hills (CPE), and Gabriel’s Court (45,658 SF) traded to Realty Capital Partners and The Town Lake Company (Hoodline). Jay Paul’s $227.5 million C-PACE loan on Springdale Green is the largest in Texas history (Commercial Observer).
