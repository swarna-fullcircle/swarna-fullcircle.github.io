Office investment sales · Downtown Austin

CRE Daily revisits 515 Congress — Aquila / Glendon / Palmatum buy ~268K SF as CBD pricing looks for a floor

CRE Daily · Sep 29, 2026 · also CRE Market Beat · Yahoo Finance / Austin Business Journal · Sep 24

National CRE ink is still circling yesterday’s downtown office trade. CRE Daily’s Sep. 29 brief frames the joint venture of Aquila Commercial, Glendon Capital Management, and Palmatum Ventures acquiring 515 Congress — a 26-story, about 268,000-square-foot tower at Congress and Sixth — from KBS for an undisclosed price. County appraisal is cited at $117 million in 2026 (down from $150 million in 2022); an Aquila spokesperson put the tower at about 80% leased, with roughly 66,000 square feet available and tenants including Austin Business Journal, Boeing, Cloudera, and WebAI. CRE Daily pairs the deal with Hines’ $151 million buy of 405 Colorado in July and reads two sizable CBD trades in three months as a possible pricing floor after the oversupply correction. JLL Capital Markets (Mike McDonald, Jonathan Napper, Ryan Stevens, and Drew Fuller) represented KBS; Aquila managing principal Ben Tolson has said the JV plans upgrades for current and prospective tenants. For me, this is today’s investment-sales headline: local operating capital plus outside money is underwriting refreshed, well-leased towers even while tenants still shop hard on concessions.

Retail investment sales · Central Austin

JLL brokers Casis Village sale — Randalls-anchored Tarrytown center, 92% leased on 3.4 acres

REBusinessOnline · Sep 29, 2026

Neighborhood retail still has liquidity when the tenancy is right. REBusinessOnline reports JLL negotiated the sale of Casis Village, a shopping center in Austin’s Tarrytown neighborhood. Square footage was not disclosed; the site spans 3.4 acres. Grocer Randalls anchors the center, which was 92 percent leased at sale, with other tenants including Estilo Boutique, Starbucks, Tarrytown Pharmacy, Zed’s, and Face Foundrie. Chris Gerard and Shea Petrick of JLL represented the seller — a joint venture between Ivy Cos. and 35 South Capital; the buyer was not named. For CRE owners and brokers watching grocery-anchored product inside the urban core, this is a clean Sep. 29 data point: high occupancy and a national grocer still move the bid even when price and buyer stay off the record.

Data centers / land entitlement · Caldwell County

PowerHouse’s nearly 900-acre Uhland site leaves city ETJ — Caldwell County now holds the data-center path

Austin Business Journal · Sep 28, 2026

The southern corridor’s biggest entitlement geography story is still live. Austin Business Journal reports PowerHouse Data Centers — fully owned and operated by Virginia-based American Real Estate Partners — is moving a nearly 900-acre Caldwell County project near Uhland forward without city oversight after landowners petitioned to release the property from the City of Uhland’s extraterritorial jurisdiction under a state law that allows them to do so. ABJ’s framing: the huge site has been “released into Caldwell County purview,” paving a path for a data center under county rather than municipal ETJ control. Earlier Bisnow and Data Center Dynamics coverage (May 2026) had tracked PowerHouse’s push for a roughly 500-acre campus inside the broader Caldwell Valley Technology Park corridor and the preference for county jurisdiction after local pushback. For me, this still sits next to Abbott’s statewide TCEQ permit pause: leaving city ETJ is not the same as clearing state air and water authorizations — CRE owners along that corridor should underwrite both clocks.

Industrial delivery · Kyle / Hays County

Kyle Technology Park Phase I opens — Cadence McShane / TIG deliver 75,000 SF; SABJ adds Sep. 29 coverage

San Antonio Business Journal · Sep 29, 2026 · also REJournals · Sep 28

Southern MSA industrial product is open for business, and regional ink is still catching up. San Antonio Business Journal’s Sep. 29 piece frames Kyle Technology Park Phase I as an industrial campus opening in the San Antonio–Austin corridor, with full buildout measured at about 300,000 square feet. REJournals’ Sep. 28 completion note fills in the specs: Cadence McShane Construction finished three warehouse buildings totaling 75,000 square feet (25,000 SF each) with 35-foot clear height for client TIG Real Estate Services, multi-tenant warehouse and flex designed, on a 41-acre site at FM 1626 and Plum Creek Meadows with I-35, Austin-Bergstrom, West Austin, and San Antonio access. Studio 8 Architects was architect of record; Cadence McShane also completed earthwork, utilities, drainage, and a new crossover road. Master plan: up to 11 buildings and roughly 300,000 SF for office, industrial, showroom, and build-to-suit. I’m keeping Hays County small-bay flex on the board next to Caldwell’s power-heavy data-center fight — two very different industrial demand stories a short drive apart.

Mixed-use / retail pipeline · Round Rock

Round Rock clears alcohol sales at The District — Mark IV’s mixed-use retail leasing path opens

Community Impact · Sep 28, 2026

North MSA mixed-use just removed a tenanting hurdle. Community Impact reports Round Rock City Council approved a sitewide exemption allowing alcohol sales at The District, Mark IV Capital’s upcoming mixed-use project near I-35 and SH 45. The variance was needed because the property line sits within 300 feet of the IDEA Public Schools Round Rock Tech campus, where city ordinance otherwise bars alcohol sales. Mark IV’s Bob Boone told council about 17 acres of the 65.5-acre development would have been constrained, reducing interest from grocery, restaurant, and other retail tenants. Construction update from the same presentation: the parking garage for the first residential building is about halfway done; first-phase retail is expected to break ground in late September or October and complete in fall 2027, with first phase of the overall project framed for early 2027. For me, this is a practical CRE leasing story — ordinance geography can block or unlock the tenant mix as surely as rent.

Multifamily entitlement · Southwest Austin

Stratus Circle C Tract 110 still draws ink — ~1,000 units, aquifer politics, and a dissolving sponsor

The Real Deal · Sep 28, 2026 · also FOX 7 Austin · Sep 24 · Austin Business Journal · Sep 25

Southwest Austin’s biggest near-term multifamily entitlement remains on my board. The Real Deal’s Sep. 28 write-up frames the Austin City Council rezoning that could put about 1,000 residences on Stratus-tied land at 11010 ½ South MoPac Expressway — a roughly 67-acre Circle C tract previously zoned for office, with about 16 acres candidates for redevelopment and 10% of units framed as affordable per ABJ. TRD flags two open questions the vote didn’t settle: the site sits in the Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone (Save Our Springs Alliance opposes redevelopment there), and Austin-based Stratus announced plans to dissolve earlier this year, leaving uncertainty over who ultimately delivers the project. FOX 7’s earlier coverage captured the school-zone traffic fight near Kiker Elementary and Stratus CEO Beau Armstrong’s case that residential peak traffic would be lighter than a full office build-out, with a full TIA still ahead at site development. Corridor density won a vote; aquifer, access, and sponsor continuity will still price the schedule.

Data centers / regulation · Statewide (MSA impact)

Abbott’s TCEQ data-center permit pause still frames every campus clock — including Caldwell

Texas Tribune · Sep 21, 2026 · also TechTarget · Sep 24 · Community Impact · Austin Business Journal · Sep 22

Digital-infrastructure entitlement is still on a statewide brake — and the Uhland ETJ release makes the sequencing risk sharper, not softer. The Texas Tribune reports Gov. Greg Abbott on Sept. 21 directed TCEQ Executive Director Kelly Keel to halt new permits sought by data centers until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board finish audits covering electricity use, water use, community impact, ownership, and tax breaks. TechTarget’s Sep. 24 follow-up quotes TCEQ’s Ryan Vise confirming the agency has paused air and water permits and other authorizations for infrastructure directly supporting data centers, will fold ERCOT findings into its evaluations, and still owes Abbott a compliance update by Oct. 19; developer-facing dates now include Oct. 12 for ERCOT’s State and Community Impact RFI responses and Dec. 10 for ERCOT’s planned report to the PUC. Community Impact and Austin Business Journal had already flagged “behind-the-meter” builds still needing TCEQ air/water authorizations. For CRE around Taylor, Bastrop, Georgetown, Four Points, and Caldwell/Uhland, schedule risk remains live.
