Semiconductors / economic impact · Austin and Taylor

Samsung says its Austin and Taylor campuses put $10.9 billion into the Central Texas economy in 2025, with the Taylor fab expected to be operational by the end of 2026

Samsung Austin Semiconductor · Oct 7, 2026 · plus Business Wire · Oct 9 · Austin American-Statesman · Oct 9

A quieter weekend in the headlines gave me a chance to step back and look at the anchor behind so much of the activity I’ve been tracking out east. In its Oct. 7 release on a third-party 2025 Economic Impact Report, Samsung Austin Semiconductor said its northeast Austin fabs and the Taylor campus injected a total of $10.9 billion into the Central Texas economy last year, supported more than 28,000 jobs, paid $2 billion in wages, and generated $218.2 million in annual revenue for local taxing districts. The company says significant Taylor operations began in Q4 2025 when its office building opened to hundreds of employees, that it aims to establish the Taylor fab using EUV technology to produce 2nm chips with the site expected to be operational by the end of 2026, and that it plans to invest more than $37 billion in the region in the coming years. Put that next to this week’s approval of the 664-acre Big Watt–PowerHouse Taylor Technology Campus, which its developers say could reach up to one gigawatt with first power targeted for the first half of 2028, and the US 79 corridor is stacking up long-term demand for industrial, supplier, and workforce housing space.

Multifamily pipeline + trades · North and South Austin

Permits issued for The Kent, a 396-unit, seven-story project on Kramer Lane near The Domain, as Oak Hill Highline Phase II starts in South Austin and TR3 Capital buys Villas Del Sol

Groundbreak · Oct 11, 2026 · Groundbreak · Oct 10 · TR3 Capital via Biswire · Oct 9

Three apartment updates from the last couple of days. Groundbreak reports that building permits have been issued for The Kent, a 396-unit, seven-story, roughly 381,000-square-foot building on 2.9 acres at 2631 Kramer Lane in North Austin. HPI Real Estate Services is developing with Belshaw Mulholland Architects; construction started Aug. 18, with completion slated for mid-2029. Groundbreak also reports that Phase II of Oak Hill Highline, also known as the Coop at Southpark Meadows, is under construction at 1802 Oak Hill Lane: 132 units in a five-story building with a $26 million TDLR estimate, part of a 360-unit development in which half the units are income-restricted, with construction running through September 2028. On the trade side, Austin-based TR3 Capital announced in an Oct. 9 Biswire release that it acquired Villas Del Sol, a 294-unit community built in 1983, through TR3 Capital Fund V, calling it the largest acquisition in the firm’s history; the release did not disclose a price. A sponsor that sat out the 3% cap rate years stepping in now is one more sign that buyers see better entry points in Austin multifamily.

Retail / entitlements · Lakeway

Lakeway approves PUD rezoning for 30.94 acres of Lakeway Town Center expansion, with Phase 3 businesses targeted to open in mid-2028

Groundbreak · Oct 10, 2026

A west-side retail item. Groundbreak reports that Lakeway City Council unanimously approved PUD rezoning on Sept. 21 for Phases 3 and 4 of the Lakeway Town Center expansion on Lohmans Crossing Road, developed by Tom DeCicco of Lakeway Plaza Partners. The rezoning covers 30.94 acres across 14 privately owned parcels, moving them from C-1 and C-2 to PUD for restaurants, retail, and drive-throughs in buildings up to 45 feet tall. Phases 1 and 2, roughly 15 acres, are already complete with Juiceland, Starbucks, CVS, and PNC Bank. Council capped drive-throughs at four across the two new phases, required a special-use permit for any hotel or brewery, and prohibited outdoor live music, and an updated traffic impact analysis must come before the Phase 3 site plan. Nearby Yaupon Creek HOA residents opposed the PUD. Phase 3 businesses are targeted for mid-2028, with Phase 4 and project completion expected by mid-2029. For retail tenants looking at the Lake Travis trade area, that’s a defined window for new pad and inline space.

Infrastructure + advanced manufacturing · Hutto–Taylor corridor

East WilCo Highway Segment 2 opens between Hutto and Taylor months ahead of schedule, the same week Blue Origin picked Hutto for its ~1.3 million SF Constellation Park

Community Impact · Oct 9, 2026 · plus Community Impact · Oct 8 · KUT · Oct 8 (Blue Origin)

Roads follow jobs, and last week eastern Williamson County got both. Community Impact’s Jillian Nachtigal reports that the county held an Oct. 8 ribbon-cutting for East WilCo Highway Segment 2, which runs from CR 137 in Hutto to CR 404 in Taylor with one travel lane each way plus a center turn lane. The $53.1 million segment, funded by the 2023 voter-approved road bond, was finished months ahead of schedule, and East WilCo Highway now connects SH 130 in Pflugerville to Chandler Road in Taylor; at full buildout it will reach I-35 in Jarrell. The county also completed the $7.1 million Southeast Loop connection, adding 1.65 miles of new roads and bridges. That lands right after Blue Origin’s Constellation Park announcement, which I covered earlier this week: Community Impact reported the company will occupy roughly 1.3 million square feet in the existing industrial park along Hwy. 79 between Innovation Boulevard and Alliance Boulevard, with a nearly $555 million capital investment and more than 2,000 jobs that KUT says are expected over the next 10 years. New east–west capacity is exactly what industrial users and their suppliers look for along this corridor.

Industrial leasing / manufacturing · Georgetown

Solar parts maker Protec PV leases 82,000 SF at Berry Creek Business Park in Georgetown and plans to hire 200 by April

Austin Business Journal · Oct 9, 2026

Another occupier for Berry Creek Business Park, the same Georgetown park where TSS recently took about 380,000 square feet. The Austin Business Journal’s Justin Sayers reports that solar parts manufacturer Protec PV Inc. has leased 82,000 square feet at Berry Creek and plans to hire 200 employees by April. ABJ’s angle is that demand for the company is being driven not just by data centers but by pretty much anything that requires power. Protec’s own site says the company provides encapsulants to the solar industry. For Georgetown’s industrial owners, a mid-size manufacturing lease with a fast hiring ramp is a healthy signal that the I-35 north submarket is drawing more than distribution users.

Institutional / medical · North Austin

UT wants to build another tower in North Austin as part of its AI-native UT Dell Medical Center

Austin Business Journal · Oct 9, 2026 · background from Community Impact · Aug 13, 2026

The Austin Business Journal’s Sean Hemmersmeier reports that the University of Texas plans to build an AI-native medical center in North Austin featuring a new tower designed to symbolize research and innovation, with UT President Jim Davis saying the school is focused on bringing innovative ideas to the build-out. For scale, Community Impact reported in August that the UT System Board of Regents added Phase I of the UT Dell Medical Center to its capital improvement program: a 27-acre complex with a hospital tower, outpatient facilities, an emergency department, and 300–500 beds, in collaboration with UT MD Anderson, inside the 300-acre UT Dell Campus for Advanced Research off MoPac. Phase I is expected to cost up to $1 billion of a roughly $5 billion total, with completion targeted for December 2030. A project of that size is a long-term demand driver for medical office, hospitality, and housing in the MoPac/Parmer area.

Retail / mixed use · Leander

Northline adds The Market District, a 30,000 SF retail and dining hub in Leander leaning toward Asian businesses, targeted for Q1 2028

CultureMap Austin · Oct 9, 2026

Close to home for me in Leander. CultureMap’s Brianna Caleri reports that Northline, the mixed-use development around Leander Station, is adding The Market District, 30,000 square feet of retail and dining space west of The Shops at Northline, with a tenant mix expected to “lean toward Asian businesses.” The developer’s release points to Samsung’s $42 billion semiconductor plant in Taylor as the anchor for a broader wave of Korean investment across Williamson County, and Northline Leander Development Company founder Alex Tynberg said the goal is to bring retailers, restaurants, and services not offered anywhere else in the area. No leases have been announced; Partners Real Estate is leading tenant recruitment for this first phase, and The Shops at Northline, anchored by Sprouts Farmers Market, is set to open later in 2026. Northline’s plan also includes Leander’s new City Hall, a town square, a boutique hotel, and a library, cultural center, and museum. Opening is expected in the first quarter of 2028.

Market data · Office and industrial, Q3 2026

Still my reference point this week: CBRE’s Q3 numbers show office vacancy at 23.6% after Waterline delivered, while industrial leasing tops 9.77 million SF year to date

CBRE Research · Oct 8, 2026

I’m keeping CBRE’s Austin Q3 2026 figures on the page through the weekend because they frame almost everything above. On office, net absorption was 253,000 square feet (577,000 SF year to date), but vacancy rose 40 basis points to 23.6% and availability rose to 28.5%, driven by Waterline Tower delivering downtown with more than 700,000 SF of vacant Class A space; CBRE says that excluding that building conditions were steady, the pipeline is at its lowest level in more than 10 years, and tenant demand reached 5.1 million SF, up 45% year over year. On industrial, net absorption reached 1.22 million SF against 918,000 SF of deliveries, year-to-date leasing hit 9.77 million SF, already above full-year 2024 and 2025 totals, and average asking rents fell 2.1% year over year. Leases like Protec PV’s in Georgetown are part of that industrial leasing story.
