Data centers / annexation · Taylor / Williamson County

Taylor votes tomorrow on the 664-acre Big Watt–PowerHouse campus, and the city frames annexation as the only way to hold it to city rules

The Texas Dispatch · Oct 6, 2026 · also Bisnow · Oct 5 · KUT · Oct 2 · City of Taylor · Oct 2

The vote I’ve been flagging all week is tomorrow, Thursday, Oct. 8. The Texas Dispatch’s Oct. 6 explainer lays out what’s actually at stake for the Taylor Technology Campus (formerly Project Mustang) between FM 112 and US 79: because Texas cities can’t regulate land use in their extraterritorial jurisdiction, the city says annexation is how it can enforce limits on noise, lighting, water, and setbacks. Under the proposed agreement, sound would be capped at the greater of 65 decibels or pre-construction levels (versus the state’s 85-decibel limit otherwise), cooling would be a closed loop filled once with 1.575 million gallons of reclaimed water, buildings would sit at least 300 feet from the north, east, and west lines, and power and battery facilities would stay 700 feet from the south line behind berms. The developers’ consultant projects $1.38 billion in gross tax revenue to Taylor over 20 years and a $4.8 billion regional impact, with roughly $28 million in community benefits and no tax abatement requested, figures the outlet notes haven’t been independently checked. Bisnow reported Oct. 5 that the campus is planned for 500 MW with on-site power and that the developers don’t own the site yet; the Taylor Press reports they’ve indicated they could build with or without the city. Whichever way council goes, this sets the template for large-acreage power sites in east Williamson County.

Industrial leasing · Georgetown / Williamson County

TSS takes about 380,000 SF at Fidelis’ Berry Creek Business Park, bringing the Georgetown park to 100% leased

TSS, Inc. via Newswire · Oct 6, 2026 · also citybiz · Oct 6

Here’s a real AI-supply-chain lease on the I-35 north corridor. Data center services company TSS, Inc. (Nasdaq: TSSI) announced Oct. 6 a seven-year lease for roughly 380,000 square feet across Buildings 1 and 3 at Berry Creek Business Park in Georgetown, developed by Fidelis and less than a mile from TSS’s existing rack-integration facility. TSS expects to occupy the first building this month and the second once buildout is finished in 2027; it also operates about 97,000 square feet of warehouse space in Round Rock today. Fidelis Industrial & BTS president and CEO Kevin ONeil said the TSS lease makes the park 100% leased. Financial terms weren’t disclosed, as citybiz notes. For context, Berry Creek is the 521,000-square-foot Fidelis project at 3000 N. I-35 where USA Packaging signed earlier this year, and it’s another sign that Georgetown/Round Rock is absorbing the spec big-box space built a few years ago.

Data centers / industrial land · Hutto and the TDLR pipeline

Skybox Hutto 4 tops this week’s state filings at $550 million, while Skybox waits until 2028 to learn its power allocation next door

Community Impact · Oct 6, 2026 · also CRE Daily · Oct 6 · The Real Deal Texas · Oct 5

Hutto stays on my radar from two angles. Community Impact’s Oct. 6 roundup of the most expensive Austin-metro filings with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation is led by Skybox Hutto 4 at 3399 County Road 132: a one-story, 844,300-square-foot core-and-shell building with office and a future data center use, estimated at $550 million, with a filed timeline of Jan. 20, 2027 to Oct. 30, 2028. The same list includes a 55,000-square-foot Dick’s Sporting Goods on Burleson Lane in Bastrop ($11 million) and a Gringo’s Tex-Mex on I-35 in Kyle. Meanwhile, CRE Daily’s Oct. 6 brief (following The Real Deal and Austin Business Journal) recaps that Skybox won rezoning for its separate 140-acre Project Sequel, plans to close in February, and is pairing with Prologis on an industrial build-to-suit plan, because under the state’s current grid-connection process it won’t know its power capacity until 2028. CRE Daily’s takeaway matches mine: power, not land or zoning, is now the gating item for data centers here.

Mixed-use / hospitality entitlements · East Riverside

Mill Creek’s 1404 East Riverside PUD is approved: 1.3 million SF, 180-foot towers, 381 units, and 513 hotel rooms

Groundbreak Austin · Oct 6, 2026

A big one on the south shore cleared its entitlement hurdle. Groundbreak Austin reports Oct. 6 that Mill Creek Residential’s 1404 East Riverside PUD, at 1400½ through 1408 East Riverside Drive, has been approved. The Ziegler Cooper Architects design totals 1.3 million square feet in towers up to 180 feet, with 381 residential units (15 affordable), 513 hotel rooms, and 1,300 parking spaces. The project dedicates 1.17 acres of public parkland with direct access to the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail and is targeting Austin Energy Green Building 3-Star; it now moves to permitting and construction. A hotel count that size on Riverside tells me developers still see room for lodging and lakefront mixed use just across from downtown.

Mixed-use / retail podium · South Congress

Intracorp starts construction on Leland South Congress after landing a $173 million construction loan

Austin Business Journal · Oct 6, 2026 · also Urbanize Austin · Oct 2024

South Congress gets another vertical start. Austin Business Journal reports Oct. 6 that Intracorp has begun construction on its long-gestating condo project along South Congress Avenue after securing a $173 million construction loan; the full story is behind ABJ’s paywall. Urbanize Austin’s October 2024 coverage of the ceremonial groundbreaking describes the Leland South Congress at 2121 S. Congress as a seven-story building with about 270 condos and 22,000 square feet of ground-floor retail and restaurant space anchored by a 6,000-square-foot outdoor dining paseo, with Shop Companies handling retail leasing. Construction financing at that scale for for-sale product is a meaningful vote of confidence, and the ground-floor retail is worth watching for restaurant and boutique users wanting a SoCo address.

Multifamily capital markets and pipeline · South and Southwest Austin, San Marcos

ORIX buys Strata on Bluff Springs Road, OHT breaks ground on The Barton off SH 71, and a 1,460-unit San Marcos plan goes on hold

Multi-Housing News · Oct 6, 2026 · also Real Estate Trail via Connect CRE · Oct 6 · Groundbreak Austin · Oct 6

Three multifamily data points from one day. Multi-Housing News reports Oct. 6, citing Yardi Matrix, that ORIX USA bought Strata, a 322-unit community at 8340 Bluff Springs Road less than a mile from I-35, from Maverick Development Group, with a $42 million Franklin BSP Realty Trust acquisition loan maturing in 2031. Real Estate Trail, summarizing Connect CRE, says OHT Partners broke ground on The Barton, a 402-unit garden and townhome community on more than 60 acres at 8956 W. State Highway 71. And Groundbreak Austin reports Oct. 6 that American Ventures’ District at San Marcos, a 1,460-unit mixed-use plan with a 130-room hotel, grocery, retail, and office at E. McCarty Lane and Rattler Road, has been placed on hold with no revised timeline. Capital is still trading and building here, but selectively.

Entitlements / permitting · City of Austin

Austin’s city manager gives Development Services the final say when departments clash over development rules

Austin Free Press · Oct 5, 2026

This one matters to every site plan in the city. Austin Free Press’ Chad Swiatecki reports Oct. 5 that a Sept. 25 memo from City Manager T.C. Broadnax names Development Services Director Keith Mars as the final department-level authority when departments disagree over how development rules apply, and says some review functions now handled by Watershed Protection and Parks and Recreation will move to Development Services. Mars told the outlet a new Executive Permitting Committee of department leaders will handle conflicts and that specialists will still provide technical analysis; the memo doesn’t yet spell out which functions shift or when. Mayor Pro Tem Chito Vela called it a needed fix for interdepartmental gridlock, while Council Member Marc Duchen said it surprised him and raised concerns about losing specialist expertise. For owners and developers, a single arbiter could shorten the delays that kill deal timelines, so I’ll be watching how it’s implemented.

Retail / grocery anchors · San Marcos and Buda

H-E-B’s 114,000 SF San Marcos store opens this morning as grocery-anchored retail keeps marching down I-35

MySA · Oct 5, 2026 · also MySA · Oct 4

The southern I-35 corridor gets its grocery opening today. MySA’s Nicholas Hernandez reported Oct. 5 that H-E-B’s newest San Marcos store, a 114,000-square-foot location at I-35 and McCarty Lane (1074 E. McCarty Lane), opens at 6 a.m. Wednesday, Oct. 7. It’s the third H-E-B in San Marcos and includes an H-E-B Fresh Bites with fuel and a car wash plus the city’s first True Texas BBQ. That follows MySA’s Oct. 4 report on H-E-B’s nearly $61.2 million store planned on a former landfill site at 15300 S. I-35 in Buda. It’s an interesting contrast with the District at San Marcos going on hold just down McCarty Lane: the grocery anchor is open, and shadow-anchored pad and inline demand usually follows.
