Data centers / state permitting · Statewide, including Bastrop County

TCEQ’s Oct. 19 letter to the governor is now public: the agency says it has paused data-center permits and is suspending some claimed authorizations — while watchdogs and Community Impact point to stormwater coverage still listed as active

TCEQ · Oct 19, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 20, 2026 · FracTracker Alliance / Public Citizen · Oct 2026

Yesterday I noted I couldn’t find TCEQ’s update to Gov. Abbott; it’s now posted. In the Oct. 19 letter, TCEQ says it “has paused the issuance of all permits related to the construction or development of infrastructure directly supporting data centers,” that staff are screening pending applications and pausing those subject to the directive, and that it is screening claimed authorizations that don’t require an affirmative permit and, “where possible,” suspending them for data-center activities. It also says it has met with TWDB, the PUC and ERCOT to align directives. The letter gives no restart date. Community Impact’s Oct. 20 story puts that next to the FracTracker/Public Citizen findings that, as of Oct. 16, TCEQ had moved at least 35 data-center-related applications forward, and reports that construction stormwater permits for projects in the report — including EdgeConneX’s second campus in Bastrop County — were still listed as “active” at press time. The governor’s press secretary told Community Impact that “no permits have been approved,” and a TCEQ spokesperson said the commission “has not issued any permits related to data centers.” Community Impact also reports that applications tied to the $366 million Pacifico Energy/BlackChamber data center filed Oct. 7 in Cedar Creek had not been approved or advanced as of Oct. 20. For anyone underwriting land or power-adjacent sites east of Austin, the freeze is now official in writing, with no end date beyond ERCOT finishing its audit.

Industrial / advanced manufacturing · Dog’s Head, Southeast Austin

Amazon files a $100 million, 1.2 million-square-foot robotics plant at Dog’s Head, with construction slated to start in November

Austin American-Statesman · Oct 20, 2026 · The American Developer · Oct 20, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 20, 2026

The biggest new filing of the week. The Statesman reports that a Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation filing made Monday describes a 1.2 million-square-foot “core and shell” building at 475 Patton Ave. housing office and manufacturing operations plus a utility plant, at an estimated $100 million, with construction beginning in mid-November and finishing by mid-July; The American Developer lists the start as Nov. 16. Amazon has said the robotics plant would be operational by late 2027 and could create as many as 500 manufacturing and engineering jobs. It’s the first official movement at Dog’s Head — the nearly 2,650-acre former sand-and-gravel site near the airport that Endeavor is redeveloping — since environmental groups and residents sued in September over the tax increment reinvestment zone Council approved in July, the Statesman notes, and Travis County votes on joining the financing district have stalled. Put this beside the airport’s utility work and the warehouse filings at The Row, and Southeast Austin is clearly where the metro’s next large industrial chapter is being written.

Capital markets · Austin-based sponsor, Texas focus

Endeavor Real Estate Group closes a $210 million value-add fund aimed at more than $3 billion in total capitalization

Connect CRE · Oct 20, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 20, 2026

Fresh equity from one of Austin’s most active developers. Connect CRE reports that Endeavor closed a $210 million private equity fund with a particular focus on Texas, plus Sun Belt markets such as Nashville, Charlotte and Salt Lake City. With anticipated leverage and co-investment, the fund is expected to support more than $3.0 billion in aggregate capitalization, subject to market conditions. Investors include national insurance firms, a large Texas-based endowment and other private investors, and the strategy is value-add across multifamily, retail, student housing, hospitality, industrial and mixed-use, with 30 to 40 investments planned. In a market where many owners are capital-constrained, a local sponsor with dry powder is a buyer to keep in mind.

Office / market conditions · Downtown, Domain and West Central

A local brokerage says Austin Class A office is tilting toward landlords: rates up about 10% in a year and just 227,707 square feet under construction

ECR · Oct 20, 2026

A useful counterpoint to the “office is oversupplied” narrative, from ECR’s research team. They report Class A asking rates rose from $36.01 to $39.61 per square foot over the past year, Class A sublease space fell about 40%, and Class A vacancy is 4.2% in the Domain and 3.4% in West Central. With Waterline delivered, only 227,707 square feet of office remains under construction, down from roughly 2 million a year ago, and Endeavor’s 5th & Walsh — more than 55% preleased — is reportedly quoting in the $80s per square foot. The caveats matter too: downtown Class A vacancy is still 23.6%, and roughly 6.1 million square feet of Class B sits vacant metro-wide at average asking rents of $24.93. My takeaway for tenants with Class A needs in the next 12 to 24 months is the same as ECR’s: start early.

Industrial / spec development · Cedar Park, Williamson County

Scannell Properties moves toward a groundbreaking on a 161,849-square-foot light-industrial building in Cedar Park

Austin Business Journal · Oct 20, 2026 · TDLR filing · registered Sept 3, 2026 · BLDUP · Oct 20, 2026

Closer to my side of the metro. ABJ reports Indianapolis-based Scannell is eyeing a groundbreaking on its Cedar Park industrial project after buying land in July. The state filing describes Building 1 on Little Elm Trail as a 161,849-square-foot light-shell building at an estimated $12 million, privately funded, with no tenant assigned and a listed Nov. 1, 2026 start. BLDUP adds that it sits on a 16-acre site with 52 dock-high doors, 335 parking spaces and 32-foot clear height, though it says groundbreaking is planned for 2027 — so I’d treat the exact start as unsettled. Smaller-bay, dock-high product in the northwest suburbs has been scarce, so this is one I’ll be watching for leasing.

Healthcare real estate · Lake Travis, Cedar Park and Round Rock

Ascension Seton agrees to buy Family Hospital at Lake Travis and two urgent-care centers in Cedar Park and Round Rock

Becker’s Hospital Review · Oct 19, 2026 · Connect CRE · Oct 19, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 5, 2026

Medical real estate following suburban growth. Becker’s reports that Ascension Seton signed a definitive agreement to acquire Family Hospital at Lake Travis, which provides 24/7 emergency and inpatient care, plus Family Medical Center at Lakeline and Family Medical Center at Round Rock from Cedar Park-based Family Hospital Systems. Terms weren’t disclosed, and the deal is expected to close later this year subject to customary conditions — so it’s pending, not done. Ascension Texas’ Jamie Youssef said in the release that Austin’s suburbs “are among the fastest-growing communities in the nation,” and that health services need to expand to match. Health systems planting flags in Lakeline, Round Rock and Lake Travis tend to pull medical office and service retail with them.

Economic development / semiconductors · Round Rock

Round Rock Council votes Oct. 22 on a $600,000 incentive for PulseForge’s $20 million semiconductor-tool expansion

Community Impact · Oct 20, 2026

A smaller deal, but a telling one for Round Rock’s flex and light-industrial market. Community Impact reports PulseForge, which makes specialized tools for semiconductor chip packaging at 400 Parker Drive, would commit $20 million — $6 million to expand its facility and $14 million in business personal property — and add 60 jobs. In return it would receive $600,000 in Type B sales-tax funds: $250,000 in 2030 after $10 million invested and 20 jobs, and $350,000 in 2032 after the full investment and the remaining 40 jobs. The chamber says PulseForge has identified a 20,000-square-foot space next to its existing facility but is also helping it look at other properties if it wants to build elsewhere to scale. Council meets at 6 p.m. Thursday.
