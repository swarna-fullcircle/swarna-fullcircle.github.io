Data centers / permitting · Hays County & statewide

Answered: Hays County held its development-rules hearing but took no action, with adoption possible Nov. 10. And TCEQ now says STEERS should be back in mid-November

Hays County Commissioners Court meeting record (OpenCity) · Oct 27, 2026 · TCEQ · checked Oct 29, 2026 · The Texas Dispatch · Oct 21, 2026 · The Texas Tribune · Oct 21, 2026

I’ve been waiting all week on two open questions, and both have answers this morning. First, Hays County. According to the posted record and transcript of Tuesday’s Commissioners Court meeting, the court held the required 11 a.m. public hearing on its rewritten Development Regulations but took no action, as it had committed to do. Residents criticized the removal of the minimum lot-size chart and called the water-availability standards weak. County staff responded that the lot-size rules remain in the platting and septic regulations, and said the court could adopt the new rules as soon as its Nov. 10 session or on Dec. 1. Staff also described a plan to adopt the regulations first and then enter agreements with groundwater conservation districts on groundwater-availability study standards, possibly by January or February in the worst case. The draft lists a Jan. 1, 2027 effective date. As a reminder, The Texas Dispatch summarized the draft as tying county incentives for “high-intensity uses” (100,000 gallons of water a day, 20 megawatts of peak power, or mechanical cooling for data centers and similar uses) to closed-loop or air-cooled systems and on-site generation or storage. Those are conditions for incentives, not bans. Second, the state. TCEQ’s STEERS e-filing portal is still offline, but the agency’s notice now says it is reworking the system so staff can review data-center-related applications before anything is authorized, and expects that work to be done in mid-November. In the meantime, TCEQ extended the Multi-Sector General Permit renewal deadline to Dec. 28 and issued an episodic waiver so applicants who can’t wait can email their MSGP, construction general permit and concrete-production filings. My takeaway for owners and developers with any industrial or construction project in the pipeline, data center or not: check whether you need stormwater coverage before mid-November and plan for the manual process.

Office · Downtown & Northwest Austin (Quarry Lake)

Office tells two stories this week: Indeed puts its entire Indeed Tower space up for sublease, while a 98%-leased Quarry Lake building trades a year after its turnaround began

Austin Business Journal · Oct 28, 2026 · JLL · Oct 28, 2026 · ECR · Oct 20, 2026

The Austin Business Journal reports that Indeed is seeking a subtenant for its entire 308,000-square-foot office space at Indeed Tower, the 36-story downtown tower at 200 W. Sixth St. where the company got naming rights when it signed its lease in 2018. That’s a big block hitting a downtown market where ECR still puts Class A vacancy at 23.6%. On the other side of the ledger, JLL Capital Markets closed the sale of Lakeside at Quarry Oaks, a three-story, 123,272-square-foot office building at Braker Lane and Seton Center Parkway, from Brick Row Holdings to DML Capital (JLL calls it Texas-based; ABJ says Houston). JLL also arranged acquisition financing from a life insurance company. It was 98% leased at closing, and ABJ notes it has gone from 35% occupied to 98% leased since Brick Row bought it in summer 2025. JLL points to its proximity to the planned UT Dell Medical Center campus (slated for 2030) and to tenant HeartFlow. No price was disclosed. My read: well-located, well-run suburban product is getting leased and financed, while downtown tenants with more space than they need are still giving some of it back. Tenants who want sublease bargains downtown should be watching this one.

Capital markets · Austin metro

ULI and PwC’s 2027 Emerging Trends moves Austin back up to No. 21, from No. 30, as investors work through our supply

PwC / Urban Land Institute · Oct 27, 2026 · RealtyWire · Oct 27, 2026

The industry’s big annual sentiment survey came out Tuesday. Emerging Trends in Real Estate 2027 ranks Austin 21st for overall real estate prospects. That’s up from 30th in the 2026 edition but still below our 15th-place ranking in 2025. The report says Austin is “contending with the consequences of rapid development” while it keeps the state’s strongest job growth rate and keeps attracting tech talent. Dallas–Fort Worth is No. 1 for the third straight year. Nationally, data centers had the survey’s steepest one-year drop, which the authors tie to power limits, execution constraints and “regulatory friction,” not weaker demand. Industrial and distribution posted the strongest investment score among established property types, while multifamily and single-family ratings declined. For Austin, that lines up with what I’m seeing locally: industrial absorption is improving, apartment supply is still being absorbed, and data-center permitting is in flux. The authors themselves caution that the rankings are directional, so I read 21st as “recovering, not there yet.”

Industrial · Southeast Austin & metro-wide

Amazon’s 1.2 million-square-foot “Project Toaster” robotics plant has foundation permits, plus my condensed Q3 industrial recap

Texas Construction Hub · Oct 28, 2026 · ECR · Oct 27, 2026 · Lee & Associates – Austin · Q3 2026 · Newmark · Q3 2026

New detail on a project already on my calendar: Texas Construction Hub reports that Amazon’s proposed robotics manufacturing building at 475 Patton Ave. in southeast Austin, known as Project Toaster and about 1.2 million square feet, now holds city foundation permits. An Oct. 19 state registration lists a $100 million core-and-shell package designed by WSP USA, with a filed schedule of Nov. 16, 2026 through July 13, 2027, and Amazon projects roughly 300–500 manufacturing and engineering jobs. The site is part of the wider Dog’s Head development, where environmental groups and residents are challenging the development and financing agreements. Carried over, condensed from yesterday: ECR called Q3 Austin industrial’s strongest quarter in at least a year (about 2.6 million SF of net absorption), Lee & Associates had vacancy down to 19.26%, and Newmark reported Tesla’s 538,720-square-foot Mustang Ridge lease as the quarter’s largest, alongside Flex in Georgetown, AI Prefab Solutions in Hutto and Siete in Buda. Rents haven’t turned yet, so occupiers needing 2027 space still have leverage.

Infrastructure & land · Southwest Austin (Oak Hill)

The $677 million Oak Hill Parkway is substantially complete, and Southwest Austin real estate is already positioning around it

Community Impact · Oct 28, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 8, 2026 · OHT Partners · Oct 15, 2026 · REBusinessOnline · Oct 26, 2026

Community Impact reports TxDOT has substantially finished the seven-mile Oak Hill Parkway rebuild after five years of construction. It turned a four-lane undivided road into a six-lane divided highway with frontage roads, 15 miles of shared-use paths and flyovers at “The Y” (Hwy. 71/290), with landscaping, paths and painting wrapping up through November. One honest caveat from the story: some Oak Hill retailers say foot traffic is still below pre-construction levels now that the corridor is more of a thruway. The capital is following the road. OHT Partners began construction Oct. 6 on The Barton, a 402-unit garden-and-townhome community on more than 60 acres at 8956 W. Hwy. 71 with over 40 acres of green space and first units expected in early 2028, and it cites the parkway in its pitch. Carried over: a Trademark Property Co./Cohen & Steers venture bought the 92.5%-leased Oak Hill Plaza (REBusinessOnline; no price disclosed). For retail owners along the corridor, the question now is visibility and access from the new frontage roads.

Mixed-use & land · Manor, Georgetown ETJ, West Campus & Northwest Austin

Manor Town Center reaches financial close with a Nov. 14 groundbreaking target, a 571-unit neighborhood advances near Georgetown, and the Arboretum and West Campus projects keep moving

Business Wire (Hunt Real Estate & Infrastructure) · Oct 27, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 28, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 27, 2026 · CultureMap Austin · Oct 27, 2026

In Manor, the city and P3 partner Hunt Real Estate & Infrastructure announced they reached financial close and issued notice to proceed on Manor Town Center. That covers the 8-plus-acre civic campus, with a new City Hall, council chamber, the city’s first public library and a gathering lawn, inside Shenandoah Development Group’s 84-acre master development. Voters authorized $90.1 million in bonds in 2023. Hensel Phelps is design-builder, and the team is targeting a Nov. 14 groundbreaking for enabling infrastructure. Civic anchors like this are what tend to pull private retail and multifamily into a suburban core. Near Georgetown, the Austin Business Journal reports developers are advancing Serenity Ranch, a mixed-use neighborhood in the city’s extraterritorial jurisdiction with 571 residential units of varying density on the table. I haven’t seen the full unit mix or a timeline yet. Carried over, condensed: Asana Partners plans a new roughly 14,000-square-foot retail and dining building at The Arboretum (construction spring 2027, per CultureMap), and state filings list a 30-story, 300-unit student tower at 2307 Rio Grande St. in West Campus ($97.3 million, Feb. 2027 start). This week’s Community Impact permit list also adds a $25 million, four-story, 48,000-square-foot Grace Covenant Church building at 9431 Jollyville Road.
