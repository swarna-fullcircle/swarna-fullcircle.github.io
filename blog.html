Multifamily entitlement · Central Austin

Council clears Rosedale School rezoning — 435-unit path still blocked by a deed-restriction lawsuit

Community Impact · Sep 25, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Sep 24

Central Austin just got a zoning win that still isn’t a construction calendar. Community Impact reports Austin City Council gave final approval Sept. 24 — after three postponements since May — to rezone the former AISD Rosedale School site at 2117 W. 49th St. from single-family to multifamily, clearing one of two hurdles for OHT Partners’ proposed 435-unit, six-story apartment plan on about 4.62 acres with a 75-foot height limit. Because neighbors filed a valid petition, the case needed a supermajority; Council also adopted a District 7 amendment directing staff to consider street impact fees for traffic-safety work, including at 49th and Burnet. AISD agreed in 2025 to sell the closed campus to OHT for $26 million as the district works a budget shortfall, but Community Impact stresses the sale still depends on AISD winning its Travis County lawsuit against Rosedale homeowners over a 1938 deed restriction — no trial date set yet. Neighbors’ Play Fair With Rosedale group has floated a smaller roughly 150–225-unit alternative, and Council Member Mike Siegel said mediation talks that looked close later fell apart. For me, this is the CRE land-to-delivery read: zoning can move while title risk and deed covenants still hold the shovel.

Multifamily entitlement · Southwest Austin

Stratus wins Circle C Tract 110 rezoning — ~1,000 apartments next to Kiker, traffic fight unresolved

FOX 7 Austin · Sep 24, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · Sep 25

Southwest Austin’s biggest near-term multifamily entitlement just crossed Council. FOX 7 reports City Council approved items tied to Stratus’s Circle C Tract 110 near Kiker Elementary — rezoning land originally framed for office into multifamily for about 1,000 apartment units (including affordable), plus removing a prior requirement that the developer build a South Bay Lane construction extension. Residents and the Protect Circle C Coalition pressed hard on school-zone traffic and a proposed Dahlgreen Avenue access point they say sits roughly 500 feet from the elementary campus; parents want a traffic study now, not later in site permitting. Stratus CEO Beau Armstrong’s statement, carried by FOX 7, counters that of roughly 190 acres the firm is developing only about 16 acres at just over 8% impervious cover, with about 174 acres preserved as permanent open space; he argues residential peak traffic would be lighter than a full office build-out under existing zoning, that the city required a secondary access point, and that a full TIA comes at site development. Austin Business Journal’s Sep. 25 write-up frames the same MoPac-adjacent Circle C rezoning as unlocking a huge multifamily project. For me, this is the MSA housing-pipeline CRE overlay: corridor density is winning votes, but school-access and open-space politics will still price the schedule.

Office investment sales · Downtown Austin

KBS exits 515 Congress — Palmatum / AQUILA / Glendon buy the 268K SF Class A tower

CRE Market Beat · Sep 24, 2026

Downtown office liquidity is still finding buyers for the right capital plan. CRE Market Beat reports KBS Real Estate Investment Trust III sold 515 Congress — a 26-story Class A tower totaling 267,956 square feet at Congress Avenue and Sixth Street — to a joint venture of Palmatum Ventures, AQUILA, and Glendon Capital Management, with JLL Capital Markets (Mike McDonald, Jonathan Napper, Ryan Stevens, and Drew Fuller) representing the seller. Sale price was not disclosed. During its hold, KBS invested more than $15.5 million in lobby, lounge/conference, and elevator modernization aimed at keeping the tower competitive in the CBD. Paired with the ATP Colorado expansion still absorbing downtown square footage on shorter, cheaper terms, I’m reading this as the investment-sales counterpart: institutional capital will underwrite refreshed CBD product even while tenants still shop hard on concessions.

Office leasing · Downtown Austin

ATP scraps the $47M Congress high-rise path — Colorado expansion is the leaner downtown office play

KUT · Sep 24, 2026 · also Community Impact · Sep 23

Downtown Class A absorption is still happening — just with a shorter leash and a sharper cost story. KUT’s Sep. 24 follow-up reports the Austin Transit Partnership board unanimously approved staying at 203 Colorado St. and expanding next door at 201 Colorado St. in March 2027, authorizing up to $13.5 million for a four-year lease, $6 million for renovations and equipment, and $400,000 to extend the current lease through February. The agency plans about 54,000 square feet as headcount grows from roughly 200 people on site today (only about 64 are ATP employees; the rest are light-rail contractors, consultants, and partner staff) toward about 280. KUT’s rough annualized math puts the new package near $4.88 million a year over four years versus about $5.37 million a year for the earlier 7½-year Class A path at 100 Congress Avenue — about $500,000 less per year, and a clear retreat from what coverage framed as a roughly $47 million relocation package. Community Impact’s Sep. 23 board read still anchors the same deal points and cites ATP’s Alex Gale on co-locating contractors saving up to about $50,000 a month in third-party space. For me, this is still the CRE office read: public-sector leasing is absorbing downtown square footage, but stewardship politics are cutting term and trophy rent, not demand.

Data centers / regulation · Statewide (MSA impact)

Abbott’s TCEQ data-center permit pause gets sharper edges — air/water freeze, Oct. 12–Dec. 10 calendar

Texas Tribune · Sep 21, 2026 · also TechTarget · Sep 24 · Community Impact · Austin Business Journal · Sep 22

Digital-infrastructure entitlement is still on a statewide brake — and the sequencing risk just got clearer. The Texas Tribune reports Gov. Greg Abbott on Sept. 21 directed TCEQ Executive Director Kelly Keel to halt new permits sought by data centers until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board finish audits covering electricity use, water use, community impact, ownership, and tax breaks. TechTarget’s Sep. 24 follow-up quotes TCEQ’s Ryan Vise confirming the agency has paused air and water permits and other authorizations for infrastructure directly supporting data centers, will fold ERCOT findings into its evaluations, and still owes Abbott a compliance update by Oct. 19; developer-facing dates now include Oct. 12 for ERCOT’s State and Community Impact RFI responses and Dec. 10 for ERCOT’s planned report to the PUC — even as TCEQ has not fully clarified how the freeze applies to projects outside ERCOT’s ≥25 MW interconnection review. Community Impact and Austin Business Journal had already flagged “behind-the-meter” builds still needing TCEQ air/water authorizations, and ABJ named a high-profile Bastrop County gas-powered campus Anthropic has been in talks to lease as an early pause read (Axios Austin / The Real Deal earlier tied Anthropic to talks on a planned about 2,842-acre Cedar Creek campus with roughly 710 megawatts of natural-gas generation). For CRE around Taylor, Bastrop, Georgetown, and Four Points, schedule risk remains live — and the next six weeks of audit paperwork may matter as much as any land closing.

Land / mixed-use · Southeast Austin

Lawsuit seeks to void Dog’s Head development agreement and TIRZ financing

Austin Current · Sep 21, 2026 · also KUT · Austin Business Journal

The MSA’s biggest mixed-use land story is still in court, not just Council chamber. Austin Current and KUT report that Save Our Springs Alliance, PODER, and nearby residents sued the City of Austin and City Council seeking to void both the 45-year Dog’s Head development agreement with Endeavor and the Tax Increment Reinvestment Zone financing that would return more than $1 billion in property and sales taxes to help pay for infrastructure over 35 years. Plaintiffs call the financing an “illegal and unconstitutional use of taxes” and argue the agreement unconstitutionally delegates legislative authority; they also say the site does not meet the state’s “unproductive, underdeveloped, or blighted” TIRZ test given its location beside major highways, Austin-Bergstrom, and Tesla’s Gigafactory. City officials have estimated the roughly 2,600-acre Southeast Austin project (Colorado River, US 183, SH 130) could generate as much as $3.5 billion in tax revenue over 30 years; Endeavor’s first announced tenant remains Amazon’s planned robotics facility. A city spokesperson said Austin had not been served as of Monday evening and would respond through the courts. For me, this is still the CRE risk read: even after unanimous Council action, title-to-delivery on mega mixed-use turns on whether the development agreement and TIRZ survive litigation.

Retail / site redevelopment · Buda (Hays County)

H-E-B’s former-landfill Buda store gets hard numbers — ~132.6K SF, ~$61.2M, mid-2028 target

Community Impact · Sep 23, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal

Southern MSA retail just got a construction calendar, not just an incentive headline. Community Impact reports a Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation filing registered Sept. 22 puts H-E-B’s new Buda supermarket — on the long-vacant former landfill along the southbound I-35 access road north of Main Street — at 132,563 square feet and about $61.25 million in construction cost, with crews expected to start May 1, 2027, and finish June 12, 2028 (dates that can still shift). The store is still framed as creating at least 50 full-time jobs in its first year, and H-E-B has said it will repurpose at least 60% of the existing Buda store for other retail and sales-tax uses while completing a traffic impact analysis for roadway improvements. Austin Business Journal’s Sep. 23 piece separately flagged that the company could ramp construction next year on the roughly 21-acre landfill conversion after earlier incentive approvals, with opening potentially in 2028. For me, this is the CRE retail read south of Austin: grocery anchors still underwrite difficult sites when city/county partners clear remediation and entitlement risk — and the backfill commitment on the old box matters almost as much as the new store’s SF.
