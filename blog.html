Semiconductors / industrial · Taylor

Samsung says construction on its second Taylor chip plant starts next month, and its first fab is “still on track” to come online by year-end

Austin American-Statesman · Oct 15, 2026 · KVUE · Oct 15 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 15

The biggest headline in East Williamson County this Friday comes from Samsung. At a Thursday event in Taylor, the Austin American-Statesman reports, the company said construction on its second plant will begin next month as demand for chips used in artificial intelligence keeps growing. John Taylor, vice president of infra technology and leader of Samsung’s Austin and Taylor facilities, said the first fab is “still on track” to be online by the end of the year — roughly five years after the original $17 billion announcement. The Statesman notes Samsung has been moving tools into the first building since spring, that Tesla is its first and only officially named customer under a $16.5 billion deal, and that the company plans as many as 10 chip plants on more than 1,200 acres near Taylor. KVUE adds that Samsung Austin Semiconductor announced $22.84 million in local investment, including $10 million for 14 nonprofits through its new Spark! program and $1.5 million for a Skilled Trades Employment Pathway program at Taylor ISD, where the superintendent pointed to high demand for electrical and HVAC workers at the fabs. For my clients, a second fab moving to vertical construction means another multi-year wave of contractor, supplier, and workforce demand — think flex and small-bay industrial, temporary and permanent housing, and service retail along the SH 130 and US 79 corridors.

Data centers / power & water · Bastrop, Travis, Williamson

Bastrop County asks TCEQ for a public meeting on Pacifico’s Cedar Creek gas plant, while the AG sends 156 data-center water letters — including to Travis and Williamson developments

Community Impact · Oct 14, 2026 · CBS Austin · Oct 13 · KVUE · Oct 13 · Office of the Texas Attorney General · Oct 15

The data-center review is now playing out on several fronts at once. On Oct. 13, Bastrop County commissioners unanimously approved a resolution asking TCEQ to hold a public meeting on the natural gas-fired power plant Pacifico Energy proposes to support a 2,842-acre Cedar Creek data center campus, Community Impact reports. CBS Austin, citing TCEQ filings, describes a standard permit application for a 48-turbine facility of roughly 1,300 megawatts that would power two data center buildings; the vote does not guarantee a hearing. A TCEQ spokesperson told KVUE the agency has paused data-center air and water permits at the governor’s direction while ERCOT audits planned projects, and will deliver a full compliance update to the governor by Monday, Oct. 19. Separately, Attorney General Ken Paxton announced Oct. 15 that his office sent letters to 156 data center developments requiring up-to-date water-use information — a list of counties that includes Travis and Williamson. As for the Texas Water Development Board’s Oct. 14 enforcement update, I still have not found it made public as of this morning. My read for land sellers and buyers in Bastrop and East Williamson: state permitting and public-process risk remain the gating items on data-center timelines.

Retail / mixed use · Round Rock

Mark IV Capital breaks ground on a 41,525-square-foot dining and retail phase at The District, across I-35 and SH 45 in Round Rock

Community Impact · Oct 15, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 14

Retail space in the north metro stays tight, and one developer is building into it. Community Impact reports Mark IV Capital broke ground Oct. 15 on a 41,525-square-foot section of The District with six buildings along a Hill Country greenbelt-inspired walkway, able to house nine to 14 restaurants or lifestyle businesses, with completion expected in early 2028. It is going up alongside Origin at The District, the luxury residential and retail project that broke ground in March; the 56-acre development plans more than 1,000 additional residential units beyond the 316 under construction, plus office and hospitality, with total investment expected to exceed $1 billion. Mark IV’s Randall Tuller said in the release that “Metro Austin’s retail market remains among the tightest in the country.” For restaurant and lifestyle operators I work with, this is a pre-leasing window worth watching in a submarket where new quality space has been scarce.

Land / public-private · West Austin and Mueller

Austin and UT near a deal to transfer Muny to the city — and the same Brackenridge amendments set up a regulating plan and TIRZ for UT land, with Council voting Nov. 19

City of Austin · Oct 14, 2026 · The American Developer · Oct 15

This one is about far more than golf. The City of Austin announced Oct. 14 that it is in discussions with UT Austin on an agreement conveying ownership of Lions Municipal Golf Course to the city as part of amendments to the 1989 Brackenridge Development Agreement, which governs how UT may develop its land on either side of Lake Austin Boulevard. Per the release, the amendments would also outline an update to UT’s lease at Mueller, an exchange of city rights-of-way, a proposed Tax Increment Reinvestment Zone, and a mutually agreed regulating plan. Staff will brief the Environmental and Planning Commissions this month and take the amended agreement to City Council on Nov. 19; the regulating plan and TIRZ return for consideration in spring 2027. The American Developer points out that Community Impact has reported possible 65-foot height caps and 200-foot setbacks from Lady Bird Lake, which are not in the city’s release. If you hold or are underwriting land near Lake Austin Boulevard, the spring 2027 regulating plan is where the real development rules will be written.

Industrial & office capital · North and South Austin

A Blackstone-linked seller deeds one Corridor Park building, Athene and SMBC record four Austin loans in a day, and a $7.5 million North Lamar office permit is issued

The American Developer · Oct 15, 2026

A few public-record signals on who is trading and lending. The American Developer reports a warranty deed recorded Oct. 14 in which BRE Jupiter S2 ICON TX Owner LP — the “BRE” naming convention Blackstone Real Estate uses, though the deed doesn’t name Blackstone and it hasn’t confirmed — conveyed the Corridor Park 1B building in North Austin to SP Logistics Austin Owner LLC; no price appears on the deed. A JLL listing describes the six-building, roughly 449,600-square-foot complex at 200 Parker Drive as built in 1996 and about 95% leased at last marketing. On the debt side, Athene Annuity and Life and Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corp. recorded four deeds of trust on Oct. 13, including Athene financings against 11209 Metric Blvd. (TCAD market value $19.9 million) and 210 E. St. Elmo Rd. ($15 million) — appraisal values, not loan amounts. And the city issued a permit Oct. 14 for a new three-story, 13,000-square-foot office building at 4330 N. Lamar Blvd. with a declared job value of $7.5 million, Austin Canyon Corp. as applicant and contractor. Piecemeal dispositions of older industrial plus active insurance-company lending is a combination I’ll keep tracking.

Senior housing / downtown district · Leander

Hayden Grove Northline, a 234-unit, $50 million senior living community, is approved in Leander’s Northline with construction set for Nov. 2

Groundbreak · Oct 15, 2026

Close to home for me: Groundbreak reports Verdot Capital’s Hayden Grove Northline is approved at 461 John Webster St. in Northline, Leander’s roughly 116-acre mixed-use downtown district. The four-story, roughly 324,500-square-foot project, designed by InSite Architects and Planners, will mix independent living, assisted living, and memory care. It is registered with TDLR at an estimated $50 million, with construction set to begin Nov. 2, 2026 and completion expected Nov. 30, 2028; Verdot’s Central Texas pipeline also includes Cedar Park and Pflugerville. Senior housing adds daytime population and steady healthcare and service demand — a good fit for the ground-floor retail and medical office that Northline is trying to fill.

Zoning follow-up · North Lamar

North Lamar / Beaver Street (~545 units): Groundbreak now lists the SOM-designed project as under construction, though the Planning Commission’s Oct. 13 action still isn’t posted

Groundbreak · Oct 15, 2026 · City of Austin Planning Commission · meeting Oct 13

An update with a caveat. Groundbreak reports that construction began Sept. 1 on the 545-unit mixed-use development at 8605–8613 North Lamar Boulevard and 604–706 Beaver Street, with architecture by Skidmore, Owings & Merrill and completion expected by the end of 2030, replacing auto shops on a little over 2 acres with ground-floor commercial along North Lamar. The same story notes the project required the neighborhood plan amendment (NPA-2026-0026.01) and rezoning (C14-2026-0030) with DB90 that the Planning Commission heard Oct. 13 — and the city’s posted backup still reads “action pending,” with no Oct. 13 minutes published yet. So I’m still not reporting a recommendation, and City Council’s date remains TBD. The corridor is proposed for a future light rail extension, which is why I keep this case on the list.
