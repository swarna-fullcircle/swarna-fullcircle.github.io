Williamson & Hays · Data-center policy

Georgetown P&Z unanimously recommends denying the Inner Loop Exchange data center; Council needs 6–1 to approve on Dec. 8

CBS Austin · KVUE · Austin Politics · Nov 17–18, 2026

Here’s the outcome I promised from last night. CBS Austin reports Georgetown’s Planning and Zoning Commission voted unanimously to recommend denial of the rezoning for the proposed data center on about 28 acres near East University Avenue and Northeast Inner Loop, after more than 100 people signed up to speak. Commissioners cited the site’s closeness to neighborhoods and its potential effect on future development; residents raised light and noise concerns. The request would raise the maximum data-center building footprint from 5,000 to 290,000 SF. Developer representative Phillip Niels said the project would use low-level lighting and limit noise, and that if built as planned it “will be the largest taxpayer in Georgetown,” delivering $10 million to $15 million a year in property taxes. It isn’t dead yet: City Council is expected to take it up Dec. 8, and because P&Z recommended denial, approval would take a 6–1 vote. In Kyle, KVUE’s late-night report says council took up Council Member Claudia Zapata’s item to begin the process of defining data centers and prohibiting them in all zoning districts, with residents speaking mostly in support. Any ban would still have to come back for a formal vote, and I haven’t yet seen a clear report of what council decided, so I’ll confirm that next. Tonight at 6, Austin’s Environmental Commission considers a 17-point recommendation for SB Energy’s data center on the former 3M campus on RM 2222, including a 70 MW total power cap and no drinking water for cooling (Austin Politics).

Industrial · Southeast Austin

Kendra Scott is the tenant for 124,000 SF at Burleson Tech, and the week’s biggest state filings point to more industrial

Austin Business Journal · Nov 16, 2026 · Community Impact · Nov 17, 2026

Last week I noted a $10 million warehouse renovation at 7051 Burleson Road carrying a “Kendra Scott Warehouse” label with no tenant assigned. Austin Business Journal has now confirmed it: Kendra Scott is leasing a 124,000 SF warehouse at Holt Lunsford’s Burleson Tech, which will total more than 600,000 SF when complete, and is moving from its current warehouse on East Stassney Lane, with the new facility expected to open next year. Community Impact’s list of the week’s five most expensive TDLR filings in the metro puts that project on a Jan. 8 to June 10, 2027 timeline. The list is led by Project Juniper, a 1 million SF one-story tilt-up warehouse for a confidential client at 11902 FM 969 ($67 million, Nov. 23, 2026 to Dec. 15, 2027), and also includes a $4.5 million Sprouts Farmers Market on East Hwy. 290 in Dripping Springs, a $1.6 million Chipotle at 460 Kauffman Loop in Georgetown and an $800,000 gas station in Elgin.

Land · I-35 corridor

A 308-acre Lockhart site hits the market for a commercial hub, while Kyle Park shows what the corridor can absorb

Austin Business Journal · Nov 17, 2026 · REJournals · Bisnow

Austin Business Journal reports that landowners in Lockhart are looking for a developer to turn a 308-acre parcel into a commercial hub that would add tax base and amenities to one of the region’s fastest-growing cities; ABJ says industrial could be a strong fit, though other uses could work too. For a sense of what’s already working down the corridor: Transwestern Investments and NewQuest have started construction on the retail portion of Kyle Park, about 412,588 SF at I-35 and Bebee Road, with tenants including Dick’s Sporting Goods, Burlington, HomeGoods, TJ Maxx and Barnes & Noble, and the adjacent 328,256 SF Kyle Commerce Center shallow-bay industrial project is due in 2027 (REJournals). Bisnow’s recap of its Central Texas Mega-Metro Summit is a useful reality check: developers there said financing, entitlements and especially water and utility access are making I-35 projects slower and more expensive to get started.

Central Austin · UT real estate

UT unveils a new near-term plan for the former Erwin Center site as Council gets its Muny briefing

Austin Business Journal · Nov 17, 2026 · Austin Politics

Austin Business Journal reports UT has a new plan for the former Frank Erwin Center site at 1701 Red River St., though the long-term vision for the property is still unclear as the university works out how its real estate will take shape over the next five to 25 years. Separately, Council got its Nov. 17 work-session briefing on the amended Brackenridge agreement that would keep Lions Municipal Golf Course as a park while giving UT more development capacity on the surrounding land. Austin Politics lists the ground rules as 65-foot heights, 75% impervious cover and a 200-foot lake setback, and the vote is still set for Dec. 3. Between Erwin, Brackenridge and the medical district, UT is one of the biggest landowners to watch in central Austin.

Retail · Highland Park

Highland Market starts leasing 12 small-shop spaces on Hancock Drive

REBusinessOnline · Nov 17, 2026

I first flagged Ivy Cos. and 35 South Capital’s Hancock Drive purchase in September. REBusinessOnline reports their joint venture is now underway on Highland Market, an 18,900 SF retail redevelopment in Highland Park designed by Austin firm Firmah, with 12 tenant spaces ranging from 874 to 2,900 SF. Leasing has started; no completion date has been announced. Small-format neighborhood retail in established central neighborhoods remains one of the tightest segments I work in.

Earlier this week · Condensed

Tesla on Parmer, office absorption, the Austin Lineage, SCADA tenants and Amazon’s Project Toaster

Austin Business Journal · CBRE · Community Impact · The Real Deal · TDLR · Nov 13–16, 2026

Market reports link Tesla to 82,251 SF of office space at 717 E. Parmer Lane (ABJ, The Registry), and CBRE counts 253,000 SF of positive Q3 office net absorption, with Class A asking rents averaging $58.54 per SF.
