Land / mixed-use · Southeast Austin

Lawsuit seeks to void Dog’s Head development agreement and TIRZ financing

Austin Current · Sep 21, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal

The MSA’s biggest mixed-use land story just moved from Council chamber to court. Austin Current reports that Save Our Springs Alliance, PODER, and nearby residents filed suit against the City of Austin and City Council seeking to void both the 45-year Dog’s Head development agreement with Endeavor and the Tax Increment Reinvestment Zone financing that would return more than $1 billion in property and sales taxes to help pay for infrastructure over 35 years. Plaintiffs call the financing an “illegal and unconstitutional use of taxes” and argue the agreement unconstitutionally delegates legislative authority; they also say the site does not meet the state’s “unproductive, underdeveloped, or blighted” TIRZ test given its location beside major highways, Austin-Bergstrom, and Tesla’s Gigafactory. City officials have estimated the roughly 2,600-acre Southeast Austin project (Colorado River, US 183, SH 130) could generate as much as $3.5 billion in tax revenue over 30 years; Endeavor’s first announced tenant remains Amazon’s planned robotics facility. A city spokesperson said Austin had not been served as of Monday evening and would respond through the courts. For me, this is the CRE risk read: even after unanimous Council action, title-to-delivery on mega mixed-use still turns on whether the development agreement and TIRZ survive litigation.

Data centers / regulation · Statewide (MSA impact)

Abbott orders TCEQ to pause new data-center permits pending ERCOT and water audits

Texas Tribune · Sep 21, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal · governor’s letter

Digital-infrastructure entitlement just got another statewide brake — and that ripples through every Austin-area land play chasing power and water. The Texas Tribune reports Gov. Greg Abbott on Sept. 21 directed TCEQ Executive Director Kelly Keel to halt new permits sought by data centers until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board finish audits covering electricity use, water use, community impact, ownership, and tax breaks. Abbott’s letter (posted by the governor’s office the same day) frames a “whole-of-government” pause so no other state agency moves regulatory approvals until that information is in hand, and asks TCEQ for a compliance update by Oct. 19, 2026. The order builds on his Aug. 3 ERCOT grid-connection moratorium and a TWDB push after as few as 28% of facilities answered a water-usage survey. Tribune coverage notes the move also pressures “behind-the-meter” projects that try to bypass ERCOT interconnection. For CRE around Taylor, Bastrop, Georgetown, and Four Points, I’m reading this as schedule risk on air/water permitting even when local land control looks clearer.

Data centers / adaptive reuse · Northwest Austin

Four Points town hall zeroes in on SoftBank’s former 3M campus — R&D campus or data center?

Community Impact · Sep 18, 2026 · also Austin American-Statesman

Inside city limits, the digital-infrastructure debate still has a very specific address. Community Impact reports dozens of Four Points residents met Sept. 17 with City of Austin officials and an SB Energy representative about the former 3M campus off River Place Boulevard — a Karlin Real Estate “Highpoint 2222” adaptive-reuse play that Travis County records show sold to SB Energy in early 2026. SB Energy’s Jaime Carlson framed Project Cosmos as renovating the site for U.K. semiconductor maker Graphcore’s U.S. engineering headquarters to research, develop, and test power-dense chips, with no zoning change, noise limits honored, closed-loop chillers aimed at holding water use at or below historic 3M levels, and electric capacity capped at 50 megawatts. Residents still pressed on wildfire risk, vibration, and whether the campus stays R&D or becomes a data center — an Aug. 31 SEC filing cited in coverage lists Cosmos as a data center. Separately, the Austin American-Statesman’s earlier SEC-filing read (SE Cosmos LLC; ~107 acres purchased in April) describes nearly $1 billion in financing to convert the roughly 1.1 million-square-foot office campus into an R&D technology center with about 50 megawatts of critical IT load, plus a SoftBank-affiliate lease expected to generate roughly $2.5 billion for data-center and office uses — all while Austin City Council works toward restricting large and hyperscale data centers in city limits. Paired with Abbott’s TCEQ pause, this remains the in-city CRE collision: recycled campus inventory, substation upgrades, water benchmarking, and entitlement politics on one of Northwest Austin’s largest tech sites.

Industrial / rail logistics · Taylor

Partners Real Estate plans TaylorPort 3 — a 573,000 SF rail-served building at RCR

Taylor Press · Sep 18, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal

Northern MSA industrial keeps stacking next to Samsung. Taylor Press reports Partners Real Estate is advancing TaylorPort 3 — a planned 573,000-square-foot building with loading docks along the dedicated rail line at RCR Taylor Logistics Park — still in planning and design, with no construction start date disclosed yet. It would join Partners’ existing TaylorPort inventory on about 58 acres: a 183,000-square-foot rail-served building occupied by Tesla and a 366,000-square-foot building for Compal USA Technology. Managing director Mitchell Hanzik called TaylorPort 3 the largest of the three phases and said the newest building will occupy Partners’ last parcel at RCR, with the firm potentially looking for more Taylor-area land. Austin Business Journal separately highlighted the 573,000-square-foot rail-served product (cross-dock configuration, office pods, truck courts, heavy power) and Compal’s lease next door. For me, this is the supplier-and-logistics CRE read beside Samsung’s Taylor fab: speculative big-box inventory racing to catch institutional tenants before the park’s remaining parcels are gone.

Land · Georgetown

Georgetown’s Yearwood MUD final vote is today — commercial corner on Reagan

Community Impact · Sep 14, 2026 · also Williamson County Sun

The northern MSA land pipeline hits a decision point today. Community Impact reports Georgetown City Council gave initial approval Sept. 8 to establish an in-city municipal utility district for the Yearwood property — roughly 728 acres north of Sun City, west of Ronald Reagan Boulevard and Rattlesnake Road, long operated as a stone quarry. The Yearwood family, Freehold Capital Management, and the city have been negotiating since February. The plan is multiuse: majority residential (duplexes, townhomes, and 45-to-60-foot single-family lots, house count reduced to 1,728) with commercial land planned at the Reagan–Rattlesnake corner. In exchange for MUD consent, dedications include a 140-acre Berry Creek Park, trails, amenity centers, a 7-acre public-safety site, and a 110-foot-wide minor arterial linking Reagan northward. Final approval is slated for today’s Sept. 22 meeting — so I’m watching that commercial pad as the CRE piece of a much larger Georgetown entitlement story.

Data centers / land · Taylor

Texas AG opens investigation into Taylor data center site tied to 1999 parkland deed

KUT · Sep 17, 2026 · also Texas Attorney General · Austin Business Journal

Entitlement risk stays loud on one of Taylor’s contested digital-infrastructure sites — especially beside the new statewide permit pause. KUT and a Sept. 17 Texas Attorney General press release report that Ken Paxton’s office is investigating whether a proposed Blueprint Projects AI data center can proceed on land conveyed in 1999 “to be held in trust for future use as parkland.” Coverage traces the chain: a conveyance to the Texas Parks and Recreation Foundation, later transfers to the City of Taylor and the Taylor Economic Development Corporation, then a 2025 sale of the property to NCP Travis TPP Project, LLC for $10 million. Blueprint’s planned facility is described as about 135,000 square feet with an on-site electricity substation; a residents’ lawsuit was previously dismissed for lack of standing and remains on appeal. Austin Business Journal separately flagged the state investigation on Sept. 18. For me, this is the CRE overlay: title history and charitable-trust questions can freeze or reshape a data-center land play even after local economic-development conveyances.

Industrial / cleanroom · North Austin

Prudential’s North Austin cleanroom ribbon-cutting is Oct. 1 — ~100K SF and 150 jobs

Austin Business Journal · Sep 17, 2026 · also Prudential Overall Supply

Cleanroom demand is translating into bricks-and-mortar expansion inside Austin proper — and the public groundbreaking is nine days out. Austin Business Journal reports Prudential Overall Supply is preparing a roughly 100,000-square-foot North Austin facility — a $40 million to $45 million investment expected to create about 150 jobs — as lab and cleanroom space becomes more common across the market. The company’s own groundbreaking notice points to 1001 West Howard Lane, with a public ribbon-cutting set for Oct. 1, 2026, and a Q1 2028 completion target for a combined cleanroom and industrial laundry plant (its 38th U.S. location and ninth ISO Class 3 cleanroom laundry facility). I’m reading this as the support-ecosystem story behind semiconductor, life-sciences, and advanced manufacturing tenants: when cleanroom inventory grows, the service footprint follows.
