Data centers / power & permitting · Cedar Creek, Bastrop County

Project Longhorn’s first building — a 498,165-square-foot, $366 million data center in Cedar Creek — is approved, two days before TCEQ owes the governor its data-center permit update

Groundbreak · Oct 17, 2026 · Dock Line Magazine · Oct 2026 · Rackline · Oct 2026

The Bastrop County data-center story keeps moving this Saturday. Groundbreak reports that BlackChamber Partners has won approval for Project Longhorn Building One, a one-story, 498,165-square-foot data center at 382 Earl Callahan Road in Cedar Creek, with Gensler as design firm of record. The project was filed with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation on Oct. 7 at an estimated $366 million (roughly $735 per square foot), with construction listed from Nov. 30, 2026 to Jan. 31, 2028. Per Groundbreak, it is the first of two data center buildings planned on the 2,842-acre property, a campus it values at $2.2 billion that includes a grid-independent natural gas power plant; the developers withdrew their Chapter 312 abatement and JETI applications in August, so the project will pay full property taxes, and no tenant is named in the filing. That is the same 2,842-acre campus whose gas plant drew Bastrop County’s request for a TCEQ public meeting earlier this week. The timing matters: TCEQ must update Gov. Abbott’s office by Monday, Oct. 19 on its compliance with his Sept. 21 directive pausing data-center permits, and Rackline notes the letter doesn’t say whether that update will be public; ERCOT’s audit reports are due Dec. 10, with a PUC meeting Dec. 17. A building registration is not the same thing as a state air permit, so for landowners and buyers in East Bastrop and East Williamson, I’m still treating state permitting as the gating item on timelines.

Mixed use / suburban downtowns · Round Rock, Leander, Cedar Park, Hutto, Jarrell

After years of plans, suburban mixed-use hubs are taking shape — and Jarrell is next in line with a 30-year downtown master plan

Austin Business Journal · Oct 16, 2026 · Traded · Oct 16 · City of Jarrell

The Austin Business Journal’s Friday look at suburban mixed-use districts lines up with what I’m seeing in the north metro. Traded’s summary of the coverage: Mark IV Capital’s 66-acre, roughly $1 billion District in Round Rock (planned for more than 1,500 residences, 3 million square feet of office, 150,000 square feet of retail and 300 hotel rooms) has started its restaurant and retail plaza; Leander’s 116-acre Northline is nearing full buildout with about 20 acres left, including a new 30,000-square-foot Partners Real Estate retail project aimed at Asian retailers and restaurants, with a first phase targeting late 2027; RedLeaf’s Bell District in Cedar Park has drawn visitors to its library and trails, but further multifamily and commercial phases have been slowed partly by apartment oversupply; and MA Partners’ 35-acre Co-Op District in Hutto counts ASML as a major office tenant. Separately, ABJ reports Jarrell is planning to revitalize its historic downtown — the city’s Downtown Master Plan, prepared with Kimley-Horn, was slated for Planning and Zoning on Sept. 15 and City Council on Oct. 6, and lays out parks, walkability, street upgrades and business opportunities. With my office in Leander, I see these districts as where small-shop retail, medical office and restaurant tenants will be competing for space over the next few years.

Aerospace & defense / industrial · Cedar Park

Cedar Park’s aerospace and defense cluster grows: Firefly’s bigger cleanroom, a new spaceplane company from Firefly’s founder, and a spaceport campus that could start construction in early 2027

Community Impact · Oct 16, 2026

Community Impact has a useful deep dive on Cedar Park’s aerospace build-out. Firefly Aerospace opened a cleanroom in September that is four times the size of its previous one, to produce more lunar landers and orbital vehicles; founder Tom Markusic is launching Frontera Space, a spaceplane company also headquartered in Cedar Park. The Cedar Park Economic Development Corporation has granted incentive deals to suppliers and manufacturers including Machine Sciences Corporation, Elite Metal Finishing and missile maker Aeon. Most notable for real estate: Chief Economic Development Officer Arthur Jackson said the Central Texas Spaceport Development Corporation, a city–Williamson County partnership, is in talks with developers on sites for a Cedar Park campus that would include a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility, with construction possibly starting as early as the first quarter of 2027. That pairs well with CBRE’s Q3 read showing Cedar Park among the tightest industrial submarkets — specialized flex and light-manufacturing space here should stay in demand.

Market data · Austin MSA office, industrial, retail

Q3 by the numbers: office absorbed 253,000 SF even as Waterline delivered, industrial absorbed 1.22 million SF, and downtown retail vacancy sits under 3%

TenantBase Q3 2026 report (citing CBRE, Marcus & Millichap, Yardi Matrix)

A quick scorecard for clients weighing space decisions this fall. Citing CBRE, TenantBase’s Q3 2026 Austin report puts office vacancy at 23.6% (up 40 basis points quarter over quarter) with 253,000 square feet of positive net absorption in Q3 and 577,000 year to date, direct asking rent at $50.65 per square foot, and just 177,000 square feet under construction — all in the CBD — after the Waterline tower delivered. Submarkets diverge sharply: Southwest at 12.3% vacancy and Round Rock at 14.6%, versus the CBD at 30.1% and East at 40.1%. Industrial vacancy held at 19.3% with 1.22 million square feet absorbed in Q3, asking rents of $11.57 NNN (down 2.1% year over year), 6.67 million square feet under construction, and Cedar Park at 10.9% vacancy. On retail, it cites Marcus & Millichap for CBD vacancy under 3% and investor trading concentrating in Georgetown. My takeaway: tenants have leverage downtown and in big-box industrial, while suburban office and small-bay space in the north metro remain comparatively tight.

Hospitality / investment sales · Southeast Austin

HREC arranges the sale of the 103-key TownePlace Suites Austin South near I-35 and SH 71 to a West Coast buyer, as Austin braces for about 1,600 new hotel rooms

hotelrealtor.biz · Oct 16, 2026

A hotel trade worth noting near the airport. HREC Investment Advisors arranged the sale of the 103-key TownePlace Suites Austin South, opened in 2020 off I-35 and SH 71, to a West Coast buyer expanding its Texas portfolio; HREC’s Mark Rome pointed to Austin-Bergstrom’s planned 32 new gates and the convention center’s reopening as tailwinds. The same story cites Marcus & Millichap’s 2026 Austin hospitality report forecasting about 1,600 new rooms this year, occupancy slipping to 62% (down 170 basis points), and larger declines in South Austin and along I-35, while the new convention center is slated to open in spring 2029. Extended-stay assets near the airport continue to find buyers even as overall supply pressure builds.

Tech / lab & flex leasing · East Austin (Springdale)

Accelsius powers up its Springdale lab for multi-megawatt AI-rack cooling tests and plans to add 18,000 square feet in January 2027

The Data Center Engineer · Oct 16, 2026

A smaller but telling East Austin leasing signal. The Data Center Engineer reports that Accelsius expanded the power capacity at its Springdale lab and facility in Austin to support multi-megawatt, two-phase liquid-cooling testing for next-generation AI racks, and plans to expand its lease at the same location by an additional 18,000 square feet in January 2027. Power-dense lab and flex space is a niche that East Austin landlords with strong electrical service can increasingly market to data-center supply-chain companies.
