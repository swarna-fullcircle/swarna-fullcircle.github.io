Civic & hospitality · Downtown Austin

Convention Center rebuild: JE Dunn now holds the permit for the 373,715 SF Area 1 building

The American Developer · Nov 7, 2026

A quieter Sunday, so I’m leading with the biggest permit on the downtown board. The American Developer reports the City of Austin issued building permit 2026-137243 BP on Oct. 19 for new construction of Area 1 of the Austin Convention Center at 500 E. Third St. — a five-story, 373,715 SF building with JE Dunn Construction as contractor. The declared valuation of about $812.4 million is repeated across four sibling permits, so it’s a project-wide figure, not the cost of this building or a contract price. Area 3 (84,967 SF) was permitted earlier. With Hotel Trinity opening this week a few blocks away, it’s a good reminder that downtown hospitality is positioning for the convention center’s return.

Mixed-use · Downtown & East Sixth

The Grant at 512 Sabine is permitted, and Manifold’s 6th & Walsh nearly doubles in scope

Groundbreak · Nov 7, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Nov 6, 2026

Groundbreak reports Ryan Companies has building permits for The Grant, a seven-story, 232-unit mixed-use project of roughly 260,000 SF on a 1.8-acre site at 512 Sabine St., with street-level retail and restaurant space, a courtyard and coworking. A TDLR filing puts estimated construction cost at $79.5 million; the owner of record is an Empire Square Group entity, and the existing ~84,000 SF early-1980s office buildings are slated for demolition. Separately, ABJ reports Manifold Development’s downtown-adjacent 6th & Walsh project saw its scope nearly double in plans the city ultimately rejected Sept. 2 — the project remains under review. And Project Bolo’s 750–1,200 ft hotel-condo ask on Congress is still in city review, per Our Austin.

Office · Sublease market

Meta and Indeed lead Austin’s largest office sublease blocks

Austin Business Journal · Nov 6, 2026 · Partners Real Estate Q3 report

ABJ rounded up seven sublease availabilities that each exceed 150,000 SF, with Meta and Indeed at the top and the two largest blocks downtown. For tenants, that’s discounted, often furnished space in the submarkets they want most. For context, Partners Real Estate’s Q3 report puts metro office vacancy at 24.0% with 1.7 million SF of vacant sublease space (down from 1.8 million in Q2), and notes xAI’s 112,297 SF sublease move-in at 800 W. Cesar Chavez — proof that big sublease blocks can and do get absorbed.

Industrial · Market data

Savills Q3: Austin industrial posts its best absorption since Q2 2025, led by Georgetown/Round Rock

Savills · Nov 6, 2026 · ECR · Oct 27, 2026

Savills’ new Q3 report has Austin industrial vacancy flat at 16.1% for a third straight quarter, asking rents up 3.4% year over year to $11.23/SF, and 1.5 million SF of net absorption — the strongest quarter since Q2 2025. Georgetown/Round Rock led with nearly 0.8 million SF absorbed and carries the market’s largest construction pipeline at 5.4 million SF. ECR’s Q3 read (different methodology, larger absorption figure) names the tenants behind it: Flex’s 310,000 SF in Georgetown, Tesla taking all of Mustang Ridge Distribution Center I, and Siete’s full 396,000 SF building in Buda. My take: the north corridor is tightening, and rents may follow in 2027 if vacancy keeps easing.

Land & master-planned · Caldwell County

Hillwood clears key Lockhart approvals for a 2,600-plus-home project with commercial

Austin Business Journal · Nov 6, 2026

ABJ reports Hillwood Communities passed a major checkpoint on its Lockhart-area development after two years of discussion, with more than 2,600 homes and significant commercial development on the table. Data centers & capital markets · Week in review

Carrying forward: post-election data-center politics and Texas foreclosure flags under $600M

The Real Deal · Texas Tribune · CRE Daily · Nov 4–6, 2026

Two statewide stories from this week still frame my outlook. TRD notes Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa’s pledge to restrict new data centers, with Texas having edged Virginia as the top market by under-construction capacity; the Texas Tribune reports she has called for a moratorium until the Legislature meets in January. And CRE Daily, citing Roddy’s via TRD, says flagged Texas commercial loans for November auctions fell below $600 million after topping $1 billion in October — 33 properties, including Austin’s 70-unit Velo Flats.
