Follow-up / data centers & water · Statewide

TWDB’s Oct. 14 enforcement update reached the governor but isn’t public yet — and the Texas Tribune shows the law behind the crackdown tops out at a $500 fine

FOX 7 Austin · Oct 14, 2026 · The Texas Tribune · Oct 14, 2026 · Office of the Texas Governor · Sept 21

I promised yesterday to report back on the Texas Water Development Board checkpoint, and here is where it stands this Thursday. An official in the governor’s office told FOX 7 Austin on Wednesday evening that a report has been sent to Gov. Abbott, but it is not known when it will be released publicly — so I have no enforcement numbers to share yet. What we do have is The Texas Tribune’s Oct. 14 analysis of the rules underneath it: records it obtained for 2020 through 2025 identified 334 unique data-center facilities, of which 233 never submitted a requested water-use survey; of 329 facilities surveyed in 2025, about 30% responded, and the 97 that did reported a combined 1.4 billion gallons. Failing to file is a Class C misdemeanor with a maximum $500 fine, the board says it cannot prosecute on its own, and a TCEQ official told the Tribune the law does not allow revoking permits already issued. Abbott’s press secretary told the Tribune that no other state agency will approve data centers until the PUC, ERCOT, and TWDB have the information they need. The next date on my calendar is Monday, Oct. 19, when TCEQ owes the governor its compliance update on the data-center permit pause. For land sellers and brokers working East Williamson and Bastrop County sites, the practical read is that state permitting timelines remain the gating item, not local entitlements.

Healthcare / institutional · North Austin

UT Dell Medical Center is under construction near Braker Lane and MoPac: a $5 billion, roughly 2.5 million-square-foot campus targeting 2030 completion

Groundbreak · Oct 14, 2026

The biggest institutional build in the metro now has dirt moving. Groundbreak reports that UT Austin began construction in September on the UT Dell Medical Center at the former J.J. Pickle Research Campus, southwest of West Braker Lane and MoPac — about 2.5 million gross square feet on 27 acres within the 300-acre UT Dell Campus for Advanced Research, with total cost estimated at $5 billion. The UT System Board of Regents approved Phase 1 on Aug. 12 at up to $1 billion, including $650 million for site development; a $750 million gift from Michael and Susan Dell supports the project. Plans call for a hospital tower, outpatient facilities, and a full-service emergency department with 300 to 500 beds, a 1,000-plus-space garage, and cancer care through a partnership with UT MD Anderson. Skidmore, Owings & Merrill designed it with Shepley Bulfinch, Vaughn Construction holds the hospital contract, and substantial completion is targeted for July 2030. Next to the Domain, that is a multi-year demand driver for medical office, workforce housing, and service retail along the north MoPac corridor.

Multifamily · North Austin and Leander

Morgan opens The Watson, 345 units on Burnet Road, while Haven Housing buys the 346-unit Evergrove at Crystal Village in Leander

Connect CRE · Oct 14 and Oct 13, 2026 · Multifamily & Affordable Housing Business · Oct 12

Two north-metro apartment stories land this week, one a delivery and one a trade. Connect CRE reports that Morgan opened The Watson, a seven-story, 345-unit podium community at 11901 Burnet Road in North Austin, with approximately 45 residences reserved for households earning up to 60% of area median income; initial residents began moving in in June, with full completion anticipated this fall. Closer to my Leander office, Haven Housing — the affordable and multifamily platform of BH Properties — acquired The Evergrove at Crystal Village, a 346-unit garden-style community at 2090 Raider Way at 183A and Crystal Falls Parkway. The 15-acre property was completed in 2024 and was about 92% occupied as of September; the seller and price were not disclosed. A 2024-vintage suburban asset trading at that occupancy tells me buyers are still underwriting Leander rent growth even with new supply coming online.

Land / master plans · Hays County

Kyle’s 7,300-unit Nance-Bradshaw Ranch is approved on 2,695 acres, and Dacy Business Park starts a third small-bay phase

Groundbreak · Oct 14, 2026 · The American Developer · Oct 14 (citing Community Impact, Oct 13)

South of the city, the land story is big. Groundbreak reports Property Reserve Inc. has approval for the Nance-Bradshaw Ranch master-planned community at 3960 Cypress Road in Kyle — 7,300 units on 2,695 acres along the western bank of the Blanco River. A 2016 development agreement allows up to 9,000 living unit equivalents; prior plans called for up to 7,300 single-family homes plus nearly 37 acres of multifamily and 332 acres of commercial, and full buildout could add about 25,000 residents. In September, Kyle approved a settlement ending a six-year Save Our Springs Alliance lawsuit, which removed a proposed $30 million Blanco River bridge; the city’s Water Master Plan proposes a 5.4-mile, $37.6 million water line to the property with construction tentatively set for 2030 to 2038. On the commercial side, The American Developer, citing Community Impact, reports Dacy Business Park at 3300 Dacy Lane began a third phase Oct. 1 — four small-bay office/warehouse buildings totaling 43,050 square feet, completion expected early 2027 — after the first two phases were fully sold and leased.

Pipeline / state filings · Austin, Kyle, Cedar Creek

Out to bid: $135.8 million of Austin-area projects hit the state register, on top of $470 million in TxDOT work on IH 35 and SH 71

The American Developer · Oct 14 and Oct 13, 2026

For contractors and anyone tracking construction labor, the TDLR TABS register keeps filling. The American Developer tallies four filings registered Sept. 30 to Oct. 13 worth about $135.8 million in registrant estimates: UT Austin’s School of Civic Leadership renovation of the Biology Building at 205 W. 24th St. ($62.8 million, 70,000 square feet), Camp Mabry Building 8 for the Texas Military Department ($36 million, 145,000 square feet), the City of Kyle’s Lehman Road Pump Station with a 3.0 million-gallon ground storage tank ($24 million), and a 7,500-square-foot Menchaca Apartments clubhouse listed at $13 million. Separately, TxDOT’s Austin District registered $470.3 million of road work on Sept. 29 — $407.8 million to reconstruct IH 35 main lanes at Manor Road and 32nd Street and related frontage work up to 51st Street, plus a $62.5 million SH 71 overpass in Cedar Creek. These are estimates, not bids or contracts, and none names a general contractor yet.

Zoning follow-up · North Lamar

North Lamar / Beaver Street (~545 units): two days after the Planning Commission hearing, no vote has been posted

City of Austin Planning Commission · meeting Oct 13, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 14

I checked again this morning, and the public record still hasn’t caught up with Tuesday night’s hearing. The staff report for NPA-2026-0026.01 continues to show “October 13, 2026 – (action pending),” and the Oct. 13 minutes are not posted yet, so I’m not reporting an approve, deny, or postpone outcome. The case: 8609 N Lamar LLC, represented by Drenner Group, proposes approximately 545 multifamily units (390 on the North Lamar tracts and 155 on the Beaver Street tract, with DB90 affordability) and ground-floor commercial along North Lamar, replacing auto repair and storage uses on about 2.03 acres; staff supports the related rezoning C14-2026-0030 to CS-V-CO-DB90-NP and LO-DB90-NP. Mixed use / hospitality · Bastrop

Bastrop West — the proposed hotel, convention center, and hospital development — takes a key step forward

Austin Business Journal · Oct 14, 2026 · Community Impact · Sept 30

East of the city, the Austin Business Journal reports that the large hotel and hospital development in Bastrop took a key step forward this week. The full ABJ story is paywalled, so I’m leaning on what I could read directly: Community Impact reported Sept. 30 that Bastrop West is a proposed 400-acre development that could bring a hotel-convention center and a medical complex with a full-service hospital, that a feasibility study recommended a 300-room hotel and a 40,000-square-foot convention center on a 33-acre site, and that a hospital study identified roughly a 97% gap in local health care demand versus available sources. With Project Longhorn and the SH 71 overpass filings also in the Cedar Creek/Bastrop corridor, this is a submarket I’m watching for land and pad-site demand.
