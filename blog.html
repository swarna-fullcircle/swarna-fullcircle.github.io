What I’m watching in CRE

Data centers · Hutto

Colovore files for $280M, ~193K SF AI data center in Hutto

Community Impact · Sep 11, 2026

Digital infrastructure is still choosing the northern industrial parks. Community Impact reports Colovore — a California-based AI data center developer — plans a one-story, 192,694-square-foot facility at 2401 Innovation Boulevard in Hutto, with about $280 million in investment, 40 megawatts of capacity, and liquid cooling. Construction is slated to start Dec. 1, 2026, and finish Dec. 1, 2028, per a Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation filing. The site sits next to land that could host Blue Origin. For me, this is the ETJ power-and-land thesis in real time: AI colocation keeps landing outside Austin’s city limits while the core debates large-load rules.

Retail · Central Austin

Ivy and 35 South Capital buy 2825 Hancock for Highland Market

CultureMap Austin · Sep 10, 2026

Neighborhood retail is getting a fresh Central Austin bet. Ivy Companies and 35 South Capital purchased 2825 Hancock Drive — between Rosedale and Allandale, across from Austin Memorial Park and accessible from MoPac — and plan to renovate it into Highland Market, about 18,900 square feet for roughly 12 tenants spanning retail, local restaurants, and neighborhood services. Local firm Firmah is on design; Ivy already owns Casis Village in Tarrytown. For me, this is the kind of small-box, walkable repositioning I’m watching as capital looks past trophy corridors for everyday retail demand.

Industrial · Manufacturing

Allen Control Systems leases 192,000 SF for North Austin manufacturing

Commercial Real Estate Direct · Sep 9, 2026 · also Austin Business Journal

Defense tech is absorbing industrial space north of the core. Allen Control Systems signed a lease for 192,000 square feet at 15700 Long Vista Drive in North Austin, with plans to rapidly scale production of its Bullfrog autonomous weapon station. Per Austin Business Journal’s coverage, the company also holds an option to expand by another 150,000 square feet, and the owner had repositioned a long-vacant building for advanced manufacturing. I’m reading this as another user-driven industrial lease — the kind that keeps north-corridor product relevant even when speculative absorption is uneven.

Industrial · Taylor

Partners Real Estate breaks ground on 573,000 SF speculative building in Taylor

Austin Business Journal · Sep 9, 2026

The eastern industrial pipeline keeps extending. Partners Real Estate broke ground on a 573,000-square-foot speculative, rail-served industrial building in Taylor — next to the firm’s already-leased Compal USA Technology building — with a cross-dock configuration, office pods, truck courts, ample parking, and heavy power. For me, this is the Samsung-adjacent corridor thesis in motion: large-bay, power-ready product still pencils when rail and manufacturing demand sit nearby.

Industrial · Mustang Ridge

Tesla builds out 538,720 SF Mustang Ridge distribution center

Hoodline · Sep 8, 2026 · citing Austin American-Statesman

South-corridor industrial keeps getting pulled into the Gigafactory orbit. Hoodline, citing the Statesman, reports Tesla plans a roughly $1.44 million build-out of a 538,720-square-foot distribution center it already leases in Mustang Ridge — about 12 miles south of its Austin HQ off U.S. 183 — with construction expected to begin in December. Designed by Clay Development and Construction inside Mustang Ridge Business Park, the project sits alongside supplier Xinquan and follows Tesla’s summer leases that pushed its Greater Austin leased industrial footprint to about 3.4 million square feet (on top of more than 10 million SF of owned manufacturing). Cushman & Wakefield data cited in the piece put Q2 2026 industrial vacancy near 22.3% with nearly 790,000 SF of positive net absorption. I’m watching this as large users still taking move-in-ready product while vacancy gives them negotiating room.

Multifamily · Investment

Knightvest buys 1,027-unit North Austin multifamily portfolio

REJournals · Sep 9, 2026 · also REBusinessOnline Sep 3 / Multifamily Business Sep 8

Capital is still rotating into Austin apartments. Newmark arranged the sale of three Northwest Austin / Round Rock communities totaling 1,027 units — The Enclave at La Frontera (411), Lakeside at La Frontera (366), and Legends Lake Creek (250), known as The Silicon Hills Trio — from San Antonio–based LYND to Dallas-based Knightvest Capital. Built 2001–2004, the assets average about 936 SF per unit. Patton Jones and Andrew Dickson represented the seller; Tip Strickland and David Schwarz arranged buyer financing. This is the kind of mid-vintage corridor trade I’m watching as investors re-enter after the supply overhang.

Data centers · Northwest Austin

SoftBank affiliate details R&D and data-center plans for former 3M campus

Austin American-Statesman · Sep 4, 2026

Inside the city limits, the data-center policy fight and a major campus reuse are colliding. The Statesman reports SE Cosmos LLC — a SoftBank/SB Energy affiliate that bought the 107-acre former 3M campus in northwest Austin in April — outlined plans in a Sept. 1 SEC filing to convert the 1.1 million-square-foot office campus into an R&D technology center with about 50 megawatts of critical IT load. Another SoftBank affiliate, Silver Bands 3 (US) Corp., has leased space there for data center and office uses in a lease expected to generate roughly $2.5 billion. The filing comes as Austin City Council has directed staff to draft a ban on large- and hyperscale data centers in city limits. For CRE clients, I’m watching how power, zoning, and campus repositioning reshape northwest product while AI demand keeps pushing to Hutto, Milam County, and other ETJ sites.
