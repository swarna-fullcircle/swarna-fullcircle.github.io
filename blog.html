Data centers / permitting · Hays County, Caldwell County, Bastrop County & statewide

Hays County’s data-center incentive hearing is tomorrow, TCEQ’s filing portal is still dark, and the Uhland campus shows why county rules matter

The Texas Dispatch · Oct 21, 2026 · TCEQ · checked Oct 26, 2026 · Texas Energy & Power · Oct 25, 2026 · Groundbreak · Oct 25, 2026 · Texas Construction Hub · Sept 29, 2026 · Spectrum News · Oct 23, 2026

Still the item at the top of my week. Tomorrow, Tuesday, Oct. 27 at 11 a.m., Hays County Commissioners Court takes public comment in San Marcos on its 225-page draft Development Regulations. As The Texas Dispatch summarizes it, projects averaging at least 100,000 gallons of water a day (or peaking at 250,000), drawing 20 megawatts or more at peak, or using mechanical cooling for data centers, crypto mining or high-density computing would be “high-intensity uses.” To qualify for county incentives they would need air-cooled, hybrid or closed-loop cooling, and incentives “may be withheld or denied” unless on-site generation or storage covers at least half of peak demand or provides full backup. These are incentive conditions, not bans, and the agenda sets no adoption date. Statewide, as of this morning TCEQ’s “STEERS is temporarily down” page still gives no return date, so e-filings for every applicant — stormwater, air, storage tanks and more — are being routed to staff by email. Texas Energy & Power’s Oct. 25 roundup quotes the governor’s press secretary saying “No permits have been approved,” framing the pause as applying to approvals rather than processing; watchdogs Public Citizen and FracTracker dispute that, and TCEQ told Spectrum News the 17 items it suspended were automated stormwater construction notices, not operational permits. Next door in Caldwell County, Groundbreak’s Oct. 25 update on PowerHouse Data Centers’ campus at FM 2720 and Old Spanish Trail near Uhland reports a capacity of roughly 700 MW on land that left Uhland’s extraterritorial jurisdiction — Texas Construction Hub reported Uhland’s council acknowledged that nearly 900-acre release on Sept. 23 and cautions that PowerHouse hasn’t published a final megawatt total or building count, so I treat 700 MW as reported, not confirmed. The takeaway: once land leaves a city’s ETJ, county incentive terms like the Hays draft are one of the few levers left. In Bastrop County, Cedar Creek residents are still waiting on a TCEQ public meeting about Pacifico Energy’s proposed gas plant and data centers (KVUE). Build schedule slack into any deal that depends on state environmental sign-offs.

Airport / infrastructure · Austin-Bergstrom & COTA

After the “biggest weekend ever,” AUS braces for a near-record Monday — while the utility work behind its expansion breaks ground and Archer pitches air taxis to COTA

KXAN · Oct 25, 2026 · Austin-Bergstrom International Airport · Oct 19, 2026 · Groundbreak · Oct 26, 2026 · CBS Austin · Oct 23, 2026 · Silicon Hills News · Oct 25, 2026 · Hoodline · Oct 24, 2026

KXAN reported Sunday evening that, based on TSA projections, Austin-Bergstrom expects 44,584 passengers today — closing in on the 45,048 record set the day after last year’s U.S. Grand Prix — capping a weekend that stacked F1, Texas football and a Spurs–Rockets game at the Moody Center. That’s the demand case for the build-out. The airport broke ground Oct. 19 on its Utilities Infrastructure Campus-Wide project (designer HDR, prime contractor Sundt Archer Western), which adds aircraft-rated stormwater trunk lines and ponds, redundant medium-voltage power and telecom duct banks to support the new Central Utility Plant, Arrivals & Departures Hall and Concourse B & Tunnel; completion is expected in 2029. That’s the contract Council increased by $224 million on Oct. 22, and Hoodline reported Oct. 24 that the final bridge beam is set for the new midfield taxiways. Separately, Archer Aviation said Oct. 23 it plans an electric air-taxi network linking AUS, Austin Executive Airport and a helipad at Circuit of the Americas. CBS Austin quotes Archer saying first flights could come as early as next year, but no launch date is set and the routes still need regulatory approval. For the airport-area hospitality and industrial submarket, the traffic and the infrastructure are both real; the air taxis are still a proposal.

Office · Metro-wide, Southwest Austin & Zilker

Is Austin office tilting toward landlords? Class A rents up about 10%, a thin pipeline — and Uplands II hits 100% leased

ECR · Oct 20, 2026 · Hoodline · Oct 22, 2026 · Realty News Report · Oct 24, 2026 · Groundbreak · Oct 23, 2026

The market read I keep coming back to this week is ECR’s. The brokerage says Class A asking rents rose from $36.01 to $39.61 per square foot over the past year, Class A sublease space is down about 40%, and with Waterline delivered just 227,707 square feet of office remains under construction, versus roughly 2 million a year ago. Class A vacancy is 4.2% in the Domain and 3.4% in West Central — but downtown Class A vacancy is still 23.6%, and about 6.1 million square feet of Class B sits vacant at an average $24.93. The deals line up with that: Hoodline (citing Realty News Report) reports supplement maker Humann signed 12,745 square feet at Uplands II, 5301 Southwest Parkway, leaving Drawbridge Realty’s building fully leased; and Realty News Report’s Oct. 24 briefs add that JLL represented SolarWinds and Aquila Commercial represented landlord Generational Commercial Properties on the 100,000-square-foot headquarters lease at Zilker Point. Groundbreak confirms 74-story Waterline at 98 Red River is complete. My advice to tenants with 2027–2028 expirations in quality space: start early.

Multifamily · North Austin / The Domain, Burnet Road & Uptown ATX

HPI bets on the Domain with 396-unit The Kent as Austin apartment starts fall 35%; The Waylon opens on Burnet

Community Impact · Oct 20, 2026 · Realty News Report · Oct 24, 2026 · Bisnow · Oct 22, 2026 · Groundbreak · Oct 23–26, 2026

Community Impact details The Kent, a seven-story, 396-unit community HPI is building at 2631 Kramer Lane in North Burnet-Gateway (groundbreaking announced in August), with first units expected late 2028 and full completion early 2029. HPI says it’s investing more than $20 million of its own capital alongside private investors; total cost wasn’t disclosed. The supply backdrop in HPI’s release is the story: Austin Investor Interests data shows 3,300 apartment starts in the first half of 2026, down 35% year over year, and the Austin Apartment Association projects about 13,000 openings in 2026 and 7,000 in 2027. HPI is timing delivery to the UT Dell Medical Center off MoPac (2030). Already delivering nearby: Riverside and KTGY’s 300-unit The Waylon at 7618 Burnet Road, built on a former office surface lot (Realty News Report), and Morgan’s 345-unit The Watson at 11901 Burnet (Groundbreak). Carried over, condensed: Brandywine sold its 57% stake in the 341-unit Solaris House in Uptown ATX in a deal described as $136 million, netting $16.3 million (Bisnow); the buyer is undisclosed. Fewer starts today point to tighter supply around 2028.

Downtown / municipal finance · Georgetown & Williamson County

Georgetown’s downtown plan — a 450-space garage with ground-floor retail and a TML headquarters move — as patio rules get a first reading tomorrow

Community Impact · Oct 25, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 23, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 22, 2026

Carried over, condensed. Community Impact reports Georgetown plans a second downtown garage near East Ninth and South Main with the Texas Municipal League Intergovernmental Risk Pool, which is moving its headquarters from Austin into the former Wesleyan Building: 450 spaces with ground-level retail and dining, about 200 reserved for TML staff on weekdays, construction slated for 2027 and opening in early 2029. A two-year street-patio and sidewalk-cafe pilot has its first reading Tuesday, Oct. 27, with final adoption targeted in November. The counterweight: certified 2026 values fell 1.8% instead of rising a projected 8%, trimming almost $100 million of capacity over three fiscal years and pushing a planned May 2027 bond election back at least a year. Williamson County also approved a $498,700 Freese and Nichols contract for a countywide water infrastructure plan. Good news for Square-area retail; a caution flag for anyone underwriting suburban tax growth.
