Data centers · State policy & local votes

With a new governor coming in, KUT reports that Texas’ welcome mat for data centers may change, and three Austin-area votes land in the next 10 days

KUT / NPR · City of Austin · City of Kyle agenda (via GridWatch) · KLBJ · Community Impact · Nov 17–27, 2026

In a Nov. 27 piece aired on NPR, KUT’s Leigh Walden reports that Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa “has other ideas about data centers.” On CNN, she said data centers have moved into mostly rural communities where “there are no rules and laws to protect Texans.” The story notes that Gov. Abbott signed sales-tax exemptions for large data centers and later imposed a pause on new projects while the state studied their impact. That pause has already expired. John Hall, CEO of the Houston Advanced Research Center, told KUT he expects Hinojosa to impose a moratorium and then new regulations. He also said data centers should have to bring their own water and power and offset their emissions. A K&L Gates lobbyist said some clients are fine with more regulation. Data centers will be on the agenda when the Legislature convenes in January. Locally, three votes are coming up. Dec. 1: Kyle takes up Council Member Claudia Zapata’s tabled item directing staff to bring back an ordinance that would prohibit data centers in all zoning districts. It is direction to staff only, not a ban. Dec. 3: Austin Council considers phase one of its rules. The draft ordinance would require a city license for data centers whose data halls total 100,000 SF or more, and the city couldn’t accept applications until June 1, 2027. Land-use rules come in phase two, which Council is expected to take up by May 2027. Dec. 8: Georgetown Council considers the 68 MW Inner Loop Exchange rezoning, and because P&Z recommended denial, approval needs six of seven votes. My read for landowners: the policy risk on data-center sites just went up at both the state and the city level. If you’re marketing land on a power story, underwrite for a longer, more conditional entitlement path.

Office leasing & redevelopment · Northwest Austin

7700 Parmer signs 103,000 SF with four new tenants while its owner plans 1,800 apartments, a hotel and more office on the parking lots

Austin Business Journal · The Real Deal · CRE Daily · Nov 23–27, 2026

ABJ first reported the leases, and The Real Deal and CRE Daily followed with more on the redevelopment plan. Four new tenants signed a combined 103,000 SF at the roughly 912,000 SF campus. Two are named: Vaco by Highspring and Regus. Owner Accesso Partners described the other two as a multinational tech company and a global professional services firm. Counting existing tenants such as Electronic Arts and eBay, at least 443,000 SF is now leased. Accesso plans to keep all of the existing office and replace the surface lots with garages and new development: 1,800 apartments, a 340-key hotel, 100,000 SF of retail and 800,000 SF of new office, with groundbreaking planned for 2027. TRD reports that the site plan permit had been extended to this month and that Accesso’s request for a five-year extension, filed in June, is still under review. My read: this is what a parking-lot reset looks like in the suburbs. Leasing momentum gives the owner a better story for entitlements and financing, but the extension request is the item to watch.

Industrial · US 290/SH 130 & Georgetown

ZAP approves light-manufacturing zoning for the 792,000 SF Park 290 Logistics Center, and city records tie Link Logistics to Project Juniper

City of Austin ZAP minutes · Groundbreak · Texas Construction Hub · Community Impact · ABJ · Nov 17–26, 2026

One item now has a confirmed result. According to the Nov. 17 Zoning and Platting Commission minutes, ZAP voted 6-0 on consent to approve CH-PDA zoning for Park 290 Logistics Center at 11653 Decker Lane, and staff recommended that version. Groundbreak describes the property as Brookfield’s completed four-building, 792,139 SF Class A complex on 66.27 acres near the US 290/SH 130 interchange. The owner had originally sought industrial zoning, which staff opposed. The case still goes to City Council. Nearby on FM 969, Texas Construction Hub reports that city site plan records list Link Logistics Real Estate as the contact for the owner of the 127-acre tract where Project Juniper is registered: a 1,015,000 SF, $67 million warehouse for a confidential client. The state filing listed a Nov. 23 start, but I haven’t seen confirmation that work has begun or who the tenant is. Carried forward from yesterday: Community Impact reports Georgetown’s advanced manufacturing pipeline at more than $600 million and 3,000 jobs, and ABJ reports Balcones is planning about 700,000 SF of shallow-bay industrial there. My read: East Austin is getting more flexible on use at existing big-box product, which helps lease-up, while new supply keeps moving north and east.

Multifamily · Rents, trades & student housing

Austin rents are still down 2.5% year over year, but Bastrop and Georgetown East are rising, and Villas at Grand Avenue trades for $37.4 million

Yardi Matrix · The Real Deal via CRE Daily · Groundbreak · Nov 23–27, 2026

Yardi Matrix’s November report puts Austin’s average asking rent at $1,514 in September, up 0.1% on a trailing three-month basis but down 2.5% year over year. August occupancy was flat at 92.4%. Drawing on Yardi data reported by The Real Deal, CRE Daily says 16 of Austin’s 44 submarkets posted annual rent gains, led by Bastrop (+4.5%), where SpaceX and Starlink have major manufacturing operations, and Georgetown East (+4.3%). Through September, sales totaled $833 million, the fewest in a decade by that report, at an average of about $182,000 per unit. On the deal side, Groundbreak reports that Dallas-based SPI Advisory paid $37.4 million for the 280-unit, mixed-income Villas at Grand Avenue in Pflugerville, with Freddie Mac acquisition financing. Groundbreak also reports that Subtext bought Skyloft, the 18-story, 212-unit (674-bed) student tower in West Campus, as part of a two-property deal. And UT announced plans for 2,500 new student beds, starting with the 1,075-bed Whitis Avenue hall, which is set to open in fall 2027. My read: rent growth is following jobs. Submarkets with a manufacturing anchor are recovering first.

Retail · Market data & suburban projects

Austin retail vacancy holds at 3.6% even as 1.38 million SF delivers in one quarter, and Lakeway approves the 77,000 SF Bee Creek District

Business News Austin (Matthews data) · Community Impact · Nov 16–27, 2026

Business News Austin, using Matthews’ Q3 data, reports retail vacancy at 3.6%, up from about 3.1% a year earlier. Q3 net absorption was 1.17 million SF against 1.38 million SF of deliveries. Asking rent was $31.79/SF, and 3.22 million SF was still under construction, mostly in suburbs such as Georgetown. Trailing 12-month sales rose 75% to $1.1 billion. Community Impact reports that Lakeway Council approved PUD zoning for the Bee Creek District, a nearly 15-acre, 77,000+ SF mixed-use project on Hwy. 71 with restaurants (including four drive-thrus), retail, office, a gym and an open-air music venue. As a condition of approval, 40% of the commercial space must be built and occupied within three years, and the full buildout must be done within five. My read: vacancy is rising because of new supply, not weak demand. Well-located pad and neighborhood-center space is still leasing.

Corporate moves & civic anchors

Austin drew about one-third of Texas HQ relocations since 2005, while Manor breaks ground on its civic campus and UT’s arena leases move forward

Austin Business Journal · Groundbreak · Community Impact · Nov 19–27, 2026

In a Nov. 27 report based on YTexas data, ABJ says the Austin area attracted about one-third of all corporate headquarters relocations to Texas since 2005, more than twice as many as Houston. ABJ says DFW and Austin together account for 80%. In Manor, Groundbreak reports that Hunt Real Estate & Infrastructure broke ground Nov. 14 on the 66,840 SF Manor Town Center Civic Campus (City Hall and library). It sits on more than 8 acres within an 84-acre master development on US 290, and completion is expected in mid-2028. Carried forward: UT regents authorized ground leases for a roughly 6,000-seat Bluebonnet Hill Arena and student-athlete housing (Community Impact), and Austin agreed to cost-share up to $6.35 million in oversized utilities for the 260-acre Winding Trails project at FM 973 and SH 130 (Texas Construction Hub). My read: a city hall or a big public anchor tends to set the pattern for commercial pads around it, so Manor’s US 290 frontage is one to watch.
