Airport · Permits · East Austin mixed use

Austin’s biggest permit this week is a $60 million, six-gate Concourse M at the airport, and East Austin’s $106 million Right Angle is cleared to build

Morningline Leads · Austin Signals · Groundbreak · Nov 17–23, 2026

The largest building permit the City of Austin issued last week went to Austin-Bergstrom. Austin Signals and Morningline Leads report that the permit for Concourse M at 10113 Maintainers Dr. was issued Nov. 17 with a filed value of $60 million and about 38,224 SF. Morningline notes the $60 million is the estimate filed with the city, not a contract amount. It is a six-gate reliever concourse for narrow-body jets, reached by shuttle from the Barbara Jordan Terminal, and Community Impact (as cited by Morningline) reported a spring 2028 opening target. It is the first new building in the airport’s larger expansion program, which includes a new 26-gate Concourse B. In East Austin, Groundbreak reports that The Right Angle at 2700 E. Fifth St., registered at $106 million, received eight permits on Nov. 9 covering 476 apartments in four buildings plus amenity space, a parking garage and a retail and restaurant shell, with Andres Construction as contractor. For me, both point the same direction: airport capacity and dense housing keep pulling hospitality, flex and neighborhood retail demand toward the east side.

Office · Northwest Austin

7700 Parmer adds four new tenants totaling more than 100,000 SF as the owner plans mixed use on its parking lots

Austin Business Journal · Nov 23, 2026

The Austin Business Journal reports that the 7700 Parmer campus in Northwest Austin has signed four new tenants occupying more than 100,000 SF of office space, with Vaco by Highspring among them. ABJ says the leasing comes as the owner keeps planning a large mixed-use redevelopment of the campus’s parking lots. The full story is behind ABJ’s paywall, so I am only sharing what the public summary says. To me, the Northwest and Parmer corridor keeps proving it can lease large blocks of space, and adding housing and retail on surface parking is how older suburban campuses stay competitive.

Capital markets · Downtown multifamily · Retail

A $93 million construction loan closes for The Grant on Sabine Street downtown, and 90Ten buys a Burnet Road strip center

GlobeNewswire (Sunrise Realty Trust) · Groundbreak · Nov 2026

Sunrise Realty Trust (Nasdaq: SUNS) announced Nov. 23 that it closed a $31 million B-note within a $93 million construction loan for The Grant at 512 Sabine St. in the Sixth Street district; a third-party lender holds the $62 million A-note. The release describes 232 apartments and about 8,235 SF of ground-floor retail on a 1.75-acre block near Waterloo Greenway, sponsored by Empire Square Group, with Ryan Companies as developer and general contractor. Construction debt for a downtown project, even in a layered A/B structure, is a sign that lenders are willing to come back. On the retail side, Groundbreak reports that 90Ten acquired the 14,260 SF strip center at 5335–5341 Burnet Rd. on 1.08 acres zoned CS-MU-CO-NP. Small, well-located neighborhood retail on established corridors is still trading.

Industrial · Taylor & the US 79 corridor

Taylor adds more industrial: TaylorPort plans about 160K SF of expansion, and Park 79 Phase 2 proposes two buildings totaling 160,447 SF

Austin Business Journal · City of Taylor · P&Z Radar · Business News Austin · Oct 30–Nov 22, 2026

ABJ reports that Quannah Partners plans about 160,000 SF of new space at TaylorPort, the industrial park whose first phase broke ground in 2024 to attract Samsung suppliers; ABJ says Samsung itself ended up occupying about 40% of the park. Nearby, the City of Taylor noticed public hearings on an Employment Center Plan for Park 79 Phase 2 on about 9.63 acres east of Wing Street near US 79. P&Z Radar’s summary of the agenda packet describes two industrial buildings totaling about 160,447 SF (88,781 SF and 71,666 SF), with requested warrants on civic space and landscaping. I have not seen a decision posted. For context, Business News Austin’s Q3 comparison has CBRE at 9.77 million SF of regional industrial leasing year to date, while vacancy is still in the mid-teens to about 19% depending on the brokerage. Small-bay and mid-size product close to Samsung is the part of the market I’m watching most closely.

Data centers · Power & water

Data-center check-in: Austin Energy’s 13 inquiries total up to 664 MW, San Marcos says no to a utility district’s water request, and Georgetown votes Dec. 8

City of Austin · Community Impact · KVUE · City of Bastrop · Oct 28–Nov 20, 2026

A City of Austin staff memo puts the local demand in perspective: as of Sept. 30, Austin Energy had 13 capacity inquiries from potential data centers seeking 20 MW or more. If all were fully built out, which staff call unlikely, they would add 664 MW over 12 years, and none exceeds 75 MW. Council takes up phase one of the rules (a definition plus registration requirements) on Dec. 3, and land-use rules are expected in April or May 2027. In Hays County, Community Impact reports San Marcos unanimously denied a water contract request from County Line SUD, which has data-center plans, and postponed a water sale to Kyle until the two cities’ drought plans line up. Georgetown City Council takes up Inner Loop Exchange on Dec. 8 after P&Z recommended denial, so approval needs six of seven votes. I still haven’t found an official record of the Kyle ban-direction item from Nov. 17 or of Bastrop’s second reading, so I am leaving both as unconfirmed. Water is quickly becoming as important a constraint as power.

Manufacturing & infrastructure · Follow-ups

Still on my radar: Samsung’s proposed $1.9B Austin fab expansion, the $412.2M I-35 award, and Daimler’s San Marcos decision

Groundbreak · Texas Construction Hub · City of San Marcos · Nov 19–22, 2026

Groundbreak reports Samsung Austin Semiconductor has proposed a $1.9 billion expansion at its Northeast Austin fab, with operations targeted for 2030; the reported Starlink customer tie is unconfirmed. Texas Construction Hub confirms the $412.2 million I-35 contract from US 290 East to MLK Jr. Boulevard was awarded Nov. 20 to Austin Bridge & Road, with TDLR dates of Feb. 2027 to Feb. 2032. In San Marcos, the City says Daimler Truck’s Project Hercules is still one of several candidate sites, with Chapter 380 readings Dec. 1 and Dec. 15 and a site decision expected by year-end. It is not a done deal.
