Data centers / permitting · Hays County, Bastrop County & statewide

Hays County’s data-center incentive hearing is Tuesday — and TCEQ’s online filing portal is still dark heading into the week

The Texas Dispatch · Oct 21, 2026 · TCEQ · checked Oct 25, 2026 · Spectrum News · Oct 24, 2026 · KVUE · Oct 20, 2026

This is the item I’d put at the top of any client’s week. On Tuesday, Oct. 27 at 11 a.m., Hays County Commissioners Court holds its public hearing on the 225-page draft Development Regulations (dated Oct. 15). As The Texas Dispatch summarizes it, the draft defines a “high-intensity use” as a project averaging at least 100,000 gallons of water a day (or peaking at 250,000), drawing at least 20 megawatts at peak, or using mechanical cooling for data centers, crypto mining or high-density computing. To qualify for county incentives, those projects would need air-cooled, hybrid or closed-loop cooling (with narrow exceptions), and incentives “may be withheld or denied” unless on-site generation or storage covers at least half of peak demand or provides full backup; 50 MW-plus projects would also need dispatchable generation or a firm contract for it. These are incentive conditions, not bans — Texas counties don’t have general zoning power — and no adoption date has been set. Statewide, as of this morning TCEQ’s “STEERS is temporarily down” page still posts no return date, so stormwater and other e-filings for every applicant, not just data centers, are going through agency staff. Spectrum News (Oct. 24) reports TCEQ calls the 17 suspended items automated construction notices of intent rather than operational permits, while Public Citizen says they were still active on Oct. 19; Spectrum says TCEQ didn’t answer when exactly they were suspended. In Bastrop County, KVUE reports residents near Cedar Creek are pressing TCEQ for a public meeting on Pacifico Energy’s proposed gas plant and two data centers (Pacifico’s own map shows about 600 acres); commissioners requested one, and as of KVUE’s report the county hadn’t heard back. No ground has been broken there. My read: for land and infrastructure deals south and east of Austin, entitlement timelines and incentive math are being rewritten in real time — build slack into every schedule.

Downtown / municipal finance · Georgetown & Williamson County

Georgetown rolls out its downtown plan — a 450-space garage with ground-floor retail and a TML headquarters move — even as certified values dip 1.8%

Community Impact · Oct 25, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 23, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 22, 2026

New this morning from Community Impact: Georgetown has started work on more than half the priority projects in its 2024 Downtown Master Plan. The headline for CRE is a second downtown parking garage near East Ninth and South Main, a public-private partnership with the Texas Municipal League Intergovernmental Risk Pool, which is moving its headquarters from Austin into the former Wesleyan Building downtown. The garage is planned for 450 spaces with ground-level retail and dining; about 200 spaces would be reserved for TML staff on weekdays, with full public access evenings and weekends. It’s still in design, with construction slated for 2027 and opening in early 2029. A two-year street-patio and sidewalk-cafe pilot has its first reading Oct. 27, with final adoption targeted in November, and new wayfinding and gateway signage is going up now. The other side of the ledger: Community Impact reported Oct. 23 that Georgetown’s certified 2026 property values fell 1.8% instead of rising a projected 8%, trimming the city’s debt and spending capacity by almost $100 million over three fiscal years. Council agreed to push some parks and recreation projects back and postpone a planned May 2027 bond election by at least a year. Separately, Williamson County approved a $498,700 contract with Freese and Nichols on Oct. 20 for a countywide water infrastructure plan, expected to take about a year. For retail and office owners around the Square, more parking and outdoor seating are real tailwinds; for anyone underwriting suburban tax growth, that 1.8% decline is worth noting.

Multifamily · North Austin / Uptown ATX & Burnet Road

A clearer picture of the Solaris House sale — Brandywine’s 57% stake, closed Sept. 30 — plus The Waylon’s 300 units open on Burnet Road

Bisnow · Oct 22, 2026 · Hoodline · Oct 23, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 22, 2026 · KTGY · Oct 19, 2026 · Realty News Report · Oct 24, 2026

An update and a correction to how I framed this earlier in the week. Bisnow and Hoodline, citing Brandywine’s third-quarter results, report that what closed was Brandywine’s 57% stake in the 341-unit Solaris House joint venture, described as a $136 million sale, and that it closed Sept. 30 with the building about 90% occupied. Brandywine netted $16.3 million after repaying a $76.5 million construction loan and its preferred partner; the buyer still hasn’t been disclosed. Because of the joint-venture structure, I’m no longer quoting a per-unit price as a clean comp. Bisnow also reports CEO Jerry Sweeney said Brandywine’s Austin occupancy of 66% is about a 400-basis-point drag on the REIT overall, with IBM’s Uptown ATX lease expiring in 2027, while One Uptown is now 97% leased and he expects the CapMetro rail station there to open next year. On Burnet Road, Riverside and KTGY celebrated the grand opening of The Waylon at 7618 Burnet Road — 300 apartments in five stories on a six-acre site that used to be surface parking for an existing office building, with a new 476-space garage replacing that parking. It’s a good template for office owners sitting on excess surface lots.

Airport / infrastructure · Austin-Bergstrom

AUS sets the final bridge beam for its new midfield taxiways, days after Council approved the $224 million utility contract increase

Hoodline · Oct 24, 2026 · Austin Industries · City of Austin action notes · Oct 22, 2026

Austin-Bergstrom announced on X that the final bridge beam is installed for its New Midfield Taxiways project, Hoodline reported Oct. 24. The project adds two parallel taxiways, each 4,170 feet long, connecting the east and west sides of the airfield, and is designed to support the future midfield concourse the airport describes as a 26-gate Concourse B. Austin Bridge & Road says it set all 49 beams for the Taxiway P bridge, engineered for a Boeing 777-300ER at maximum takeoff weight. Completion timing isn’t settled in public materials — Hoodline notes RS&H has said 2026 while Community Impact has reported spring 2027. Carried over: the city’s tentative action notes show Council approved Item 27 on consent Oct. 22, adding $224 million (up to $344 million total) to the airport’s campus-wide utility infrastructure contract, plus $21.09 million in AHFC loans for five income-restricted projects. For the airport-area industrial and hospitality submarket, the expansion keeps moving from plans to steel.

Advanced manufacturing / incentives · Round Rock

PulseForge’s $600K Round Rock deal: city staff say the chip-tool maker wants to source land and build a factory

City of Round Rock · Oct 22, 2026 · Round Rock Council briefing · Oct 20, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 23, 2026

Carried over with a new detail. Round Rock approved a performance-based agreement Oct. 22 with PulseForge — which runs a 56,000-square-foot facility at 400 Parker Drive with about 50 employees — requiring at least $20 million in investment ($6 million real property, $14 million business personal property) and 60 new jobs averaging $100,000-plus, in exchange for up to $600,000 in Type B grants paid in 2030 and 2032, with clawbacks. At the Oct. 20 council packet briefing, city economic development staff said the company has identified 20,000 square feet next door but “would really like to source land here and build a factory,” and that it is pursuing federal CHIPS and Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund money. That’s a live requirement to watch in the Round Rock flex and light-industrial market. On the same agenda, KB Home asked to fold the 71.88-acre Verona Tract into Round Rock MUD No. 1 to expand Salerno (ABJ says 330 homes); I still haven’t confirmed the vote outcome from an official record.

Office · Downtown, Zilker & The Domain

Hospitality / land · Wimberley (Hays County) & Lockhart (Caldwell County)

Travel + Leisure Co. buys The Cedars Ranch in Wimberley; Lockhart’s 535-acre commercial district pitch

Travel + Leisure Co. (Business Wire) · Oct 23, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 23, 2026 · CRE Daily · Oct 23, 2026

Carried over, condensed. Travel + Leisure Co. bought The Cedars Ranch, a 20-acre Wimberley property with 21 geodomes, yurts and cabins, and will rebrand it as the first Eddie Bauer Adventure Club “Outpost,” with renovations through 2027 and stays expected in summer 2027; the price wasn’t disclosed in what I read. In Caldwell County, the proposed 535-acre Lockhart Commercial District near Highway 183 — a 500-room hotel and conference center, sports venues, an amphitheater and maker/industrial space — sits outside city limits, so annexation and entitlements come first, with a first phase targeted for 2029.
