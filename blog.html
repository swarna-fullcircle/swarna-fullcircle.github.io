Data centers / state permitting · Bastrop County & statewide

EdgeConneX’s second Bastrop County campus is among the 17 suspended stormwater approvals — and watchdogs say air-permit reviews haven’t paused

Community Impact · Updated Oct 22, 2026 · KTTZ · Oct 23, 2026 · Spectrum News · Oct 23, 2026 · Texas Public Radio · Oct 22, 2026

The TCEQ permitting story moved closer to home. Community Impact’s updated report says the 17 data-center stormwater and wastewater “notices of intent” that TCEQ suspended include the one for EdgeConneX’s second data center campus in Bastrop County (and a Google facility in Tarrant County) — a change from earlier in the week, when that Bastrop authorization was still listed as active. TCEQ spokesperson Victoria Cann confirmed the electronic filing system was shut down and that applications “must be physically provided” to the agency. KTTZ adds useful detail: FracTracker’s Sam Law estimates the 17 suspended approvals covered about 5.2 square miles where clearing and earthwork had been allowed, he has asked TCEQ for a compliance inspection because it’s unclear whether work actually stopped, and the seven air-emission certifications flagged in the report still show as “issued.” Law’s other worry is that people may assume a pause means nothing is moving and miss 30-day public comment windows. To Spectrum News, TCEQ said the documents are automated construction NOIs, “not operational permits,” and the governor’s office said TCEQ “has not taken and will not take any proactive actions” to approve data-center permits. Texas Public Radio notes ERCOT’s audit findings go to the PUC Dec. 10. My take for clients: any project touching data-center infrastructure should assume manual review and slower state timelines through at least December — and everyone else needing a stormwater authorization should still plan on paper filing until STEERS is back.

Advanced manufacturing / incentives · Round Rock

Round Rock approves incentives for PulseForge’s $20 million chip-tool expansion; KB Home eyes 72 more acres at Salerno

City of Round Rock · Oct 22, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 23, 2026 · The American Developer · Oct 14, 2026

A semiconductor-supply-chain win just up I-35. Round Rock City Council and its Transportation and Economic Development Corporation approved a performance-based agreement Oct. 22 with PulseForge, which makes semiconductor manufacturing equipment and digital thermal processing tools and currently runs a 56,000-square-foot facility at 400 Parker Drive with about 50 employees. The company must invest at least $20 million — $6 million in real property improvements and $14 million in business personal property — and add 60 jobs averaging at least $100,000 a year, in two stages by the end of 2029 and 2031. In return it can earn $600,000 from the city’s Type B sales tax fund ($250,000 in 2030 and $350,000 in 2032), with clawbacks. The city says PulseForge is weighing either expanding its current operations or building a new manufacturing facility in Round Rock, and ABJ reports the company plans to double its footprint and headcount. That’s a live requirement worth watching for anyone holding flex or light-industrial product in Round Rock. On the same Oct. 22 agenda, KB Home asked to fold the 71.88-acre Verona Tract into Round Rock MUD No. 1 to expand its Salerno community; ABJ says the plan would add 330 homes. I haven’t confirmed the vote outcome on that item.

Office / mixed-use · Downtown & The Domain

Waterline is complete at 98 Red River; Virtu’s 55,000 SF at The Republic gets fresh coverage; Axiom lists Domain Tower 2 as its HQ

Groundbreak · Oct 23, 2026 · REBusinessOnline · Oct 23, 2026 · Lincoln Property Co. · Austin Business Journal · Oct 22, 2026

Downtown’s skyline story hit a milestone. Groundbreak reports Waterline — the 74-story, 1,025-foot tower at 98 Red River, the tallest building in Texas — is complete, with 352 Bosque Residences apartments, the 252-key 1 Hotel Austin (open since September) and about 703,000 square feet of office space, roughly 2.7 million square feet in all. Lincoln Property Company and Kairoi Residential developed it, DPR Construction built it in 48 months, and a $675 million construction loan backed it. A few blocks west, REBusinessOnline reported Oct. 23 that financial firm Virtu signed about 55,000 square feet across two high-rise floors at The Republic, with CBRE and Sage Realty NYC representing the tenant. As with Jackson Walker’s 110,000 square feet there, this lease was first announced Aug. 19 by Lincoln and DivcoWest — so it’s confirmation, not a new deal. Carried over from yesterday: ABJ reports Flex spinoff Axiom Solutions is leasing at Domain Tower 2, which its SEC Form 10 lists as its principal executive offices, and that the tower is now 93% leased. The common thread is still flight to quality: new, amenitized towers keep winning the big tenants.

Hospitality / land · Wimberley (Hays County) & Lockhart (Caldwell County)

Travel + Leisure Co. buys The Cedars Ranch in Wimberley; Lockhart’s 535-acre commercial district pitch keeps drawing attention

Travel + Leisure Co. (Business Wire) · Oct 23, 2026 · Austin Business Journal · Oct 23, 2026 · CRE Daily · Oct 23, 2026

Two outer-ring hospitality plays. Travel + Leisure Co. announced Oct. 23 that it bought The Cedars Ranch, a 20-acre property in Wimberley, and will rebrand it Eddie Bauer Adventure Club – Texas Hill Country — the first “Outpost” in that vacation-club brand. It has 21 accommodations today (geodomes, yurts and cabins), with phased renovations through 2027, reservations opening in early 2027, stays expected in summer 2027, and a future expansion phase in planning. The price wasn’t disclosed in what I read. In Caldwell County, CRE Daily (citing Bisnow and ABJ) recaps the proposed Lockhart Commercial District: 535 acres near Highway 183 and Silent Valley Road with a 500-room hotel and conference center, sports venues, a nine-hole golf course, a 5,000-seat amphitheater, maker and industrial space and 15 acres of housing. The land sits outside city limits, so annexation and entitlements come first; the team is targeting a first phase in 2029. For investors, the Hill Country and US 183 south corridors keep pulling experiential and hospitality capital.

City policy / infrastructure · Austin-Bergstrom & citywide

Recap: Council approved the $224 million airport utility contract increase on consent, plus $21 million in AHFC housing loans

City of Austin action notes · Oct 22, 2026 · Community Impact · Oct 22, 2026 · KXAN · Oct 23, 2026

Carried over from yesterday because it still matters for the airport submarket. The city’s tentative action notes show Item 27 — adding $224 million to the Sundt/Archer Western contract for the airport’s campus-wide utility infrastructure, for a ceiling of up to $344 million — passed on consent. Staff backup ties the work to storm drainage, water quality and power redundancy that the Central Utility Plant, Arrivals & Departures Hall and Concourse B schedules depend on. The airport-overlay zoning case at 1019 Dalton Lane (requesting limited industrial) was also approved. As the AHFC board, Council authorized $21.09 million in loans for five income-restricted projects — Jordan II, The Maven, Rowen Vale, The Lenora and Austin Art Village — and KXAN reports city staff highlighting 54 deeply affordable units at or below 30% MFI near high-frequency transit. Action notes stay tentative until minutes are approved.

Multifamily investment sale · North Austin / Uptown ATX

Industrial / advanced manufacturing · Leander, Pflugerville, Southeast Travis County

Resurgent takes 106,000 SF in Leander, Pflugerville launches a defense-manufacturing accelerator, and Terafab sets a 2027 test-chip goal

Austin Business Journal · Oct 22, 2026 · Reuters · Oct 20, 2026

Carried over, condensed. ABJ reports stealth Austin startup Resurgent Industries, which builds automated production facilities, leased 106,000 square feet in Leander and plans to start production next year. ABJ also reports Pflugerville is partnering with Q-Branch on an accelerator for defense-manufacturing startups. And Reuters reported Musk said on an Oct. 20 call that Terafab aims for a first SRAM test chip at its Austin research facility around May or June 2027, with volume production by Q3 2028 — dates he called “very aggressive.” Add PulseForge in Round Rock and it’s a steady drumbeat of advanced-manufacturing demand along the 183A, SH 130 and I-35 corridors.
