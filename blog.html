Industrial / advanced manufacturing · Georgetown

Aalo Atomics plans a $25M advanced manufacturing build-out in Georgetown

Community Impact · Sep 17, 2026 · also Austin American-Statesman

Northern MSA industrial continues to pull advanced-manufacturing tenants into existing warehouse product. Community Impact reports Austin-based Aalo Atomics — which designs factory-built modular nuclear plants aimed at powering AI data centers — plans a $25 million renovation of a 204,350-square-foot one-story warehouse at 4813 N. I-35 in Georgetown into lab, machine-shop, and production-floor space. A Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation filing cited in coverage pegs construction from Nov. 15, 2026, through Jan. 30, 2028. The Austin American-Statesman separately framed the project as a roughly 200,000-square-foot production expansion about 40 miles north of Aalo’s East Ben White headquarters, after the company’s test reactor reached criticality in July. For me, this is the CRE read: adaptive reuse of large-bay industrial along the I-35 corridor, tying Georgetown warehouse inventory to the same power-and-AI demand story driving data-center land east of Austin.

Data centers / land · Taylor

Texas AG opens investigation into Taylor data center site tied to 1999 parkland deed

KUT · Sep 17, 2026 · also Texas Attorney General · Austin Business Journal

Entitlement risk just got louder on one of Taylor’s contested digital-infrastructure sites. KUT and a Sept. 17 Texas Attorney General press release report that Ken Paxton’s office is investigating whether a proposed Blueprint Projects AI data center can proceed on land conveyed in 1999 “to be held in trust for future use as parkland.” Coverage traces the chain: a $10 conveyance to the Texas Parks and Recreation Foundation, later transfers to the City of Taylor and the Taylor Economic Development Corporation, then a 2025 sale of the property to NCP Travis TPP Project, LLC for $10 million. Blueprint’s planned facility is described as about 135,000 square feet with an on-site electricity substation; a residents’ lawsuit was previously dismissed for lack of standing and remains on appeal. Austin Business Journal separately flagged the state investigation on Sept. 18. For me, this is the CRE overlay: title history and charitable-trust questions can freeze or reshape a data-center land play even after local economic-development conveyances.

Data centers · Bastrop County

EdgeConneX files for two more data center buildings in Bastrop County

Austin Business Journal · Sep 17, 2026

The Cedar Creek digital-infrastructure map just got more concrete. Austin Business Journal reports EdgeConneX has filed for two additional data-center buildings in Bastrop County and indicated a November groundbreaking on that pair — a roughly $1.2 billion investment that continues the company’s Cedar Creek buildout east of Austin-Bergstrom. Coverage places the company’s longer-range plan at about a dozen buildings in the area and an investment total well into the billions; EdgeConneX has also said it intends to stay in the community for the long term and has committed to Gov. Greg Abbott’s proposed standards for data-center developers. For me, this is the next chapter after the broader “data centers swarm Cedar Creek” story: specific filings, capital, and a construction calendar in the eastern MSA even as Austin debates large-load rules inside city limits.

Industrial / cleanroom · North Austin

Prudential plots a 100,000 SF North Austin cleanroom facility and 150 jobs

Austin Business Journal · Sep 17, 2026 · also Prudential Overall Supply

Cleanroom demand is translating into bricks-and-mortar expansion inside Austin proper. Austin Business Journal reports Prudential Overall Supply is preparing a roughly 100,000-square-foot North Austin facility — a $40 million to $45 million investment expected to create about 150 jobs — as lab and cleanroom space becomes more common across the market. The company’s own groundbreaking notice points to 1001 West Howard Lane, with a public ribbon-cutting set for Oct. 1, 2026, and a Q1 2028 completion target for a combined cleanroom and industrial laundry plant (its 38th U.S. location and ninth ISO Class 3 cleanroom laundry facility). I’m reading this as the support-ecosystem story behind semiconductor, life-sciences, and advanced manufacturing tenants: when cleanroom inventory grows, the service footprint follows.

Retail · Tech Ridge

Cava, Tacodeli, and JuiceLand head to Tech Ridge’s new dining pad

Austin Business Journal · Sep 17, 2026 · also Tacodeli / Community Impact

Northeast Austin retail leasing is filling out around one of the market’s busiest retail nodes. Austin Business Journal reports JuiceLand, Cava, and Tacodeli are joining Einstein Bros. Bagels at Tech Ridge’s new dining destination near H-E-B and the AMC — a corridor already packed with national retail. Tacodeli’s own announcement places the restaurant at 12705 Center Lake Drive as its eleventh Austin-area location, with JuiceLand and Einstein as immediate neighbors and Cava anchoring the opposite end; Community Impact earlier flagged Cava’s late-2026 opening target at Suite 110. For me, this is the CRE takeaway: amenity leasing catching up to workforce density along Parmer and I-35, not just another restaurant headline.

Hospitality · Downtown Austin

Crews break ground on dual-branded Embassy Suites and Tempo hotel downtown

Austin Business Journal · Sep 15, 2026 · also DLR Group

I’m still watching this as the clearest hospitality signal in the core. Austin Business Journal reports a dual-branded Hilton project at Fifth and Lavaca — part Embassy Suites, part Tempo — as one of the first new construction starts downtown in some time, with about 480 rooms expected online in the coming years. Coverage around the Sept. 14 groundbreaking describes a 30-story tower co-developed by Peachtree Group and Merritt Development Group, with architecture and interiors by DLR Group, plus a rooftop bar and lounge, podium restaurant, ballroom/conference space, and an early-2029 opening target. For me, this is capital committing vertical inventory in the CBD even while office absorption stays selective.

Office · Near downtown

Endeavor breaks ground on 198,000 SF 5th & Walsh office-retail project

REBusinessOnline · Sep 14, 2026

Selective office delivery is still happening — when the lease story is already half written. REBusinessOnline reports Endeavor Real Estate Group has broken ground on 5th & Walsh, a 198,000-square-foot office and retail project near downtown Austin designed by Studio8 Architects and Lake Flato Architects. The building will include four floors of office (Endeavor plans to take 25,000 square feet for its new headquarters) plus 21,000 square feet of ground-floor retail and restaurant space, with shell construction targeted for late 2028. Other undisclosed office tenants have already leased another 85,000 square feet, bringing the office component to about 55% preleased. I’m reading this as the walkable, amenitized product thesis in action: new office still pencils when preleasing and owner-user commitment de-risk the start.

Land · Georgetown

Georgetown’s Yearwood MUD heads toward a Sept. 22 final vote — with a commercial corner on Reagan

Community Impact · Sep 14, 2026

The northern MSA land pipeline stays on my calendar — final vote is just days away. Community Impact reports Georgetown City Council gave initial approval Sept. 8 to establish an in-city municipal utility district for the Yearwood property — roughly 728 acres north of Sun City, west of Ronald Reagan Boulevard and Rattlesnake Road, long operated as a stone quarry. The Yearwood family, Freehold Capital Management, and the city have been negotiating since February. The plan is multiuse: majority residential (duplexes, townhomes, and 45-to-60-foot single-family lots, house count reduced to 1,728) with commercial land planned at the Reagan–Rattlesnake corner. In exchange for MUD consent, dedications include a 140-acre Berry Creek Park, trails, amenity centers, a 7-acre public-safety site, and a 110-foot-wide minor arterial linking Reagan northward. Final approval is slated for the Sept. 22 meeting — so I’m watching that commercial pad as the CRE piece of a much larger Georgetown entitlement story.
